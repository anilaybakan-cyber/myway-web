Matera

A nine-thousand-year cave city: a story of stone, bread and rebirth

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Matera

Arriving in Matera confuses time itself: the cave houses (sassi) carved into the ravine tell of a city inhabited without pause for nine thousand years. Emptied in the 1950s as 'the shame of Italy', the caves are today UNESCO-listed and reborn with cave hotels, stone lanes and terraces over the canyon. Mel Gibson shot 'The Passion' here; you'll understand at first glance — these stones are old enough to play Jerusalem.

🗣️ Local Concept: Vicinato (vee-chee-NAH-to)

The neighbourhood order built around a shared courtyard in the Sassi: shared well, shared oven, shared life. That solidarity was Matera's secret of survival; it lives on in the corner conversations today.

3 Unwritten Rules for Matera

1

Stay a night — Matera begins at dusk

When the day-trippers leave, the stones of the Sassi fill with light and the city becomes another place. One night in a cave hotel is the difference between visiting Matera and living it.

2

Don't drive into the Sassi

The historic core is a ZTL (restricted traffic zone) and most lanes are stairs anyway. Leave the car in the upper-town car parks; use your hotel's luggage service, or come with a light bag.

3

Solid shoes, a full water bottle

Matera is an up-and-down city: cobbles, steps and summer sun. Heels and flip-flops don't walk here; double the water if you're descending into the canyon.

Getting Around

Matera Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Reaching Matera is easy and vehicles are useless within it: this city is toured by shoe.

🚆

The Appulo Lucane train from Bari

The little train from the annex of Bari Centrale reaches Matera Centrale in about 1.5 hours; on Sundays a bus replaces the thinner service. Tickets are cheap, the view is Puglia's plains.

🚌

Intercity buses

Direct buses run from Rome, Naples and the southern cities; Matera's main stop is a walk from the centre. The night buses are budget-friendly.

🥾

Walking the Sassi

Stone lanes and stairways from Barisano to Caveoso: the only real transport. Constant climbs between the upper town (Piano) and the Sassi — trust your knees, rest often.

🚗

If you come by car

Car parks sit in the upper town (Via Saragat and others); the ZTL is camera-enforced and the fine finds your letterbox. If your hotel is in the Sassi, ask about luggage and access beforehand.

🚕

Taxi or car for the belvedere

The Murgia Timone viewpoint across the ravine is 15 minutes by car or 45 on foot over the suspension bridge; bring a torch for the sunset return.

💡 Luggage tip

A wheeled suitcase drums on the cobbles and turns to torture on the stairs; the backpack is king, and the hotel's pickup service is its vizier.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Matera: Best Areas

The Matera question: do you want to sleep in a cave, or looking at one?

Sasso Barisano

The comfortable sassi

The more restored and hotel-rich of the two: boutique cave hotels, restaurants and bell-tower views. The best-balanced address for a first Matera night.

📍 Cave hotels • Restaurant density • Restored fabric

Sasso Caveoso

The raw one

The less-touched, barer sassi: rock churches, empty caves and canyon-edge lanes. Atmosphere thick, comfort variable — the sleep closest to Matera's memory.

📍 Rock churches • Canyon edge • Raw atmosphere

Civita — the rock's crown

Most central

The rocky spur between the two sassi, the cathedral's quarter: minutes from everything, views to both sides. Small and in demand — book early.

📍 Beside the cathedral • Between the sassi • Views both ways

Piano — the upper town

The practical choice

Baroque churches and flat streets — the 'normal' Matera: no luggage drama, fair prices, five minutes down to the Sassi. Friend of the stair-averse and the car-borne.

📍 Flat streets • Easy access • Good value

Across the canyon — agriturismo

Most panoramic

Farmhouses on the Murgia plateau facing the Sassi head-on: at sunset the city sits in your palm. A car is essential; the reward is silence and that frame.

📍 The frontal view • Country silence • Car essential
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Matera

The stone city lives its seasons hard: an oven in summer, silent in winter, golden in spring and autumn.

Spring

Apr – Jun
Stone in Bloom

The Gravina ravine greens, the walking trails open, the lanes stay friendly even at noon. Crowds rise at weekends; midweek Matera is nearly yours alone.

Summer

Jul – Aug
The Oven Months

The stone heats all day and the lanes empty at noon; life moves to morning, evening and the cool of the caves. The Festa della Bruna on July 2 is the city's wildest day.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Golden Light

September and October are Matera at its most photogenic: the stone turns honey, the evenings cool, the crowds recede. November builds its own mood of misty mornings.

Winter

Dec – Mar
Silent Stone

Visitors thin out, the cave hotels light fires, and the presepe tradition turns the Sassi into a stage. If snow falls — rare, but it happens — Matera becomes a fairytale photograph.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Matera

Lucanian cooking is poverty's inventive genius: the bread sacred, the pepper crisp, the pasta handmade.

🍞

Pane di Matera

The legendary horn-shaped loaf with thick crust and yellow crumb (IGP protected): baked in wood ovens, fresh for a week. The city's scent drifts from these bakeries.

⭐ IGP product · from the wood oven
🌶️

Peperoni cruschi

Crisp-fried dried peppers: a meze on their own, a crown on eggs and pasta. Buying a packet for the suitcase is entirely legitimate.

⭐ Signature taste · buy a packet too
🍝

Cavatelli and orecchiette — with mollica

Handmade pasta with garlicky fried breadcrumbs (mollica) and cruschi: Matera's 'poor but royal' plate.

⭐ Trattoria classic · ask for cruschi
🥣

Crapiata

The vicinato soup of wheat, legumes and potato simmered low and slow: the neighbours' harvest celebration dish. It even has its own festival in early August.

⭐ Heritage dish · good in both seasons
🥗

Cialledda

Stale Matera bread resurrected with tomato, cucumber and olive oil: the cold 'poor salad' of summer, a warm version in winter. Respect for bread, turned into a plate.

⭐ Summer classic · with pane di Matera
🍷

Aglianico del Vulture

The powerful red of the volcanic Vulture slopes: 'the Barolo of the south'. Found by the glass in cave enotecas; its pairing with cruschi and cheese is a masterclass.

⭐ Cave enoteca · by the glass
🍷 The bakery rule

Start the day at a bakery: warm focaccia, a handful of taralli, and ask when the bread comes out. 'Is the pane ready?' is a conversation-starter in this city.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Matera

Even Matera's surface counts as hidden; these six descend one layer further into the stone.

✨ A cathedral beneath the city

Palombaro Lungo

A giant cistern of five thousand cubic metres under the main square: 'the cathedral of water'. In these underground vaults you grasp that the Sassi could never have lived without it; visited on a short tour.

✨ The cave of frescoes

Cripta del Peccato Originale

Eighth-century frescoes in a rock church outside town: 'the Sistine Chapel of the rupestrian churches'. Visits by reservation only — worth every effort.

✨ A church on the canyon's lip

Madonna de Idris

The church fused to the rock above Caveoso: a frescoed rock chapel within, one of the canyon's best views without. Climb near sunset.

✨ The far bank's viewpoint

Murgia Timone Belvedere

The viewpoint on the far slope, reached over a suspension bridge: the Sassi head-on, like a film still. 'The Passion' was shot here; the sunset is watched here.

✨ Sculpture in cave form

MUSMA

A contemporary sculpture museum installed in a palazzo's cave floors: modern forms in stone hollows. The most elegant version of Matera's 'cave + art' equation.

✨ The door to the story

Casa Noha

FAI's short-film museum: Matera's journey from shame to pride in 25 minutes. See it BEFORE touring the Sassi — you'll look at the stones with new eyes.

✨ Sequence tip

The ideal order: Casa Noha first (the story), then the Sassi (living it), Murgia Timone last (seeing the whole). Stone viewed with the story known speaks differently.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Matera has few traps — but day-trip haste is itself one. Know these four.

🚗 TRAP

Entering the ZTL 'just for a minute'

The satnav's offer to 'take you to the hotel door' ends in a camera-issued ZTL fine; reversing out of the narrow lanes is its own drama.

💡 What to Do Instead: Leave the car in an upper-town car park and walk, or request the hotel pickup; confirm the ZTL hours with your hotel.
📸 TRAP

The two-hour 'seen it' tour

Stepping off a coach, shooting the belvedere and leaving is not seeing Matera; the city's soul lives in evening light and inside the caves.

💡 What to Do Instead: Stay a night: sunset, a night walk in the stone lanes and the morning bakery smell — that is Matera's real ticket.
🎫 TRAP

'Official guide' bargaining on the square

Some 'guides' who approach at the gates are unlicensed; you pay well for a memorised loop.

💡 What to Do Instead: For a guided visit, book a licensed guide or the Casa Noha + rock churches combination in advance.
🍽️ TRAP

The frozen 'typical menu' on a view terrace

Some panoramic terraces sell ready-made plates as a 'menu tipico'; the view is lovely, the cavatelli tired.

💡 What to Do Instead: Choose the trattoria that talks about handmade pasta and the day's dish; among cave restaurants, the small one is usually the good one.
🛡️ The golden rule

Crowds in Matera dislike stairs: descend two stairways off the main via and the tourist density drops ninety percent, leaving the stones to you.
