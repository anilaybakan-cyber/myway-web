Sardinia

Caribbean-blue sea, granite mountains, and an island that speaks its own language

Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Sardinia

Sardinia is not an island but a small continent: Caribbean-blue coves on the coast, granite mountains inland, three-thousand-year-old stone towers called nuraghi, and a language apart from Italian. The yachts of the Costa Smeralda are the shop window; the real Sardinia lives in shepherd villages, in the crackle of bread, and in the ease of people who say 'ajò' and hit the road. Don't underestimate the distances, don't hurry the sea — this island belongs to slow travellers.

🗣️ Local Concept: Ajò (ah-YO)

Sardinian for 'come on, let's go': the island's mood in one word. To the sea, to the mountains, to the table — no matter; say ajò and a Sardinian counts you as one of their own.

3 Unwritten Rules for Sardinia

1

Rent a car — no negotiation

Public transport is weak between the coastal towns; the coves, nuraghi and mountain villages only happen by car. In summer book months ahead; automatics sell out early.

2

Don't tour the island from one base

Cagliari to Alghero is four hours. Set separate bases for north, east and south, or devote the trip to one region; the ambition to 'see it all' turns a holiday into motorway.

3

Leave the sand and shells where they are

Taking sand, pebbles or shells from the beaches is illegal and seriously fined; suitcases get scanned at the airports. For a souvenir, a photo and a bottle of mirto suffice.

Getting Around

Sardinia Public Transit & Airport Transfers

In Sardinia the plan starts with a car; the rest is choosing coves.

Cagliari is the gate to the south, Olbia to the northeast (Costa Smeralda), Alghero to the northwest. Fly to the region your route is built on; transfers across the island take hours.

Night ferries from Civitavecchia, Livorno and Genoa land at Olbia and Cagliari; bringing your own car can beat summer rental prices. Book the cabin early.

The coast roads are scenic but winding, the inland roads narrow. Fill up between villages, keep coins for beach car parks. A small car wins on the narrow cove lanes.

The main railway runs the Cagliari–Sassari axis, slow but scenic; the historic narrow-gauge 'Trenino Verde' is not transport but an excursion in itself.

Cala Goloritzé, the La Maddalena archipelago and most hidden coves are best reached from the water: daily boat tours and gommone (dinghy) hire are everywhere. Ask the engine limit for licence-free boats.

⚡ Pro Tip

Some famous beaches (La Pelosa, Tuerredda, the Goloritzé path) run summer visitor quotas with online booking; check the day before and go early in the morning.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Sardinia

Behind the postcard coves hides a three-thousand-year-old island; these six fit sea and history into the same day.

Su Nuraxi — Barumini

The largest nuraghe complex, UNESCO-listed: a stone tower and village fabric from 1500 BC. Guided tours go inside the tower — the entrance lesson to the island's mysterious civilisation.

Cala Goloritzé

Ogliastra's jewel with its granite arch and needle spire: reached only by footpath (quota-controlled) or by swimming in from a boat. The reward is the Mediterranean's purest cove.

Bosa

Coloured houses stacked along the Temo river below the Malaspina castle: the island's most photogenic town, left in Alghero's shadow. Also the home of Malvasia wine.

Orgosolo

Barbagia's rebel village, an open-air gallery of over 150 political-poetic murals: shepherd Sardinia's voice on the walls. A shepherd's lunch nearby completes the classic.

Neptune's Grotto

The great stalactite cave in Alghero's Capo Caccia cliffs: by the 654-step cliff stairway (Escala del Cabirol) or by boat. Both are part of the show.

Tharros

The Phoenician-Roman city set on the sea at the Sinis peninsula: blue behind every column. With the San Giovanni di Sinis church and the mullet shacks nearby, a perfect half day.

(bwon-JOR-noh)

(GRAH-tsyeh)

(pehr fah-VOH-reh)

(sah-LOO-teh)
