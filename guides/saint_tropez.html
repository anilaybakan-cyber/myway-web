Saint-Tropez

Behind the yachts and beach clubs, a fishing village is still hiding — finding it takes a little time

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Saint-Tropez

When you hear Saint-Tropez, you think yachts, beach clubs and Brigitte Bardot. All true — but in summer this face of the city is so dominant that the real Saint-Tropez becomes hard to see. Until the 1950s this was a small fishing village nobody knew about. The traces of that village are still here today: colourfully painted fishing boats in the old harbour, elderly men playing pétanque under the plane trees of Place des Lices, the stone streets of La Ponche neighbourhood. To reach them, skip the peak season or set out very early in the morning. Saint-Tropez is small — everything is within walking distance. But getting here is another story: no train station, and traffic seizes up completely in summer.

🗣️ Local Concept: Pétanque (peh-TANK)

The French boules game — metal balls thrown to land closest to the target ball, a game that can be played for hours. Under the plane trees of Place des Lices, elderly Tropéziens play in the mornings and late afternoons. Watching is free; to join you need to wait for an invitation. One of Saint-Tropez's most authentic and accessible local experiences — just a few steps from the yacht harbour.

3 Unwritten Rules for Saint-Tropez

1

If you must come in July-August, book well in advance

In midsummer the roads around Saint-Tropez are gridlocked for hours, beaches overflow and prices peak. If you have to come during this period, book at least three months ahead. If coming by car, leave it in the out-of-town car parks and take the shuttle bus into the centre. Very early morning and after 7pm the town gets a little breathing room.

2

Plan your transport — there's no train station

No train goes to Saint-Tropez. The nearest train station is Saint-Raphaël, then a bus ride of 1-1.5 hours. Ferry from Sainte-Maxime is 25 minutes — much more enjoyable. Summer season ferry from Nice is about 2.5 hours. If you hire a car, traffic in midsummer is very bad; people who don't plan their transport in advance lose a lot of time.

3

Not the harbour front — go to La Ponche and the Citadelle side

The cafés and restaurants on the harbour front are Saint-Tropez's most expensive and most touristy spots. La Ponche neighbourhood, the Citadelle slope and the area around Place aux Herbes are both cheaper and far more local. Go to Place des Lices for Tarte Tropézienne; for food, explore the streets behind the harbour.

Getting Around

Saint-Tropez Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Getting to Saint-Tropez isn't easy — no trains, roads are narrow and jammed in summer. The ferry is the most enjoyable option.

⛴️

Ferry — the best option

Les Bateaux Verts ferry from Sainte-Maxime reaches Saint-Tropez in 25 minutes. Daily sailings April-October. Return approximately €16-18. Trans Côte d'Azur ferry from Nice runs direct during summer season (June-September) — about 2.5 hours, enjoyable but long. Ferry connections also available from Saint-Raphaël. Book tickets in advance in summer.

🚌

Bus — cheap but slow

Varlib/ZOU! route 7601 from Saint-Raphaël train station goes to Saint-Tropez. About 1-1.5 hours, €2-3. Can stretch to 2-3 hours in summer traffic. Bus connections also available from Les Arcs-Draguignan train station. Budget-friendly but requires patience.

🚗

Car — freedom but traffic

Your own car or a hire car is the most flexible option — essential for seeing the surrounding villages. But the N98 road is gridlocked for hours in midsummer. Solution: leave the car at Les Marines de Cogolin or Port Grimaud car parks 3-4km from town and get to the centre by shuttle bus or boat. Parking in town is both expensive and nearly impossible.

🚲

Bicycle — ideal for Pampelonne

Saint-Tropez centre is small and walkable but Pampelonne beach is 5 kilometres away — a bicycle is the smartest choice. Many hire points in and around town. The cycling path to Pampelonne is partly shaded. Electric bikes are also available — more comfortable for the hilly sections.

🚶

Walking — sufficient within town

Saint-Tropez centre is genuinely small: from the harbour square to Place des Lices is 5 minutes, La Ponche 7 minutes, the Citadelle 15 minutes. The entire historic centre is walkable. The Sentier du Littoral coastal walking trail extends all the way to Pampelonne — about 35km, requires a full day.

💡 Port Grimaud — the French version of Venice with gondolas

8 kilometres north-west of Saint-Tropez, a canal city built in the 1960s. Houses open directly onto the water; small boat tours are available. Ideal for stopping before or after the Sainte-Maxime ferry. There's a free outer area to walk; boat tours of the inner canals are ticketed. A calmer alternative away from Saint-Tropez's crowds.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Saint-Tropez: Best Areas

Saint-Tropez and surroundings — the town itself is expensive; surrounding villages are both more affordable and more authentic.

Saint-Tropez centre

Most central

The harbour square, Place des Lices and La Ponche all within walking distance. Prices very high in summer — finding a room under €200 is difficult. The right place if you want to be close to the yacht harbour. Early booking essential.

📍 Close to the harbour square • Place des Lices • Everything walkable

Ramatuelle — hilltop village

Most authentic

10km south of Saint-Tropez, a medieval village among vineyards and olive groves. 10 minutes to Pampelonne beach by bike or car. Half the price of Saint-Tropez, much quieter. Small restaurants, local market and a Provençal atmosphere in the village itself. Car essential.

📍 Close to Pampelonne • Half the price • Provençal village life

Gassin — panoramic hilltop village

Most scenic

A medieval village looking down over the bay. On France's 'most beautiful villages' list. 7km from Saint-Tropez, reached by car. Small but impressive — narrow streets, bay views, a few restaurants.

📍 Bay panorama • Most beautiful villages list • Quiet and authentic

Sainte-Maxime

Most practical

On the opposite shore of the bay, 25 minutes from Saint-Tropez by ferry. Prices noticeably lower. Has its own beaches, restaurants and shopping street. Take the morning ferry to Saint-Tropez and return in the evening. Ideal base if you're making many day trips.

📍 25 minutes by ferry • More affordable • Own beaches and restaurants

Port Grimaud

Most unusual

A canal city built in the 1960s — every house has a boat mooring out front. 10 minutes from Saint-Tropez by car or boat connection. Walkable within itself, restaurants along the canals. A good option for escaping the crowds.

📍 Canal city architecture • Close to Saint-Tropez • Unusual experience
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Saint-Tropez

Saint-Tropez is a completely different place depending on the season — crowded and expensive in midsummer, beautifully different in spring and autumn.

Spring

Apr – Jun
The best time

April and early May are Saint-Tropez's golden period. Temperatures 18-22°C, no crowds, prices half those of midsummer. Vineyards lush, the bay clear. It starts filling up from early June but still far from the July-August crowds. No restaurant reservations needed, space on the beaches.

Summer

Jun – Aug
Crowded and expensive

July and August are Saint-Tropez's most intense period. Pampelonne beach is packed, roads gridlocked, hotel prices are staggering. That said, the beach clubs are at their liveliest and evenings are long and glamorous. Very early morning and after 7pm the town gets a little breathing room. June is more manageable; July-August is genuinely very crowded.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Second best time

September and October see the crowds go, the weather stays warm and the sea remains swimmable. Grape harvest around Ramatuelle and Gassin — vineyards turn golden. No restaurant reservations needed, prices have dropped. From November some venues close but the town itself keeps going.

Winter

Dec – Feb
Quiet and local

From December to February Saint-Tropez belongs almost entirely to its residents. Many restaurants and boutiques are closed but what remains is genuinely local. Pétanque is played at Place des Lices, morning coffee at Café Sénéquier. Temperatures 10-15°C, some rainy days but many sunny moments too. Prices are a fraction of midsummer.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Saint-Tropez

Eating in Saint-Tropez is expensive — but find the right place and it's both delicious and reasonable. Avoid the harbour front, step into the side streets.

🎂

Tarte Tropézienne

Buy it from the original La Tarte Tropézienne shop at Place des Lices. Whole tart €25-35, a slice €5-8. Fresh in the morning. The orange blossom aroma is subtle, the filling creamy. Eating this pastry here is one of Saint-Tropez's most local and most accessible rituals.

⭐ Place des Lices, La Tarte Tropézienne · slice €5–8
🐟

Fresh fish — at Place aux Herbes

The small fish market at Place aux Herbes runs in the mornings — the Tropézien fishermen's daily catch is sold here. If you want fresher and cheaper fish than the tourist restaurants on the harbour front, go to the small bistros around Place aux Herbes. Get there early before it sells out.

⭐ Around Place aux Herbes · early morning · fresh daily catch
🥘

Ratatouille

Courgette, aubergine, tomatoes, peppers, onions and garlic — Provence's most pure vegetable dish. Every local restaurant in Saint-Tropez makes it; both as a main dish and alongside meat and fish. In summer, the version made with vegetables from the local market is the finest. Can also be eaten cold in the summer heat.

⭐ Local bistros and restaurants · €12–18
🥩

Daube Provençale

Provence's beef stew slow-cooked in red wine. Prepared with olive oil, onions, garlic, thyme and bay leaf, simmered slowly for hours. A winter and spring dish — rarely seen on summer menus. Between September and May, findable at small restaurants around La Ponche.

⭐ Around La Ponche · winter–spring · €18-28
🍷

Côtes de Provence rosé

From the vineyards surrounding Saint-Tropez, France's most famous rosé. Pale in colour, light in aroma, dry in character — Provence rosé is exported around the world. But the best is drunk here. A glass at cafés on Place des Lices or the bars near the harbour: €6-10.

⭐ Every café and bistro · glass €6–10 · away from the harbour
🧅

Socca and pissaladière — at Place des Lices market

At the Tuesday and Thursday morning market, local stalls sell chickpea flour pancake (socca) and onion tart slices (pissaladière). €2-4, the best morning snack. Try both as you browse the market stalls — the cheapest and most delicious way to have a Provençal breakfast.

⭐ Place des Lices market · Tues and Thurs · €2–4
🍷 Café Sénéquier — harbour icon

Historic café with a red awning on the harbour square — in the same spot since 1887. Expensive (espresso €5-7) but worth sitting at least once. The best spot for watching the mega yachts and harbour life. If you go for breakfast between 8am and 10am, it's much less crowded than at midday or in the evening.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Saint-Tropez

Saint-Tropez's finest corners are usually a few minutes from the harbour square or in the surrounding villages.

✨ Brigitte Bardot's Saint-Tropez — La Ponche neighbourhood

La Ponche — the old fishing quarter

Just behind the harbour square, a few steps in. Narrow stone streets, colourfully painted windows, old fishermen's houses. Bardot filmed And God Created Woman here in 1956 and introduced Saint-Tropez to the world. It still carries the spirit of that film. In the early morning hours there's almost no one here. Small restaurants and cafés exist but most open in the afternoon.

✨ From France's most beautiful villages list — Gassin

Gassin — medieval village overlooking the bay

7km from Saint-Tropez, 15 minutes by car. On France's 'Les Plus Beaux Villages' list. The village with the best panoramic view of the bay — nothing obstructing it, just sea. Small church, narrow streets, a few restaurants. Coming here for dinner means escaping the crowds below and gaining the view.

✨ The Citadelle and the town's finest view — affordable

Citadelle de Saint-Tropez

A 17th-century castle on the hill east of town. Contains a maritime history museum inside. But the real reason: the panoramic view of Saint-Tropez bay from the moat and ramparts around the castle. Ticketed but reasonably priced. Quietest in the early morning hours. Standing here looking out at the harbour, Pampelonne and the distant Estérel mountains, you understand why the town became so beloved.

✨ Sentier du Littoral — coastal walking trail

Sentier du Littoral — coastal path

A coastal walking trail from Saint-Tropez extending to Pampelonne and Ramatuelle. 35km in total but can be done in sections. Rocky shores, small coves, pine forests and Mediterranean views. Hot in July-August; spring and autumn are ideal. Walking it, you find your own secret cove — an experience far beyond Saint-Tropez's beach club facade.

✨ Not a museum — a real Provençal experience

Musée de l'Annonciade

Five minutes from the harbour square, in a former chapel. Signac, Matisse, Derain, Bonnard — Post-Impressionist and Fauvist painters lived and worked in Saint-Tropez at the start of the 20th century. This museum holds their works. If it's very hot outside or the harbour is too crowded, stepping into this museum means both cooling down and seeing genuinely fine art. Little-known but of real quality.

✨ Port Grimaud — Provençal village on canals

Port Grimaud

8km from Saint-Tropez, a canal city built from scratch in the 1960s by architect François Spoerry. Venice-like but Provençal — every house has a boat mooring out front, colourfully painted facades. Boat tours are ticketed; the outer area is free to explore. Perfect for stopping before or after the Sainte-Maxime ferry.

✨ Place des Lices — pétanque and shade

The town's central square but not touristy — mornings and late afternoons, elderly Tropéziens play pétanque here. Plane tree shade, dusty ground, the sound of metal balls. Watching is free and never gets old. Tuesday and Thursday market is set up on this square. The original Tarte Tropézienne shop is here too. Saint-Tropez's least touristy and most local corner.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Saint-Tropez's fame shows up in its prices, but the village itself is still a small fishing town. Know these four and live it without the jet-set bill.

🛋️ TRAP

A club sunbed at Pampelonne

At the famous beach clubs, a sunbed plus lunch easily reaches hundreds of euros; the sea is the same sea for everyone.

💡 What to Do Instead: Lay your towel on Pampelonne's public stretches or pick the path-access coves; if the clubs intrigue you, go for just a drink.
⛵ TRAP

Dining at yacht-view prices on the port front

The quay cafés add the superyacht view to the menu: an ordinary salad, an extraordinary bill.

💡 What to Do Instead: Watch the yachts for free walking the quay; eat two streets in, on the La Ponche side.
🚗 TRAP

Driving in through summer traffic

In July and August the single access road is an hours-long queue; parking is a separate war.

💡 What to Do Instead: Park in Sainte-Maxime and cross the gulf in 15 minutes by sea shuttle (bateau vert): the view is a bonus, the calm is free.
📸 TRAP

Buying a 'celebrity hunt' tour

The boat tours pointing at star villas from afar are an expensive disappointment; celebrities are on the port anyway when they want to be seen.

💡 What to Do Instead: Spend the same money on the coastal path (Sentier du Littoral): coves, headlands and the real Saint-Tropez view.
🛡️ The golden rule

In Saint-Tropez the clock is the rule: the village belongs to locals before 9 am and after 7 pm. Give the hours between to the beach, the hill villages and the path.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Saint-Tropez

Provençal French is slow and sunlit; these five are enough in the village.

🗣️ Bonjour
Hello — the mandatory first word of every exchange
🗣️ Merci
Thank you — stronger with beaucoup
🗣️ S'il vous plaît
Please — add it to the end of your order
🗣️ Santé!
Cheers! — rosé glasses rise to this
🗣️ Peuchère!
The Provençal exclamation: 'oh my / poor thing' — hear it and you've arrived in the south
