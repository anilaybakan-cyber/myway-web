Saint-Tropez

Behind the yachts and beach clubs, a fishing village is still hiding — finding it takes a little time

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Saint-Tropez

When you hear Saint-Tropez, you think yachts, beach clubs and Brigitte Bardot. All true — but in summer this face of the city is so dominant that the real Saint-Tropez becomes hard to see. Until the 1950s this was a small fishing village nobody knew about. The traces of that village are still here today: colourfully painted fishing boats in the old harbour, elderly men playing pétanque under the plane trees of Place des Lices, the stone streets of La Ponche neighbourhood. To reach them, skip the peak season or set out very early in the morning. Saint-Tropez is small — everything is within walking distance. But getting here is another story: no train station, and traffic seizes up completely in summer.

🗣️ Local Concept: Pétanque (peh-TANK)

The French boules game — metal balls thrown to land closest to the target ball, a game that can be played for hours. Under the plane trees of Place des Lices, elderly Tropéziens play in the mornings and late afternoons. Watching is free; to join you need to wait for an invitation. One of Saint-Tropez's most authentic and accessible local experiences — just a few steps from the yacht harbour.

3 Unwritten Rules for Saint-Tropez

1

If you must come in July-August, book well in advance

In midsummer the roads around Saint-Tropez are gridlocked for hours, beaches overflow and prices peak. If you have to come during this period, book at least three months ahead. If coming by car, leave it in the out-of-town car parks and take the shuttle bus into the centre. Very early morning and after 7pm the town gets a little breathing room.

2

Plan your transport — there's no train station

No train goes to Saint-Tropez. The nearest train station is Saint-Raphaël, then a bus ride of 1-1.5 hours. Ferry from Sainte-Maxime is 25 minutes — much more enjoyable. Summer season ferry from Nice is about 2.5 hours. If you hire a car, traffic in midsummer is very bad; people who don't plan their transport in advance lose a lot of time.

3

Not the harbour front — go to La Ponche and the Citadelle side

The cafés and restaurants on the harbour front are Saint-Tropez's most expensive and most touristy spots. La Ponche neighbourhood, the Citadelle slope and the area around Place aux Herbes are both cheaper and far more local. Go to Place des Lices for Tarte Tropézienne; for food, explore the streets behind the harbour.

Getting Around

Saint-Tropez Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Getting to Saint-Tropez isn't easy — no trains, roads are narrow and jammed in summer. The ferry is the most enjoyable option.

Les Bateaux Verts ferry from Sainte-Maxime reaches Saint-Tropez in 25 minutes. Daily sailings April-October. Return approximately €16-18. Trans Côte d'Azur ferry from Nice runs direct during summer season (June-September) — about 2.5 hours, enjoyable but long. Ferry connections also available from Saint-Raphaël. Book tickets in advance in summer.

Varlib/ZOU! route 7601 from Saint-Raphaël train station goes to Saint-Tropez. About 1-1.5 hours, €2-3. Can stretch to 2-3 hours in summer traffic. Bus connections also available from Les Arcs-Draguignan train station. Budget-friendly but requires patience.

Your own car or a hire car is the most flexible option — essential for seeing the surrounding villages. But the N98 road is gridlocked for hours in midsummer. Solution: leave the car at Les Marines de Cogolin or Port Grimaud car parks 3-4km from town and get to the centre by shuttle bus or boat. Parking in town is both expensive and nearly impossible.

Saint-Tropez centre is small and walkable but Pampelonne beach is 5 kilometres away — a bicycle is the smartest choice. Many hire points in and around town. The cycling path to Pampelonne is partly shaded. Electric bikes are also available — more comfortable for the hilly sections.

Saint-Tropez centre is genuinely small: from the harbour square to Place des Lices is 5 minutes, La Ponche 7 minutes, the Citadelle 15 minutes. The entire historic centre is walkable. The Sentier du Littoral coastal walking trail extends all the way to Pampelonne — about 35km, requires a full day.

⚡ Pro Tip

8 kilometres north-west of Saint-Tropez, a canal city built in the 1960s. Houses open directly onto the water; small boat tours are available. Ideal for stopping before or after the Sainte-Maxime ferry. There's a free outer area to walk; boat tours of the inner canals are ticketed. A calmer alternative away from Saint-Tropez's crowds.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Saint-Tropez

Saint-Tropez's finest corners are usually a few minutes from the harbour square or in the surrounding villages.

La Ponche — the old fishing quarter

Just behind the harbour square, a few steps in. Narrow stone streets, colourfully painted windows, old fishermen's houses. Bardot filmed And God Created Woman here in 1956 and introduced Saint-Tropez to the world. It still carries the spirit of that film. In the early morning hours there's almost no one here. Small restaurants and cafés exist but most open in the afternoon.

Gassin — medieval village overlooking the bay

7km from Saint-Tropez, 15 minutes by car. On France's 'Les Plus Beaux Villages' list. The village with the best panoramic view of the bay — nothing obstructing it, just sea. Small church, narrow streets, a few restaurants. Coming here for dinner means escaping the crowds below and gaining the view.

Citadelle de Saint-Tropez

A 17th-century castle on the hill east of town. Contains a maritime history museum inside. But the real reason: the panoramic view of Saint-Tropez bay from the moat and ramparts around the castle. Ticketed but reasonably priced. Quietest in the early morning hours. Standing here looking out at the harbour, Pampelonne and the distant Estérel mountains, you understand why the town became so beloved.

Sentier du Littoral — coastal path

A coastal walking trail from Saint-Tropez extending to Pampelonne and Ramatuelle. 35km in total but can be done in sections. Rocky shores, small coves, pine forests and Mediterranean views. Hot in July-August; spring and autumn are ideal. Walking it, you find your own secret cove — an experience far beyond Saint-Tropez's beach club facade.

Musée de l'Annonciade

Five minutes from the harbour square, in a former chapel. Signac, Matisse, Derain, Bonnard — Post-Impressionist and Fauvist painters lived and worked in Saint-Tropez at the start of the 20th century. This museum holds their works. If it's very hot outside or the harbour is too crowded, stepping into this museum means both cooling down and seeing genuinely fine art. Little-known but of real quality.

Port Grimaud

8km from Saint-Tropez, a canal city built from scratch in the 1960s by architect François Spoerry. Venice-like but Provençal — every house has a boat mooring out front, colourfully painted facades. Boat tours are ticketed; the outer area is free to explore. Perfect for stopping before or after the Sainte-Maxime ferry.
