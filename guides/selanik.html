Thessaloniki

The easygoing city of Byzantium, the waterfront and long tables

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Thessaloniki

Thessaloniki is a port city that shrugs at Athens' hurry: the waterfront invites kilometers of walking, the White Tower watches the sea, and the Ottoman lanes of the upper town, Ano Poli, climb toward the walls. Rome's Rotunda, Byzantium's UNESCO-listed churches, the Ottoman hamams and bedesten all fit into a single walk — and the house where Atatürk was born stands on one of these streets. But what makes Thessaloniki itself is not the stones, it's the rhythm: coffee is drunk long, tables rise late, nobody checks the clock. Its title as Greece's gastronomic capital is no accident.

🗣️ Local Concept: Halara (hah-lah-RAH)

The Greek word for 'relax, don't force it, let it flow': Thessaloniki's factory setting. Coffee takes two hours? Halara. Bus is late? Halara. In this city, hurry counts as bad manners — put your watch in your pocket and switch to halara mode.

3 Unwritten Rules for Thessaloniki

1

Drink your coffee at Thessaloniki speed

Order a frappé or freddo espresso and allow at least an hour: here coffee is not a drink but a way of sitting. No one moves you along and the bill never comes unasked — watching the sea over a waterfront coffee is the city's official sport.

2

Climb to the upper town and see the city from the walls

Ano Poli is the Ottoman fabric that survived the great fire: bay-windowed houses, narrow lanes, stone fountains. From the Trigonion Tower at sunset the whole gulf lies at your feet — walk up, and come down through the side alleys.

3

Surrender to the meze culture

The Thessaloniki table is built on sharing: order ouzo or tsipouro and the mezes arrive alongside, plates set in the middle. No main course is required — four or five mezes, a carafe and long conversation is the complete formula of a Thessaloniki evening.

Getting Around

Thessaloniki Public Transit & Airport Transfers

The center is walkable and the new metro carries the main artery; the waterfront, meanwhile, is the city's finest 'transit line' — on foot.

Thessaloniki Airport is 30–40 minutes from the center: the X1 bus runs day and night and connects to the metro. Taxis run a fixed tariff — the practical choice for late-night landings.

Thessaloniki's driverless metro runs beneath the main street: at Venizelou station an ancient road excavation is displayed under glass — the stop itself is like a museum. The center–station–university line covers a visitor's needs.

From Aristotelous Square to the White Tower, from Ladadika to the Rotunda, everything is walking distance: the waterfront is flat, Ano Poli is uphill. Comfortable shoes + halara pace = the ideal Thessaloniki walk.

For those skipping the Ano Poli climb, buses ascend to the walls; the coastal lines stretch to the eastern beaches. Buy tickets from machines or the app — drivers don't handle change.

Excursion boats tour the gulf from the waterfront: on some you buy a drink instead of a ticket and the deck is yours. The sunset sailing is the most pleasant way to see the White Tower from the sea.

⚡ Pro Tip

The full waterfront is one unbroken walk from the White Tower to the Concert Hall: start half an hour before sunset, pause at the Umbrellas sculpture, and settle into a waterfront cafe on the way back. This route is Thessaloniki's finest 'public transport'.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Thessaloniki

Everyone sees the White Tower; these six are the hidden addresses that open Thessaloniki's layers.

Trigonion Tower terrace

From the bastion at the top of Ano Poli the whole city and gulf lie at your feet: at sunset, local youth gather with thermoses and guitars. Thessaloniki's best view is free — bring something along and sit on the stone.

Atatürk House Museum

The pink house in the garden of the Turkish Consulate: the rooms where Mustafa Kemal was born, original furnishings and documents. Passport or ID required at the entrance — for Turkish visitors, Thessaloniki's most emotional stop.

The Kapani market tables

Small tavernas squeezed between the stalls of the city's oldest market: a table beside the fish crates, the day's freshest on the plate. The market's noise is the soundtrack — go at noon; it closes by evening.

The Rotunda

Galerius' 1700-year-old round mausoleum, once church and once mosque: gold mosaics in the dome, a single minaret at its side. All of Thessaloniki's layers in one building — the inside outshines the outside.

Around the Bit Bazaar

A courtyard of antique dealers and old record shops that turns into little ouzo tables by late afternoon: old Thessaloniki photographs, gramophones, prayer beads. Haggling allowed, hurry forbidden — the treasure hunter's corner.

Eptapyrgio (Yedi Kule)

The fortress at the very top of the walls, known by its Ottoman name Yedi Kule: a prison for many years, today a silent viewpoint. Above even Ano Poli, the city's least-known wall walk starts here.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Thessaloniki

Kalimera
(kah-lee-MEH-rah)
Good morning / good day — greet the baker and the tower clerk alike
Efharisto
(ef-hah-ree-STOH)
Thank you — looks hard, becomes reflex within three days
Yamas!
(YAH-mas)
Cheers! — as the tsipouro glasses rise; make eye contact
Kali orexi
(kah-LEE OH-rek-see)
Enjoy your meal — the waiter says it setting down the plate; say it to your table too
Ela!
(EH-lah)
Come / come on / no way — the multi-purpose exclamation of Greek daily life
