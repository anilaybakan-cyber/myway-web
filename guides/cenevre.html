Geneva

One of the world's most expensive cities — most of the best things are free

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Geneva

Geneva is a small city — but inside it sits the European headquarters of the United Nations, more than 40 international organisations, and the world's densest concentration of diplomatic missions. You notice it when you arrive: everything is too orderly, too clean, too quiet. After 10pm the streets empty. Then you sit on the shore of Lac Léman, the Alps across the water, the Jet d'Eau above, a coffee beside you — and you understand why the city was built here. At Jonction, two rivers meet in different colours. In Carouge, an Italian neighbourhood spirit starts to breathe. Geneva is small but layered.

🗣️ Local Concept: Moitié-Moitié (mwa-tYEY mwa-tYEY)

Half and half. In Geneva, when fondue is mentioned, this is what's meant: half Gruyère, half Vacherin Fribourgeois cheese. This is the standard blend. At a restaurant, don't just say 'fondue' — ask for 'moitié-moitié' and you'll notice the difference. A winter dish; from November to March.

3 Unwritten Rules for Geneva

1

If you're staying in a hotel, all transport is free — ask at check-in

If you're staying in a hotel, hostel or campsite in Geneva, you're entitled to a Geneva Transport Card. For the duration of your stay, tram, bus, train and the yellow ferry (Mouettes Genevoises) are all free. It covers the airport transfer too. Sometimes it doesn't arrive automatically — ask at check-in.

2

An expensive city — but the free things are genuinely good

Restaurant prices are among the highest in Europe; plan accordingly. But don't forget: the Jet d'Eau, the Reformation Wall, walking the Vieille Ville streets, walking to Jonction and the lakeside are all free. Many museums are free on Sundays. Lunch menus are often half the price of dinner at the same restaurant — same quality.

3

Fondue and raclette are winter dishes — don't order them in summer

November to March is the right season for fondue and raclette. In summer they're served at tourist restaurants, but these dishes belong to the cold. The right question in summer is: 'Do you have Filets de perches du Léman?' Fresh lake perch from Lac Léman — if the menu doesn't say 'du Léman', the fish may be frozen and imported from Eastern Europe.

Getting Around

Geneva Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Geneva is small and well connected. Tram, bus, train and yellow ferry all run on the same ticket — and if you're staying in a hotel, it's all free.

✈️

Airport → City

From the train station in the basement of the airport to Gare Cornavin: 7 minutes, every 10-15 minutes. After collecting your baggage, show your boarding pass at the ticket machines to get a free 80-minute ticket. If you have the Geneva Transport Card, you don't need this — it's already free.

🚋

TPG — tram and bus

6 tram lines (numbered 12-17) and a comprehensive bus network cover every corner of the city. A single ticket is valid for 60 minutes; free transfers between tram, bus, train and Mouettes. Tickets are bought at machines at stops — city buses have no machines on board, so buy before you get on.

⛴️

Mouettes Genevoises — yellow ferry

Small yellow boats linking the two shores of the lake, on the same TPG ticket. Line M1 runs from Pâquis to Eaux-Vives, line M2 from near the Mont-Blanc Bridge to Genève-Plage. A short crossing, but over Lac Léman — the easiest way to see the city from a different angle.

🚲

Bike — Geneveroule

Directly across from Gare Cornavin. Leave your ID and a 20 CHF deposit for a free day bike. Flat, safe routes along the lakeside to Carouge and Plainpalais. Season May to October.

🚶

Walking — ideal for the city centre

Vieille Ville, Pâquis and the lakeside are all connected on foot. Gare Cornavin to the Jet d'Eau: 20 minutes. Carouge: 15 minutes by tram or 35 minutes on foot. Jonction: 25 minutes' walk from the centre.

💡 Geneva Transport Card — ask at check-in

If you're staying in a hotel, hostel or campsite in Geneva, you're entitled to this card. It covers all TPG vehicles, trains and the Mouettes within Zone 10 for the duration of your stay. It sometimes isn't sent automatically — if you don't ask at check-in, you might miss it. Covers the airport transfer too. Public transport in Geneva isn't cheap; this card is a real saving.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Geneva: Best Areas

Geneva is small but its neighbourhoods are very different. Where you stay directly shapes how you experience the city.

Rive / Eaux-Vives

Most central

On the left bank of the lake, walking distance from the Jet d'Eau. The Jardin Anglais, shopping streets and the Halles de Rive food market are here. Easy tram connections everywhere. The right choice if it's your first time and you want easy access to everything.

📍 Walking distance to Jet d'Eau • Halles de Rive • Easy access to everything

Pâquis

Most lively

Right next to Gare Cornavin, on the right bank of the lake. Geneva's most multicultural neighbourhood; Asian, African and Arab communities live here. Bains des Pâquis is the summer gathering point on the lakeside. Restaurants and bars stay open late — the least quiet part of the city.

📍 Bains des Pâquis • Next to Gare Cornavin • Multicultural, lively

Vieille Ville / Old Town

Most historic

On top of the hill, with the Cathédrale Saint-Pierre and the Reformation Wall. Narrow stone streets, historic buildings, small cafés. The most touristic part of Geneva, but genuinely beautiful in the quiet hours — early morning or late afternoon. The hill is tiring but the views are worth it.

📍 Cathédrale Saint-Pierre • Reformation Wall • Panoramic views

Carouge

Most characterful

Technically a separate municipality attached to Geneva. 15 minutes by tram. Italian courtyard architecture, a weekend market, local restaurants. Completely different from the rest of the city's cool and orderly atmosphere. Go to Carouge for dinner; stay there.

📍 15 min by tram • Weekend market • Most authentic neighbourhood

Plainpalais / Jonction

Most local

Student and artist neighbourhood around the University of Geneva. The Plainpalais square turns into the city's best second-hand market on Wednesdays and Saturdays. Jonction is where the Rhône and the Arve meet — where locals swim in summer. The furthest from any tourist atmosphere.

📍 Plainpalais second-hand market • Jonction confluence • Student neighbourhood
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Geneva

Geneva lives each season differently. In summer it's the lake; in winter it's the Alps and the silence.

Spring

Apr – Jun
The most balanced time

From late April to June, Geneva's most balanced period. The lake is still cold but the shore is beautiful. The Jardin Anglais and Parc La Grange are in bloom. Crowds haven't built up yet, flights are cheaper than summer peak. Bike season opens. May is when the city is at its best: not too cold, not too crowded.

Summer

Jul – Aug
Lake season

July and August are the busiest months but the city never feels overwhelming. The lake is ready for swimming. The Geneva Festival in early August — a large fireworks display. Fresh Léman perch instead of fondue; the right choice in summer.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
The most underrated season

September and October: the crowds have gone, the weather is still mild, the lake is calm and blue. Fondue season opens. By late October, Favarger's annual chocolate pots are appearing in confectioners' windows — the first sign of L'Escalade.

Winter

Dec – Mar
Silence and the Alps

December briefly comes alive with the Christmas market and L'Escalade celebrations. January and February are when the city empties of tourists. The Bise wind — cold and sharp from the north — can blow for days; be ready for it. The Alps are close for skiing: Chamonix is an hour away, Les Gets an hour and a half. Geneva in winter is very quiet — the best time for those who like that.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Geneva

A city at the heart of Swiss-French cooking. Cheese, freshwater fish, local sausage — all different when eaten in the right place.

🧀

Fondue moitié-moitié

Geneva's classic fondue: half Gruyère, half Vacherin Fribourgeois. Bread pieces dipped in melted cheese with a long fork. The right season for fondue runs from November to March. Avoid the restaurants around Gare Cornavin — they use cheap cheese blends and the difference is noticeable. Bains des Pâquis or restaurants in Carouge are the reliable addresses.

⭐ Nov–Mar · Bains des Pâquis or Carouge · reservation required
🧀

Raclette

A half wheel of cheese heated beside the fire, the melted part scraped directly onto the plate. Served with boiled potatoes, cornichons and onions. The electric tabletop version is a shortcut — the real thing is made with a half wheel of cheese.

⭐ Winter · must be from a half wheel · tabletop version isn't the real thing
🌭

Longeole

A local sausage unique to Geneva, with protected geographical indication (PDO). Made with pork, fennel seeds and spices; produced nowhere else. One of the city's official dishes. Served with lentils or dried beans. Found at local restaurants in Carouge and at Halles de Rive.

⭐ Geneva's own PDO · Carouge and Halles de Rive
🐟

Filets de perches du Léman

Fresh lake perch fillets from Lac Léman — the main dish at lakeside restaurants in summer. If the menu doesn't say 'du Léman', the fish isn't fresh — look for that label. Lightly floured and fried in butter. May to September.

⭐ May–Sep · du Léman label essential · lakeside restaurants
🍫

Marmite de l'Escalade

A chocolate pot produced by Favarger each year from mid-November to mid-December. Commemorates the 1602 victory. Filled with small sweets; the tradition is to break it and share the contents. Geneva's most local chocolate experience — unavailable the rest of the year.

⭐ Mid-Nov to mid-Dec · Favarger · annual production
🛒

Halles de Rive

In Rive, right next to the lake. Geneva's best covered food market. Local cheese sellers, butchers, fishmongers and bakeries together. Longeole, Gruyère and fresh perch here. Not a tourist destination; this is where Genevans do their shopping. Open every day, busiest in the mornings.

⭐ Rive · open every day · best in the morning
🍷 Avoid the area around Gare Cornavin

The stretch of Rue du Mont-Blanc around the station has dozens of places serving fondue and raclette. Cheap cheese, thin watery fondue, frozen fish. Avoid these restaurants. Walk a little further: Carouge, Bains des Pâquis, or Les Armures and Café Papon in the Vieille Ville are all much better options.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Geneva

Just behind Geneva's diplomatic face, a completely different city is waiting.

✨ Two rivers, two colours — flowing side by side without mixing

La Pointe de la Jonction

At the western edge of the city, the Rhône and the Arve meet. One is turquoise-green, the other muddy brown — the two colours flow side by side for a long stretch without mixing. Clearly visible to the eye. In summer you can swim in safe areas. Easily reached on foot or by bike. Free, always open.

✨ Switzerland's strangest buildings — the Smurf neighbourhood

Quartier des Grottes

A few minutes' walk northwest of Gare Cornavin. In the 1980s, residents of a neighbourhood facing demolition occupied their buildings; architects designed organic, wavy, multicoloured façades. Now known as the 'Smurf buildings'. The complete opposite of Geneva's orderly and sterile character. Art studios and alternative cafés in the courtyards. Free, always open.

✨ A 120-metre wooden bench and the official announcement of spring

Promenade de la Treille

Just above the Vieille Ville, along the edge of Parc des Bastions. The wooden bench here, at 120 metres, is among the longest in the world. Each year, the arrival of spring is officially announced by the first bud on the chestnut tree beside this path. Panoramic views over the city, walking distance to the Reformation Wall.

✨ Where the World Wide Web was born — and the Large Hadron Collider

CERN

At the western edge of the city, on the Swiss-French border. The world's largest particle physics laboratory and the birthplace of the World Wide Web. Entry to the Science Gateway, opened in 2023, is free — but registration is required. Online registration can be made up to a month in advance and guarantees entry; on-site registration is also possible but subject to availability. Guided tours are also free but open for on-site booking only, two hours before the tour starts — they fill up very quickly. Around 30 minutes by tram.

✨ A sculpture, a treaty — and Geneva never let it go

Place des Nations — Broken Chair

In front of the United Nations building, a giant three-legged wooden chair. The fourth leg is broken — representing landmines and unexploded ordnance. Erected temporarily in 1997 to push for the signing of the Ottawa Treaty banning landmines. Geneva never took it down. A guided tour of the Palais des Nations is ticketed, but entry to the square is free.

✨ Swimming, sauna and fondue by the lake — open all year

Bains des Pâquis

A wooden jetty extending into the lake on the Pâquis shore. In summer an open-air pool and hammam: swimming, sunbathing, sauna, light food. Doesn't close in winter — one of the most popular spots for winter fondue. Ticketed but reasonably priced. In winter, reservations for fondue are essential; weekends fill up fast.

✨ Plainpalais — the city's best second-hand market

Every Wednesday and Saturday morning in Plainpalais square. Books, records, vintage clothing, antiques, electronics. Geneva's most colourful and varied open-air market. Best in the early morning. Free entry.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Geneva has few traps — but the prices themselves can be one. Know these four and protect your Swiss budget.

🚕 TRAP

Taking a taxi from the airport

Paying 40 francs for a taxi when a 7-minute train exists is the city's classic tourist mistake — and the train ticket is free in the baggage hall.

💡 What to Do Instead: Take the free 80-minute ticket from the machine by the baggage belts; at your hotel, ask for the free Geneva Transport Card.
⌚ TRAP

A 'Swiss watch' from a souvenir shop

Some of the cheap 'Swiss made' watches and knives in the Rue du Mont-Blanc windows are imported imitations.

💡 What to Do Instead: For the real thing go to an authorised dealer; for a budget gift, Swiss chocolate is always the right answer.
🫕 TRAP

Insisting on fondue in summer

It sits on menus year-round, but fondue is a winter dish; served to tourists in August it loses both its taste and its setting.

💡 What to Do Instead: In summer eat filets de perche from the lake; save fondue for a cold evening and a stone-walled brasserie.
💸 TRAP

Turning the lake into a paid package

Expensive 'city tour' packages sell a route that is already free: the Jet d'Eau view, the lakeside, the Old Town.

💡 What to Do Instead: The yellow 'Mouettes' water taxis cross the lake free with your transport card; your feet handle the rest.
🛡️ The golden rule

You're in one of the priciest cities on earth, but its best things are free: the lake, the mountain views, the Old Town, a Bains des Pâquis sunset. Save the money for food and chocolate.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Geneva

Geneva speaks French, but the Swiss way: calm, polite, and with words of its own.

🗣️ Bonjour
Hello — the mandatory first word of every exchange
🗣️ Merci
Thank you — you'll hear it constantly in Switzerland
🗣️ S'il vous plaît
Please — add it to the end of your order
🗣️ Santé!

🗣️ Adieu!
The friendly greeting of French-speaking Switzerland — both hello and goodbye
