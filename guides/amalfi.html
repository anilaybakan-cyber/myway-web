Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Amalfi Coast

The Amalfi Coast is a 50-kilometre stretch of coastline — but those 50 kilometres contain Italy's steepest, narrowest and most dramatic road. Lemon groves hanging over cliffs, small fishing villages, medieval cathedrals, colourful houses. The best thing about this coast is that all its towns are small: Amalfi town has 5,000 people, Positano 4,000, Ravello 2,500. Not crowded — but in summer the tourist wave changes everything. Come in the right season, at the right hour, knowing when high prices are unavoidable, and views you'll find nowhere else will be waiting.

🗣️ Local Concept: Sfusato Amalfitano (sfoo-ZA-to a-mal-fi-TA-no)

The lemon variety unique to the Amalfi Coast. Bigger, longer and more aromatic than regular lemons — thick skin, mildly acidic. Grown on cliff terraces for centuries. This lemon doesn't just make limoncello; pasta al limone, delizia al limone, fresh lemon squeezed over seafood — this lemon is in every dish on the coast. The bunches of lemons hanging in front of shops are not just decoration — they are a symbol of this land.

3 Unwritten Rules for Amalfi Coast

1

Don't come by car in midsummer — you'll regret it

The SS163 coastal road is both narrow and winding. Hours of gridlock are possible in July and August. If you're hiring a car and heading to the coast, go in early June or mid-September. If you're coming in midsummer: leave the car in Naples or Salerno and continue by SITA bus or coastal ferry. You don't want to get stuck in traffic by car.

2

Prices are high — but manageable

The Amalfi Coast is one of Italy's more expensive regions. A main course in Positano can start from €25-40. But on the same coast, in lesser-known towns like Cetara, Minori and Maiori, trattoria prices are much more reasonable — €12-18. Restaurants with views are always premium; two streets back the price drops but the quality doesn't change.

3

Don't try to see every town in the same day

Positano, Amalfi, Ravello, Cetara, Atrani — all very different and each needs at least half a day. Fitting everything into one day is possible but means truly experiencing none of them. If you stay three nights you can comfortably see three towns. If you're coming for a day trip, choose one town and stay there.

Getting Around

Amalfi Coast Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Two options along the coast: SITA bus or ferry. The ferry is always more beautiful, the bus always cheaper.

🚂 Getting here from Naples or Salerno

The nearest train stations to the Amalfi Coast are Naples Centrale, Sorrento (via Circumvesuviana) and Salerno. From Naples: Circumvesuviana to Sorrento, then SITA bus to Positano and Amalfi. From Salerno: direct SITA bus or ferry. Salerno is usually overlooked but is a practical entry point.

🚌 SITA Bus — the coast's backbone

Two main routes: Sorrento–Positano–Amalfi and Amalfi–Salerno. Single fare €2-3 depending on route. Daily Costiera SITA Pass €10 — unlimited rides. Tickets from tabacchi shops, cafés and machines at stops. Validate your ticket when boarding — tickets cannot be bought on the bus. In summer buses are very crowded; travelling with large luggage is nearly impossible.

⛴️ Ferry — scenic and comfortable

From April to October ferry services run between Naples, Sorrento, Positano, Amalfi and Salerno. In winter, subject to weather conditions, some routes may stop. Prices €8-20 depending on route. Travelmar, NLG and Positano Jet are the main operators. In summer book tickets in advance. The ferry journey itself is the only way to see the coast from the sea.

🚗 Car rental — proceed with caution

The SS163 coastal road is challenging with single-lane sections, blind bends and long tunnels. Congestion is unavoidable in summer. Motorino or scooter are popular in coastal towns but can be dangerous on narrow roads. If coming by car, use car parks outside the towns — parking inside is nearly impossible and expensive.

🚶 Walking trails

Sentiero degli Dei (Path of the Gods) is the most famous — Bomerano to Nocelle, the whole bay visible from above. About 7 kilometres, 2-3 hours, moderate difficulty. Inter-town paths also exist but are steep and tiring. Comfortable shoes essential. Avoid walking at midday in midsummer.

💡 Start in the early morning to beat the crowds

Positano and Amalfi town start filling from 10-11am. If you set off by ferry or SITA bus at 8am you can spend the first hours in near-empty places. After 5pm most day-trippers leave too — the towns come back to life, prices stay the same but the crowds thin.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Amalfi Coast: Best Areas

Each town has a different character — Positano glamorous, Amalfi historic, Ravello quiet, Cetara local.

Positano Most photogenic

The coast's most iconic town with colourful houses clinging to the cliff, narrow stepped streets and Spiaggia Grande beach. And also the most expensive. Dinner starts from €40-60 per person; hotel nights in high season are hard to find below €300. But the view is beyond dispute. Crowded during midsummer days; the early morning hours and late afternoon are the finest moments.

Most iconic view • Most expensive town • Spiaggia Grande
Amalfi town Most historic

A powerful maritime republic in the 9th century; today a small town of 5,000. Its cathedral on Piazza Duomo, narrow streets and the Museo della Carta — which tells the history of a town that was home to Italy's first paper mills in the 13th century — give it a completely distinctive character. The main SITA bus hub. 10 minutes on foot to Atrani.

Duomo Cathedral • SITA bus hub • Walking distance to Atrani
Ravello Most quiet

Above the coast, at 350 metres elevation. Villa Rufolo and Villa Cimbrone gardens are here. Wagner composed here, Virginia Woolf wrote here. In summer the Ravello Festival — open-air concerts, theatre. The town is small and quiet — ideal for escaping the coast's chaos.

Villa Cimbrone gardens • Ravello Festival • Silence above the coast
Cetara Most local

In the east of the coast, a small fishing town most tourists don't visit. The home of colatura di alici. Waterfront cafés and restaurants are both affordable and good. Limited accommodation but reasonably priced — 20 minutes to Amalfi by SITA bus.

Colatura di alici • Fishing town • Affordable local food
Praiano Positano alternative

A small town between Positano and Amalfi, perched on the cliff. Views similar to Positano but prices and crowds far lower. The terrace view in front of San Gennaro Church is free and breathtaking. Its beach Marina di Praia — small, sheltered, beautiful.

Positano views for less • Marina di Praia beach • San Gennaro Church terrace

Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Amalfi Coast

The Amalfi Coast can be visited year-round but each season offers a very different experience.

Spring Apr – May
The best time

April and May are the coast's most beautiful period. Lemon groves in blossom, temperatures 18-22°C, tourists present but far from summer levels. Prices 30-40% lower than midsummer. The sea is still a little cool but swimming is possible. Walking trails are ideal this season — the view from Sentiero degli Dei in mist and cloud is dramatic.

Summer Jun – Aug
Beautiful but crowded

June is the coast's most balanced summer month; the sea is beautiful and the crowds haven't yet peaked. July and August are the coast's most intense period. Positano's narrow streets are packed, buses give no room to breathe, finding a spot on the beach is hard. The sea is at its most beautiful but the town is at its most tiring. Before 8am and after 6pm are the hours the coastal strip gets a little breathing space.

Autumn Sep – Nov
Second best time

September and October are the coast's most comfortable period. Summer crowds have gone, the weather is still mild, the sea is still warm enough to swim. Prices have dropped from midsummer levels. Worth bearing in mind: rain can increase in November and some ferry services end.

Winter Dec – Feb
Quiet and authentic

From December to February the coast quietens. Some hotels and restaurants are closed, ferry services are reduced, but the towns show their real face. Temperatures 10-15°C, occasionally rainy. Ideal for winter walking — walking through the clouds on Sentiero degli Dei is a different sensation altogether.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Amalfi Coast

The coast's cuisine is built on seafood, lemon and anchovies. Restaurants with views are expensive — two streets back you'll find better value and equally delicious food.

🍝 Scialatielli ai frutti di mare

Amalfi's distinctive handmade pasta. Thicker than spaghetti, shorter than linguine. The dough is prepared fresh every morning. Served with a mixed seafood combination of prawns, mussels, squid and scallops. With fresh basil, tomatoes and olive oil. The morning's catch from the sea, on your plate the same day. You'll understand this freshness from the very first bite.

⭐ Amalfi town restaurants · fresh and local · €18–25
🍝 Spaghetti con colatura di alici

Spaghetti with garlic, chilli and olive oil, then a few drops of colatura di alici (Cetara's fermented anchovy sauce). Those few drops transform the whole dish: salty, umami and deep in flavour. The recipe looks simple but the power of colatura takes it somewhere completely different. Eat this dish in Cetara — the freshest and most affordable version is there.

⭐ Eat in Cetara · best and most affordable · €12–18
🍋 Pasta al limone

Fresh pasta made with the zest and juice of sfusato amalfitano lemons, ricotta or cream, parmesan and olive oil. The coast's simplest and most local dish. A good pasta al limone gives you the scent of lemon without weighing you down.

⭐ All coast restaurants · €14–22
🍰 Delizia al limone

A small, dome-shaped sponge cake — filled with limoncello cream, coated in lemon glaze. The most characteristic dessert of the Amalfi Coast. Must be served cold — the flavour drops if eaten warm. €3-5 at pastry shops.

⭐ Pastry shops and cafés · served cold · €3–5
🍋 Limoncello

Sfusato amalfitano lemon peel left in alcohol, then sugar syrup added — a yellow, intense, cold-served liqueur. Usually served as a free digestivo at the end of a restaurant meal. When buying, prefer producer stalls.

⭐ Everywhere · free after dinner · buy from a producer stall
🧀 Provolone del Monaco

A semi-hard cheese from the Monti Lattari mountains, made from cow's milk. DOP (Protected Designation of Origin) certified — a guarantee of production in a specific area by traditional methods. The fresh version is mild, the aged version sharp and complex. Found in cheese shops and delis along the coast.

⭐ Cheese shops · DOP certified · try the aged version
🍷 Eating in Positano — how to spend less

Restaurants facing the sea and with views in Positano are always premium priced. Two streets in — that is, climbing uphill — prices drop without any change in quality. Lunch is always cheaper than dinner. Eating a plate of food at a bar is cheaper than at a restaurant. Buying a panino or a slice of pizza and eating on the beach is both cheap and the real taste of Positano.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Amalfi Coast

The coast's best places are usually one step away from the ferry route or just over a hilltop.

Atrani Right next to Amalfi — a town almost no one knows

Just east of Amalfi town, 10 minutes on foot or 5 minutes through the tunnel. One of Italy's smallest towns. A small beach, steep streets, a church on the square and a few restaurants. A tenth of Amalfi's crowds. Same view, same coast, same sea — but neither tourist crowds nor high prices.

Cetara Cetara — birthplace of colatura di alici and the coast's most local town

East of Amalfi, 20 minutes by bus. One of the coast's smallest and least touristy towns. The annual tuna festival on 16 August — the whole town celebrates. Waterfront cafés and trattorias are both affordable and delicious. Eating here is roughly half the price of eating in Positano. Visit the small shops in town to buy colatura.

Ravello — Villa Cimbrone The infinity terrace on Villa Cimbrone's cliff edge

In Ravello town, 15 minutes on foot. The Belvedere of Infinity — a cliff-edge terrace with nothing in front of it, only the Amalfi Bay. It was Gore Vidal's favourite spot; Virginia Woolf also wrote here. The garden entry is ticketed but worth it for the terrace view.

Praiano — Marina di Praia Marina di Praia — the coast's most sheltered small cove

Below Praiano town, reached by steep steps down. Rock on both sides, open sea ahead. A small beach, one or two restaurants and a bar. Even in midsummer, far quieter than Positano or Amalfi. Prepare your legs for the descent — many steps.

Furore — La Fiordo Furore Fjord — the breathtaking cove seen in photographs

A small stop on the SS163 road between Positano and Amalfi. The real secret here is the narrow inlet opening to the sea between the rocks — like a fjord. You look down from the bridge at the roadside, or descend by steps to reach the small beach. The Tuffo del Furore is held here every year: a diving competition jumping from the bridge, with spectators coming from the whole coast. Get off at the SITA bus Furore stop.

Minori and Maiori Minori and Maiori — the coast's most relaxed and affordable towns

Two towns east of Amalfi, close to each other. Minori is small and historic with ancient Roman villa remains; Maiori has the coast's largest sandy beach. Both are far less touristy and much more affordable than Positano and Amalfi. An open-air market sets up in Maiori on Thursdays. Stroll between the stalls and enjoy the locally produced vegetables, fruit and cheeses.

✨ Limoncello from producer stalls — the price difference is significant

Small stalls line the roadside along the coast — lemon grove owners sell fresh lemons, homemade limoncello and lemon jam. Limoncello in tourist shops costs €15-25; at these stalls the same quality is €8-12. You can try before you buy too — producers usually offer small tastings.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

On the Amalfi Coast the view is free; the rest wants negotiating. Know these four and live paradise without melting your budget.

🏖️ TRAP

The sunbed surprise in Positano

The front rows of the main beach charge serious money for a day's sunbed and umbrella; finding out after sitting down is unpleasant.

💡 What to Do Instead: Ask the price before you sit; or walk to Fornillo beach — five minutes, half the price, the same sea.
🍋 TRAP

Fake 'Amalfi lemon' products

Some of the limoncello and soaps on tourist stalls are made with imported essence; the real Costa d'Amalfi lemon is IGP-protected.

💡 What to Do Instead: Look for the IGP label and buy from a producer who lets you taste; lemon-grove tours like Amalfi's Valle dei Mulini give you the story and the bottle.
🍽️ TRAP

A mediocre plate at view prices in Positano

Some cliff terraces run on photo menus: the view is heavenly, the pasta frozen.

💡 What to Do Instead: Eat in Praiano, Cetara or Atrani: same sea, half the price, twice the care. Cetara's anchovies are a reason to travel in themselves.
🚗 TRAP

Driving the coast road in August

In summer the SS163 is a queue with no parking at the end; a holiday spent at the wheel is not a holiday.

💡 What to Do Instead: Make the ferry your spine (Salerno–Amalfi–Positano) and fill the gaps with SITA buses and footpaths; forget renting a car on the coast.
🛡️ The golden rule

The Amalfi rule is about altitude: climb one town above sea level (Ravello, Scala, Tramonti) and prices fall, silence grows, and the view gets bigger.

🗣️ Grazie
