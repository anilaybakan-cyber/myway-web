Cannes

The film festival lasts just one week — the real Cannes begins in the other 51

Overview

The Vibe of Cannes

When you hear Cannes, you think red carpet, yacht harbour and film festival. But the city itself goes far beyond all of that. The old town perched on Le Suquet hill, the pine forests of the Lérins Islands, the morning crowds at Forville Market, the shopkeepers of Rue Meynadier — these are the real face of Cannes. The Croisette is the city's most expensive and most touristy facade; one street back, prices drop and the atmosphere changes entirely. Cannes is a small city — everything is walkable, and drinking an espresso while watching the yachts is a French Riviera ritual in its own right.

🗣️ Local Concept: La Croisette (la krwa-SET)

Cannes' famous seafront boulevard — 2 kilometres from the yacht harbour to Palm Beach. Luxury hotels, boutiques, sandy beaches and the red carpet during the film festival. Touristy and expensive, but worth walking once. The real tip: right next to the paid private beach concessions on the Croisette there are free public beaches — same sea, half the price.

Insider Advice

3 Unwritten Rules for Cannes

1

If you're coming during the Film Festival, book well in advance

During the Cannes Film Festival, which runs for about two weeks each May, the city fills up completely. Hotel prices triple or quadruple, restaurants are packed, the Croisette is surrounded by barriers. You can't get into the festival itself — accreditation is required. But parallel events and public screenings are free to experience the atmosphere. If you want to come during this period, book at least six months ahead.

2

Step one street back from the Croisette — prices halve

Restaurants on or facing the Croisette are two to three times more expensive than the rest of Cannes. The small bistros and cafés on Rue Meynadier, Rue d'Antibes and in Le Suquet neighbourhood are both cheaper and far more local. Go to the Croisette for the view but step one street back for food.

3

Take the early morning ferry to the Lérins Islands

Sainte-Marguerite and Saint-Honorat islands are just 15 minutes by ferry from Cannes. In summer the afternoons get very crowded. If you take the 9am or 10am ferry you'll find the islands nearly empty. On Sainte-Marguerite there's the castle where the Man in the Iron Mask was held; on Saint-Honorat, living monks produce their own wine. Separate tickets are needed for each island.
