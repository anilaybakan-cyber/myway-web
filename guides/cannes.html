Cannes

The film festival lasts just one week — the real Cannes begins in the other 51

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Cannes

When you hear Cannes, you think red carpet, yacht harbour and film festival. But the city itself goes far beyond all of that. The old town perched on Le Suquet hill, the pine forests of the Lérins Islands, the morning crowds at Forville Market, the shopkeepers of Rue Meynadier — these are the real face of Cannes. The Croisette is the city's most expensive and most touristy facade; one street back, prices drop and the atmosphere changes entirely. Cannes is a small city — everything is walkable, and drinking an espresso while watching the yachts is a French Riviera ritual in its own right.

🗣️ Local Concept: La Croisette (la krwa-SET)

Cannes' famous seafront boulevard — 2 kilometres from the yacht harbour to Palm Beach. Luxury hotels, boutiques, sandy beaches and the red carpet during the film festival. Touristy and expensive, but worth walking once. The real tip: right next to the paid private beach concessions on the Croisette there are free public beaches — same sea, half the price.

3 Unwritten Rules for Cannes

1

If you're coming during the Film Festival, book well in advance

During the Cannes Film Festival, which runs for about two weeks each May, the city fills up completely. Hotel prices triple or quadruple, restaurants are packed, the Croisette is surrounded by barriers. You can't get into the festival itself — accreditation is required. But parallel events and public screenings are free to experience the atmosphere. If you want to come during this period, book at least six months ahead.

2

Step one street back from the Croisette — prices halve

Restaurants on or facing the Croisette are two to three times more expensive than the rest of Cannes. The small bistros and cafés on Rue Meynadier, Rue d'Antibes and in Le Suquet neighbourhood are both cheaper and far more local. Go to the Croisette for the view but step one street back for food.

3

Take the early morning ferry to the Lérins Islands

Sainte-Marguerite and Saint-Honorat islands are just 15 minutes by ferry from Cannes. In summer the afternoons get very crowded. If you take the 9am or 10am ferry you'll find the islands nearly empty. On Sainte-Marguerite there's the castle where the Man in the Iron Mask was held; on Saint-Honorat, living monks produce their own wine. Separate tickets are needed for each island.

Getting Around

Cannes Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Cannes is small and walkable. Palm Bus is sufficient for getting around the city; trains are faster for neighbouring towns.

Nice Côte d'Azur Airport is the nearest airport to Cannes — about 30 kilometres away. The most practical option: get to Nice-Ville train station, then take the train to Cannes in 30-40 minutes. Express bus (Ligne Azur 210) goes direct from the airport to Cannes train station, about 1 hour, €19.50. Fixed-price taxi around €80. The train-bus combination is both affordable and reasonably fast.

Cannes train station is in the city centre. 30-40 minutes from Nice, 10 minutes from Antibes, 25 minutes from Grasse. TER regional trains run frequently. Ideal for day trips: Monaco, Antibes, Juan-les-Pins, Nice are all reachable by train. Ticket prices €5-15 depending on the route.

Cannes' yellow buses cover every point in the city. Single fare €1.80, valid for 74 minutes with free connections within that time. Day pass €4.60. Tickets from the driver or machines at stops. Direct payment by contactless bank card is also possible. Palm Imperial — red open-topped panoramic bus — runs along the Croisette for sightseeing, €1.50 single fare.

Daily ferry services from the Vieux-Port (old harbour) pier to Sainte-Marguerite and Saint-Honorat islands. Return to Sainte-Marguerite approximately €18, Saint-Honorat separate ticket. Planaria, Trans Côte d'Azur and Riviera Lines are the main operators. Busy in summer — going in the early morning hours is recommended. Fewer sailings in winter, check in advance.

Cannes centre is compact. From the train station: Forville Market 10 minutes, Le Suquet hilltop 15 minutes, Vieux-Port 10 minutes, the full length of the Croisette 25-30 minutes on foot. The climb to Le Suquet is steep but short. An evening walk along the Croisette is one of Cannes' most classic experiences.

⚡ Pro Tip

Antibes is 10 minutes from Cannes by train. Picasso Museum, old city walls and a Provençal market. Juan-les-Pins jazz festival every July. Nice is 30-40 minutes. All reachable by TER regional train, tickets €5-12. For day trip planning, set off from Cannes in the early morning and return in the afternoon.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Cannes

Cannes' finest corners are one step away from the Croisette or a few metres higher up.

Le Suquet hilltop — Notre-Dame d'Espérance

Climb the steps from Vieux-Port and you reach the top of Le Suquet. From in front of Notre-Dame d'Espérance Church you can see the yacht harbour, the Croisette and the Estérel mountains. Go 30 minutes before sunset — the light hits this hill directly. Bring a bottle of rosé and sit on the terrace. A tenth of the Croisette's crowds, free and far more beautiful.

Île Sainte-Marguerite

15 minutes by ferry from Vieux-Port. At Fort Royal you can see the cell where the Man in the Iron Mask was held — whether fact or legend is debated, but the cell itself is real. The rest of the island is pine and eucalyptus forest, clear sea coves and walking trails. Even in midsummer it stays peaceful. Have lunch on the island — the restaurant is simple but the fish is fresh.

Île Saint-Honorat

The smaller island next to Sainte-Marguerite, reached by a separate ferry. An active monastery since the 5th century — monks still live here today and tend their vines. The monastery wine is world-renowned; tastings and sales are available to visitors. The island is small, walkable in its entirety. Can be visited on the same day as Sainte-Marguerite — buy separate tickets for each island.

Vallauris — Picasso's pottery village

20 minutes from Cannes by bus. Picasso lived here between 1948 and 1955 and discovered the art of ceramics here. Active pottery workshops and small galleries still exist today. Musée National Picasso La Guerre et la Paix — a chapel that Picasso personally painted and donated to the town. Ticketed but affordable, not crowded, genuinely impressive.

Grasse — perfume city

25-30 minutes from Cannes by train or bus. The world's perfume capital — the raw materials for Chanel No.5 come from here. Fragonard, Molinard and Galimard perfume factories offer free tours; workshop sessions are ticketed. The old town has narrow streets and Provençal breakfast spots. In February-March mimosa blooms along the roads.

Forville antiques market

Every Monday Forville Market transforms from food into an antiques, vintage and second-hand market. Old photographs, frames, jewellery, books, plates. Prices far lower than auction houses. The best finds when stalls are being set up at 7am. Sellers are generally open to bargaining. A completely different and far more authentic Cannes experience, in contrast to the Croisette's glamour.
