Cannes

The film festival lasts just one week — the real Cannes begins in the other 51

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Cannes

When you hear Cannes, you think red carpet, yacht harbour and film festival. But the city itself goes far beyond all of that. The old town perched on Le Suquet hill, the pine forests of the Lérins Islands, the morning crowds at Forville Market, the shopkeepers of Rue Meynadier — these are the real face of Cannes. The Croisette is the city's most expensive and most touristy facade; one street back, prices drop and the atmosphere changes entirely. Cannes is a small city — everything is walkable, and drinking an espresso while watching the yachts is a French Riviera ritual in its own right.

🗣️ Local Concept: La Croisette (la krwa-SET)

Cannes' famous seafront boulevard — 2 kilometres from the yacht harbour to Palm Beach. Luxury hotels, boutiques, sandy beaches and the red carpet during the film festival. Touristy and expensive, but worth walking once. The real tip: right next to the paid private beach concessions on the Croisette there are free public beaches — same sea, half the price.

3 Unwritten Rules for Cannes

1

If you're coming during the Film Festival, book well in advance

During the Cannes Film Festival, which runs for about two weeks each May, the city fills up completely. Hotel prices triple or quadruple, restaurants are packed, the Croisette is surrounded by barriers. You can't get into the festival itself — accreditation is required. But parallel events and public screenings are free to experience the atmosphere. If you want to come during this period, book at least six months ahead.

2

Step one street back from the Croisette — prices halve

Restaurants on or facing the Croisette are two to three times more expensive than the rest of Cannes. The small bistros and cafés on Rue Meynadier, Rue d'Antibes and in Le Suquet neighbourhood are both cheaper and far more local. Go to the Croisette for the view but step one street back for food.

3

Take the early morning ferry to the Lérins Islands

Sainte-Marguerite and Saint-Honorat islands are just 15 minutes by ferry from Cannes. In summer the afternoons get very crowded. If you take the 9am or 10am ferry you'll find the islands nearly empty. On Sainte-Marguerite there's the castle where the Man in the Iron Mask was held; on Saint-Honorat, living monks produce their own wine. Separate tickets are needed for each island.

Getting Around

Cannes Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Cannes is small and walkable. Palm Bus is sufficient for getting around the city; trains are faster for neighbouring towns.

✈️

From Nice Airport to Cannes

Nice Côte d'Azur Airport is the nearest airport to Cannes — about 30 kilometres away. The most practical option: get to Nice-Ville train station, then take the train to Cannes in 30-40 minutes. Express bus (Ligne Azur 210) goes direct from the airport to Cannes train station, about 1 hour, €19.50. Fixed-price taxi around €80. The train-bus combination is both affordable and reasonably fast.

🚂

Train — along the French Riviera

Cannes train station is in the city centre. 30-40 minutes from Nice, 10 minutes from Antibes, 25 minutes from Grasse. TER regional trains run frequently. Ideal for day trips: Monaco, Antibes, Juan-les-Pins, Nice are all reachable by train. Ticket prices €5-15 depending on the route.

🚌

Palm Bus — city transport

Cannes' yellow buses cover every point in the city. Single fare €1.80, valid for 74 minutes with free connections within that time. Day pass €4.60. Tickets from the driver or machines at stops. Direct payment by contactless bank card is also possible. Palm Imperial — red open-topped panoramic bus — runs along the Croisette for sightseeing, €1.50 single fare.

⛴️

Lérins Islands ferry

Daily ferry services from the Vieux-Port (old harbour) pier to Sainte-Marguerite and Saint-Honorat islands. Return to Sainte-Marguerite approximately €18, Saint-Honorat separate ticket. Planaria, Trans Côte d'Azur and Riviera Lines are the main operators. Busy in summer — going in the early morning hours is recommended. Fewer sailings in winter, check in advance.

🚶

Walking — the best option

Cannes centre is compact. From the train station: Forville Market 10 minutes, Le Suquet hilltop 15 minutes, Vieux-Port 10 minutes, the full length of the Croisette 25-30 minutes on foot. The climb to Le Suquet is steep but short. An evening walk along the Croisette is one of Cannes' most classic experiences.

💡 Antibes and Juan-les-Pins — easy day trips

Antibes is 10 minutes from Cannes by train. Picasso Museum, old city walls and a Provençal market. Juan-les-Pins jazz festival every July. Nice is 30-40 minutes. All reachable by TER regional train, tickets €5-12. For day trip planning, set off from Cannes in the early morning and return in the afternoon.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Cannes: Best Areas

Location is critical in Cannes — the Croisette is expensive and touristy, Le Suquet has character, the area around the train station is practical.

Croisette — the seafront boulevard

Most prestigious

One of the world's most famous seafront promenades. Grand hotels, luxury boutiques, private beaches. Everything right in front of you but prices to match. The most expensive and crowded point in the city during the Film Festival. The right place for those who want to experience Cannes at full face value — if the budget allows.

📍 Film Festival atmosphere • Luxury hotels • Right on the seafront

Le Suquet — old neighbourhood

Most characterful

Narrow medieval streets, small restaurants and the city's finest views. 5 minutes on foot to Forville Market and Vieux-Port. Away from the Croisette's prices, quiet and authentic. The streets are steep and stepped but worth every step.

📍 Panoramic views • Close to Forville Market • Local atmosphere

Around the train station

Most practical

10 minutes to the Croisette, 10 minutes to Forville, 10 minutes to Antibes by train. The most central point for transport. Prices lower than the Croisette. Rue d'Antibes shopping street nearby.

📍 Train connections • Rue d'Antibes • Affordable options

Forville — market neighbourhood

Most local

A small neighbourhood at the foot of Le Suquet, around Forville Market. Comes to life with market activity in the mornings. The pedestrian Rue Meynadier nearby — local bakeries, delis, cheesemongers. The right place to experience Cannes like a local.

📍 Forville Market • Rue Meynadier • Morning market life

Californie — hilltop neighbourhood

Most peaceful

In the east of the city, on hillside slopes looking out to sea, filled with Belle Époque villa architecture. Reaching the Croisette requires a short walk or bus. A peaceful alternative for longer stays. For those who want to see the Riviera from above.

📍 Belle Époque villas • Peaceful atmosphere • Panoramic views
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Cannes

Cannes can be visited year-round but the Film Festival period is a category apart — come ready for it or plan around it.

Spring

Apr – May
The best time

April is Cannes' most balanced month: temperatures 18-22°C, no crowds, prices reasonable. May is split in two by the Film Festival: quiet and cheap before the festival, the city packed solid during festival week.

Summer

Jun – Aug
Crowded but lively

July and August are the French Riviera's most intense period. Cannes beaches are crowded, the Croisette overflows. That said, the sea is at its finest, evenings are long and lively. Early morning hours and after 7pm are more bearable.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Second best time

September and October are Cannes' most comfortable period. Summer crowds gone, temperatures still 22-25°C, swimming in the Mediterranean still possible. MIPCOM (October) and other professional trade fairs bring life to the city but nowhere near as crowded as the Film Festival. Restaurants and hotels more reasonably priced, the Lérins Islands nearly empty.

Winter

Dec – Feb
Quiet and sunny

From December to February Cannes quietens. Some restaurants close, beaches are empty but the weather is usually sunny and mild. In January-February mimosa (similar to yellow acacia) blooms in the hills around Grasse and Tanneron — the French Riviera's most breathtaking winter scenery.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Cannes

Cannes is an expensive city — but one street back from the Croisette you eat both better and cheaper.

🥞

Socca

At the Socca'nne stall in Forville Market, fresh socca cooked over a wood fire. Wrapped in paper, black pepper on top, €3. Eat it hot — when it cools that crispy texture disappears. You can sit at one of the cafés beside the market and ask to bring it in. On the day the market is closed (Monday is the antiques market) there's no socca either.

⭐ Forville Market, Socca'nne stall · €3 · eat it hot
🥪

Pan bagnat

The sandwich version of a Niçoise salad — round bread filled with tuna, hard-boiled eggs, tomatoes, peppers, Niçoise olives and anchovies. The lunch dish of the Provençal Riviera. Sold everywhere at kiosks and small shops. How to spot a real pan bagnat: soaked bread, raw vegetables and quality canned tuna. If there are cooked vegetables, it's the wrong version.

⭐ Kiosks and shops across the city · €5–8
🧅

Pissaladière

An onion tart — thick pastry base, caramelised onions cooked for hours, Niçoise olives and a lattice of anchovies. At first glance the appearance might suggest pizza, but it's different and far more intense. Fresh from bakeries on Rue Meynadier from the morning. Jean-Luc Pelé is the best on this street. A slice for €2-3, eat it while walking.

⭐ Rue Meynadier bakeries · Jean-Luc Pelé · €2–3 per slice
🐟

Bouillabaisse

A rich, saffron-scented broth made from rock fish, served with rouille (garlic mayonnaise) and croutons — a deeply flavourful Riviera soup. Good bouillabaisse in Cannes is expensive: €35-55 per person. Restaurants in Le Suquet neighbourhood are the most reliable. If the menu has no price or it seems very cheap, it's likely a ready-made packet.

⭐ Le Suquet neighbourhood · €35–55 per person · lunch or dinner
🍷

Rosé wine — Provence style

Provence produces some of the world's finest rosé. Every bistro and café in Cannes has Provence rosé. A glass of open rosé is €5-8, double on the Croisette. On a warm afternoon, sitting on a café terrace near Forville with this wine is one of the French Riviera's most pure moments.

⭐ Every bistro and café · €5–8 per glass · away from the Croisette
🛒

Breakfast at Forville Market

The covered market open from 7am to 1pm. Mondays are the antiques market, all other days food. Fresh fruit, vegetables, cheese, fish, flowers and local products. Picking up socca, fougasse (Provençal olive bread) and fresh cheese and sitting outside is both cheap and the most local Cannes breakfast you can have. Chefs and grandmothers are at their most animated between 8am and 10am.

⭐ Forville Market · 7am–1pm · Monday antiques
🍷 Rue Meynadier — Cannes' local street

A pedestrian street running from Forville Market to Rue d'Antibes. Bakeries, cheesemongers, delis, confectionery shops. The real Cannes shopkeepers are here, in contrast to the Croisette's luxury windows. Ideal for picking up a picnic — fresh bread, cheese and a bottle of rosé and you're ready for the beach. Most lively between 8am and 10am.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Cannes

Cannes' finest corners are one step away from the Croisette or a few metres higher up.

✨ Right above the Croisette — a viewpoint with no crowds

Le Suquet hilltop — Notre-Dame d'Espérance

Climb the steps from Vieux-Port and you reach the top of Le Suquet. From in front of Notre-Dame d'Espérance Church you can see the yacht harbour, the Croisette and the Estérel mountains. Go 30 minutes before sunset — the light hits this hill directly. Bring a bottle of rosé and sit on the terrace. A tenth of the Croisette's crowds, free and far more beautiful.

✨ The island where the Man in the Iron Mask was held — 15 minutes across

Île Sainte-Marguerite

15 minutes by ferry from Vieux-Port. At Fort Royal you can see the cell where the Man in the Iron Mask was held — whether fact or legend is debated, but the cell itself is real. The rest of the island is pine and eucalyptus forest, clear sea coves and walking trails. Even in midsummer it stays peaceful. Have lunch on the island — the restaurant is simple but the fish is fresh.

✨ A living monastery, vineyards and its own wine

Île Saint-Honorat

The smaller island next to Sainte-Marguerite, reached by a separate ferry. An active monastery since the 5th century — monks still live here today and tend their vines. The monastery wine is world-renowned; tastings and sales are available to visitors. The island is small, walkable in its entirety. Can be visited on the same day as Sainte-Marguerite — buy separate tickets for each island.

✨ Cannes' Picasso connection — just outside the city

Vallauris — Picasso's pottery village

20 minutes from Cannes by bus. Picasso lived here between 1948 and 1955 and discovered the art of ceramics here. Active pottery workshops and small galleries still exist today. Musée National Picasso La Guerre et la Paix — a chapel that Picasso personally painted and donated to the town. Ticketed but affordable, not crowded, genuinely impressive.

✨ Mimosa and the perfume capital — right behind Cannes

Grasse — perfume city

25-30 minutes from Cannes by train or bus. The world's perfume capital — the raw materials for Chanel No.5 come from here. Fragonard, Molinard and Galimard perfume factories offer free tours; workshop sessions are ticketed. The old town has narrow streets and Provençal breakfast spots. In February-March mimosa blooms along the roads.

✨ Cannes' quiet Monday ritual

Forville antiques market

Every Monday Forville Market transforms from food into an antiques, vintage and second-hand market. Old photographs, frames, jewellery, books, plates. Prices far lower than auction houses. The best finds when stalls are being set up at 7am. Sellers are generally open to bargaining. A completely different and far more authentic Cannes experience, in contrast to the Croisette's glamour.

✨ Chemin des Étoiles — handprints of cinema stars

In front of the Palais des Festivals, a walkway reminiscent of Hollywood's Walk of Fame but far more modest. The hand and footprints of stars who've attended the film festival are pressed into concrete blocks. Free, open 24/7. Lit up at night — ideal for stopping on an evening walk. Climbing the red-carpeted steps of the Palais des Festivals is also free and perfect for photos.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Cannes belongs to the stars for two weeks a year — and to smart travellers for the other fifty. Know these four and live it without paying red-carpet prices.

☕ TRAP

A 'view' coffee on the Croisette terraces

Espresso on the palm-lined boulevard sells at several times the price; you're paying not for coffee but for the right to watch Bentleys pass.

💡 What to Do Instead: Drink your coffee around the Forville market or on Rue Meynadier; live the Croisette for free, on foot.
🎬 TRAP

'Party ticket' sellers during the Festival

Most 'starry gala' tickets sold on the street and online in May are fake; real invitations are never for sale.

💡 What to Do Instead: Soak up the festival around the Palais and at the public beach screenings (Cinéma de la Plage — free).
🏖️ TRAP

The beach club sunbed bill

On the Croisette's private beaches, a sunbed and umbrella easily reach dinner-budget territory.

💡 What to Do Instead: Use the public beaches: Plage du Midi and the public stretches by the Palais offer the same sand for free.
🛥️ TRAP

Haggling for a 'celebrity villas boat tour'

The pricey 'celebrity tour' sold at the port mostly amounts to pointing at walls from a distance.

💡 What to Do Instead: Spend the same money on the Lérins Islands ferry: Sainte-Marguerite's forest and the Man in the Iron Mask's cell tell a real story.
🛡️ The golden rule

The Cannes equation is simple: the first street along the sea is the show, the second street is the city. If a price tag hurts, climb one street up.
