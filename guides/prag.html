Prague

A city so beautiful it almost doesn't seem real — and far deeper than it looks

Overview

The Vibe of Prague

Prague is one of the best-preserved medieval cities in Europe, which means it has spent thirty years learning to perform for the twelve million tourists who arrive each year. The Prague most visitors see — the overpriced beer traps on Old Town Square, the souvenir shops along Karlova, the organised pub crawls — is a city performing its own history. The real Prague runs underneath it: in the neighbourhood wine bars of Vinohrady, the riverside embankment at Náplavka on a Friday evening, the Baroque gardens that tourists walk past to reach the castle, the trams that still carry the city through every possible version of the weather. This is one of the most architecturally extraordinary cities on earth — Gothic, Renaissance, Baroque, Art Nouveau, and Communist Brutalism within walking distance of each other. The question is not whether to see the castle. The question is what happens when you stop following the crowd.

🗣️ Local Concept: Hospoda (HOS-poh-dah)

A traditional Czech pub — not a beer bar, not a gastropub, but a hospoda. Dark wood, long tables, regulars who have sat in the same seat for twenty years, draft Pilsner Urquell or Kozel served without asking. The hospoda is the social centre of Czech neighbourhood life. It is where news is exchanged, football is watched, and the country's complicated relationship with its own history is quietly negotiated. A good hospoda has no English menu and no tourist-facing décor. Finding one is the entry point to the real city.

Insider Advice

3 Unwritten Rules for Prague

1

Czech beer is refilled without asking

In a traditional hospoda, the waiter will bring another beer when your glass is nearly empty without being asked. This is correct behaviour. To stop, place a beer mat on top of your glass. Not understanding this system is the most reliable way of accidentally drinking six beers in an afternoon. The Czech Republic has the highest per-capita beer consumption in the world. This is not a coincidence.

2

The tram system is extraordinary

Prague has one of the finest tram networks in Europe — 26 routes covering the entire city including the historic centre, Malá Strana, and the castle approach. A single 90-minute ticket (CZK 40) covers all metro, tram and bus with unlimited transfers. Buy tickets at yellow vending machines in metro stations, at any newsagent (trafika), or via the PID Lítačka app. Stamp the ticket in the yellow machine when you board — unstamped tickets are treated as no ticket. The fine is CZK 1,500. The 24-hour pass (CZK 120) is the correct choice for most visitors.

3

The Czech crown — cards are not always welcome

Prague uses the Czech crown (CZK / Kč), not the euro. Many neighbourhood hospodas, market stalls, and smaller restaurants are still cash only. The exchange rate matters: airport exchange offices are the worst value in the city. Withdraw cash from a bank ATM in the city centre. Never use the private exchange offices with flashing signs — the rates are predatory and the fees are hidden.
