Giethoorn

The roadless village: canals, thatched roofs and whisper boats

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Giethoorn

In Giethoorn the main street is a canal, the pavement a footpath, the garage a boat shed: boats, not cars, pull up to the old village's houses. The thatched farmhouses have stood on their garden islands for centuries, and even the post was once delivered by punt. The coach crowds create canal traffic at noon, but in the early morning the village is nothing but duck calls and the lap of oars. Step into a whisper boat and lower your voice along with the throttle — this village is a monument to slowness.

🗣️ Local Concept: Fluisterboot (flow-ster-boat)

'Whisper boat': the nearly silent electric rental boats. The official vehicle of Giethoorn's canals — the name is also the rule: whisper, glide, don't hurry.

3 Unwritten Rules for Giethoorn

1

Drive the boat slowly, and by the rules

The canals are narrow and the midday traffic real: keep right, slow under the bridges, give moored boats and duck families a wide berth. If it's your first time at a tiller, take the mini-briefing seriously — canal courtesy is the village's constitution.

2

This is a living village, not a museum

People live in most of the thatched houses: don't enter the gardens, don't press cameras to windows, don't picnic on the lawns. Look from the path, take your photo, leave the silence behind you.

3

Stay the night and see the real village

The day-trip crowds withdraw by 4.30 pm and Giethoorn returns to itself: empty canals in the evening light, breakfast alone with the ducks in the morning mist. One night's stay is this village's true ticket.

Getting Around

Giethoorn Public Transit & Airport Transfers

You reach Giethoorn by train + bus; inside the village three vehicles are valid: boat, bicycle and feet.

Train to Steenwijk (~1h 40m with a change), then bus 70 reaches the village in 15 minutes: board with an OV-chipkaart or contactless card and get off at 'Dominee Hylkemaweg'. If driving, leave the car at the entrance car parks.

The electric boats rent by the hour and need no licence: a short briefing + a map + a smile suffice. Rent at the first hour — captaincy on empty canals is a joy; in the midday traffic it's a sport of patience.

Paddle craft are the canals' freest form: you enter the narrow side channels and the lake shore before any motor does. The reed corridors of the Weerribben-Wieden park by canoe are another planet.

The canalside paths and the Bovenwijde lake loop are flat and delightful: pedal to the neighbouring village of Dwarsgracht and see a second, crowd-free Giethoorn.

The main path (Binnenpad) walks the village end to end in 45 minutes: pass under the 176 bridges and look down from them. The path is narrow — give way to cyclists, keep off the gardens.

⚡ Pro Tip

Rent your boat not in the village centre but at the northern or southern piers: shorter queue, calmer entry. Run the route in reverse — while everyone floats toward the village, you head for the lake and tour the village on the way back.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Giethoorn

Everyone sees the main canal; these six are the hidden face of village and marsh.

Dwarsgracht and Jonen

Two mini villages along the neighbouring canal: the same thatched roofs, the same bridges, zero crowds. Reached by bike or boat — whoever wants to see Giethoorn as it was fifty years ago comes here.

Weerribben-Wieden National Park

Europe's largest lowland bog begins just beyond the village: reed corridors, water lilies and heron colonies. Enter by canoe — the park begins where the engine noise ends.

Museum 't Olde Maat Uus

Giethoorn's life of a century ago in an old farmhouse: peat cutting, punter building and canal-village daily life. The warmest way to understand why the village is the way it is.

Bovenwijde's eastern shore

The east-facing jetties of the lakeshore turn gold in the morning mist: take your coffee, sit on the planks, salute the duck convoy. The day's best half hour passes here before the village wakes.

The punter yard

The boatyard where the traditional flat-bottomed punts are still built by hand: the smell of wood shavings and a centuries-old technique. If the door is open, say hello — the builders love to talk shop.

A classic punter tour

The traditional boat pushed by pole instead of motor: the guide tells village stories while you glide at 19th-century speed. Quieter even than the whisper boat — the village's noblest transport.
