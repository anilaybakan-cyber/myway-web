Gaziantep

The gastronomy capital of pistachios, copper and mosaics

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Gaziantep

You arrive in Gaziantep hungry and leave with a full heart: this is UNESCO's city of gastronomy, the homeland of baklava, the academy of kebab and the capital of the pistachio. But Antep is not only a table — the world-famous mosaics of Zeugma, the coppersmiths' hammers ringing around the castle, six-hundred-year-old caravanserais and Turkey's oldest coffeehouse all live in these streets. At this stop on the Silk Road, the old etiquette of trade survives: the shopkeeper offers tea, the master shows his work, and the table is always shared.

🗣️ Local Concept: Fıstık (fuhs-TUHK)

The Antep pistachio: this city's gold, pride and unit of measure. It gives baklava its green, ice cream its crown, and conversation its snack. Locals grade it by 'boz iç' (the prized young kernel) and treat the harvest as a festival — leaving without pistachios is like leaving the sea without touching water.

3 Unwritten Rules for Gaziantep

1

Start the day with beyran

The official opening of an Antep morning is beyran: in halls that open at 06:00, garlicky, spicy lamb slow-cooked in bone broth awaits you. Don't resist the idea of 'soup for breakfast' — drink it once and you'll understand why Antep mornings start early.

2

Eat baklava in the morning, from a master

True Antep baklava is eaten fresh off the morning tray: the pistachio green, the pastry crackling, the butter fresh. Believe the master who says 'cold baklava is leftover baklava' — the 08:00 tray and the afternoon tray are not the same dessert.

3

Don't rush the bazaar, never refuse the tea

The coppersmiths' bazaar and the caravanserai district are not a shopping mall but a living school of craft: talk to the master, listen to the hammer's rhythm. A shopkeeper's offer of tea is not a sales tactic, it's Antep etiquette — accept it, chat, and then buy nothing if you like.

Getting Around

Gaziantep Public Transit & Airport Transfers

The airport is close to the center and the historic core is walkable; Zeugma and Rumkale call for wheels.

✈️

Arriving from the airport

Gaziantep Airport is twenty minutes from the center: shuttle services and taxis run steadily. Frequent flights arrive from Istanbul, Ankara and Izmir — the ideal distance for a weekend gastronomy escape.

🚶

A walkable historic core

The castle, the coppersmiths' bazaar, the caravanserais and the Bey quarter are all within walking distance: historic Antep can be paced in half a day. Comfortable shoes for the stone pavements — bazaar breaks stretch the walk on their own.

🚋

Tram and buses

The tram line runs from the university to the center, and buses weave the city: a Gaziantepkart covers both. Short taxi hops between center and museums are effortless too — distances in this city are small.

🚗

Wheels for Zeugma and beyond

The Zeugma Mosaic Museum is ten minutes from the center: easily reached by taxi or bus. For the Rumkale and Halfeti escape you'll need a rental car or a day tour — the road rewards you with Euphrates views.

🧭

Walking the bazaar labyrinth

From Zincirli Bedesten to the Bone Bedesten, from Sire Han to Kurkcu Han, the bazaars interlock: getting lost is part of the program. Follow the sound of hammers — the coppersmiths' quarter is the bazaar's heart.

💡 Museum tip

Go to Zeugma at morning opening: you'll stand eye to eye with the Gypsy Girl without the crowds. Give the museum at least two hours — mosaics don't like haste, and the lit halls are at their emptiest in the morning.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Gaziantep: Best Areas

The choice in Antep is twofold: a stone-mansion boutique in the historic core, or a comfortable hotel in the new city — ten minutes apart.

Bey quarter (Bey Mahallesi)

Most character

The neighborhood of stone mansions: carved doors, courtyard boutique hotels, museum-houses. The address for waking up inside historic Antep — walking distance to the bazaar and the castle.

📍 Stone mansions • Courtyard hotels • Museum-houses

Castle and bazaar district

Closest to the table

The beyran halls, baklava houses and caravanserai district at your doorstep: you can stroll to the 06:00 beyran in your slippers. The most practical base for a gastronomy-first visit.

📍 Beyran distance • Beside the bazaar • Early-morning advantage

Ibrahimli and the new center

Comfort side

Modern hotels with big rooms and parking: a strong price-to-comfort balance. Ten minutes by taxi to the historic core — the new city's evening cafes are here too.

📍 Modern comfort • Easy parking • Strong breakfasts

Around the station

Transit hub

A practical district near the bus terminal and main arteries: sensible for short stays and early departures. The center is a few tram stops away.

📍 Near transit • Economical choice • Short-stay friendly

Kavaklik

Green and calm

A quiet district known for its parks: the local choice for family visits and noiseless nights. A short taxi to the center — an evening walk in Kavaklik park is a pleasure.

📍 Beside the park • Quiet nights • Local rhythm
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Gaziantep

The Antep table is open in all four seasons; the pistachio harvest calls for autumn, spice scents for spring, and hot summer middays for courtyard shade.

Spring

Apr – Jun
Spiced Spring

The sweetest season for bazaar wandering: mild air, cool courtyards, tables brightened with spring onions and purslane. In May the Antep kitchen shifts to its spring menu with new-harvest vegetables — the walk-and-eat balance is easiest to strike in these months.

Summer

Jul – Aug
Courtyard Shade Season

Antep summer is hot: midday belongs to museums and han courtyards, morning and evening to the bazaar. Watermelon with cheese, kunefe with ice cream — the summer table adapts too. Air-conditioned Zeugma is the most elegant escape from the noon heat.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Pistachio Harvest

September is the month of the pistachio harvest: the stalls fill with the new crop, and peppers and eggplants redden on the rooftop drying lines. The weather softens, the bazaar quickens — this is Antep at its most Antep.

Winter

Dec – Mar
Beyran Season

On a cold Antep morning, the steam of a beyran hall is the most inviting sight in town: winter is the season of this kitchen's heavy artillery. Yuvalama, kebab off the embers, hot katmer — tourists thin out, the table returns to the locals, and conversation stretches long.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Gaziantep

This is Turkey's gastronomy capital: you need not a list but a curriculum — start with beyran, finish with katmer, and entrust the middle to kebab and baklava.

🍲

Beyran

The morning's boiling overture: bone broth, shredded lamb, garlic and heat. Drunk in the hall to the sound of spoons, with pickles and bread alongside — the first spoonful is a lesson, the second a habit.

⭐ 06:00 ritual · ask for it full strength
🥮

Antep baklava

Forty layers of pastry, boz iç pistachios, fresh butter: in baklava's homeland, the morning tray is a league of its own. Ask the master about the 'dry' and 'moist' styles — pastry that folds like a towel is the good sign itself.

⭐ Off the morning tray · pistachio green
🍢

The kebab curriculum

From Ali Nazik to kusleme, simit kebab to onion kebab: Antep kebab is not a list but a school. If your visit lands in season, don't miss the loquat and garlic kebabs — the embers keep a seasonal calendar.

⭐ A menu like a school · ask for the season's kebab
🥟

Yuvalama and icli kofte

Yuvalama, the festival soup of tiny chickpea-yogurt dumplings, and icli kofte, elegant bulgur shells with spiced filling: Antep home cooking speaks through these two plates. Look for a home-style lokanta.

⭐ Home-kitchen classic · the festival plate
🥞

Katmer

Clotted cream, pistachio and sugar in paper-thin pastry: the morning's sweet contender, baklava's young rival. Eaten hot at the counter — asking for 'double pistachio' is a declaration of love in Antep.

⭐ Hot off the counter · cream and double pistachio
☕

Menengic coffee and the nut stall

Menengic is the wild terebinth pistachio, ember-roasted and brewed with milk: its scent is the bazaar's memory. Fresh boz iç from the nut seller alongside — this is Antep's hospitality duo.

⭐ Milky and ember-roasted · bazaar break
🍷 Table rule

In Antep, quietly walk away from anywhere with a 'tourist menu': a good hall's menu is short and its plates are deep. Ask the master 'what should I eat today?' — in Antep that question is a mark of respect, and the answer never misleads.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Gaziantep

Everyone sees Zeugma and the castle; these six are Antep's hidden face, ringing with hammers and scented with coffee.

✨ Turkey's oldest coffeehouse

Tahmis Kahvesi

A wooden-columned hall where coffee has brewed since the 1630s: menengic on the embers, conversation at full volume, history at every table. A cup at this ancient counter is a seat in the city's memory.

✨ Stone mansion quarter

Bey Mahallesi

Carved doors, courtyard mansions, museum-houses: the stone streets of old Antep's merchant class. Break in a mansion courtyard cafe — the walls tell stories in the Antep dialect.

✨ Workshops of the hammer

The coppersmith masters

The tinner's bellows, the hammer's rhythm, blackened aprons: copper is still beaten by hand here. Buy at least a cezve from a master — one who shows you his work won't withhold his tea either.

✨ Heart of the covered bazaar

Zincirli Bedesten

A covered bazaar from the 1700s: spice sellers, kutnu fabric merchants and dried-vegetable stalls under stone arches. In the morning hours, the shopkeepers' conversation is worth more than the shopping.

✨ Euphrates escape

Rumkale and Halfeti

An hour away, a boat on the green water of the Euphrates: half-sunken Halfeti and the fortress of Rumkale on its rocky headland. A submerged minaret, black rose legends and river coolness — Antep's water side.

✨ The kitchen's museum

Emine Gogus Culinary Museum

An encyclopedia of the Antep kitchen in a stone mansion: copper cookware, century-old recipes, the etiquette of the tray table. Once you know the dishes' stories, the table tastes different — come before your bazaar tour and eat knowing what you eat.

✨ Bazaar strategy

Walk the caravanserai district between 09:00 and 11:00: shutters freshly opened, masters at work, tea stoves boiling. Move to a museum in the midday heat — the late-afternoon return to the bazaar is shopping's golden hour.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Gastronomic fame invites imitation; know these four games and you'll eat the real Antep.

🥮 TRAP

The tourist baklava box

In some windows the 'Antep baklava' label is stuck on stale stock or mass production short on pistachio; the gift-box price outruns the real thing.

💡 What to Do Instead: Buy from the morning tray, by weight, after tasting: a good baklava house never hesitates to offer a taste. Look for green pistachio and the scent of butter — the tray's freshness speaks, not the box's packaging.
🍢 TRAP

The long-menu 'kebab palaces'

A hundred-item tourist menu signals the freezer, not the embers; the waiter's 'special' is usually the most expensive line.

💡 What to Do Instead: Choose masters with short menus and halls full of locals: order by saying 'whatever the master recommends'. A customer who asks about the season's kebab is taken seriously in the kitchen.
🏺 TRAP

Machine-pressed 'hand-beaten' copper

At some stalls, press-made goods sell at hand-beaten prices; a bright polish often hides mass production.

💡 What to Do Instead: Look for hammer marks on the inner surface and buy from the master's own workshop: beaten copper's marks are irregular and its ring is deep. A master who shows you the making quotes an honest price too.
🕐 TRAP

The noon baklava-and-beyran plan

Beyran at noon and baklava in the afternoon means tasting both in the second league — beyran belongs to the morning cauldron, baklava to the morning tray.

💡 What to Do Instead: Set your stomach to Antep time: beyran at 07:00, baklava at 09:00, kebab at noon, katmer in the afternoon. The early riser wins twice in this city — both the freshness and the queue belong to the morning person.
🛡️ Golden rule

In Antep, wherever the crowd is local is an unfailing compass: look at the hall, not the sign. If shopkeepers are eating somewhere, you can sit down — in this city, the tradesman's stomach is the strictest critic.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Gaziantep

The Antep manner is generous and heart-winning; these five phrases open doors at the table and in the bazaar.

🗣️ Hoş geldin
'Welcome' — the first phrase you'll hear at the hall door and in the han courtyard
🗣️ Buyur gardaş
The Antep-style invitation — 'gardaş' is the warm word for brother; smile when you hear it
🗣️ Afiyet olsun
'Enjoy your meal' — opens and closes every table from beyran to katmer
🗣️ Ellerine sağlık
'Health to your hands' — the most precious thank-you to a master, said to kebab cooks and coppersmiths alike
🗣️ Fıstığı çift olsun
'Make the pistachio double' — the password of the katmer counter; Antep's sweet measure of generosity
