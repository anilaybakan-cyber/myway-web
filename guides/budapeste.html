Budapest

Two banks of the Danube, thermal steam and the city of ruin bars

Overview

The Vibe of Budapest

Budapest is really two cities, and the Danube makes both jealous: the Buda side is stately with its hills, castle and quiet lanes; the Pest side wild with its boulevards, coffeehouses and ruin bars. Beneath it all bubble more than a hundred thermal springs — this city has healed with water and socialised in steam for centuries. When evening comes, the Parliament burns like a golden jewel and the Chain Bridge ties the two banks together with a necklace of light. Budapest is a city of domes by day and of water by night.

🗣️ Local Concept: Romkocsma (rom-kotch-ma)

'Ruin bar': the Budapest invention of bars built into the courtyards of abandoned buildings, full of old armchairs and odd objects. The first opened in the early 2000s — today it is the city's night-time identity itself.

Insider Advice

3 Unwritten Rules for Budapest

1

Enter the baths properly

The thermal pools are not swimming pools: in 38-degree water you don't race, you converse — some even play chess. Read the slipper and cap rules at the entrance and start the hot-cold-sauna cycle slowly. Stay at least two hours; hurry is the enemy of steam.

2

Never hail a taxi off the street

In Budapest a trustworthy taxi comes when called: by app or official dispatcher. A car flagged on the street may run on the tourist tariff. Public transport is excellent anyway — metro line 1 is a historic monument in itself.

3

Put the night view on the schedule

Budapest's UNESCO panorama opens at night: Parliament, castle and bridges turn to gold in the dark. Devote one evening to the water — a boat, a riverside walk or Gellért Hill; whichever it is, wait for darkness.
