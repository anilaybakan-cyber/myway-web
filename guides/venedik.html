Venice

The impossible city built on water: get lost, drink an ombra, get lost again

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Venice

Everything you've heard about Venice is true: yes it's crowded, yes it's expensive, yes the water sometimes reaches your shoes. And yes — there is nothing else like it on earth. In this impossible city built on a hundred and eighteen islands there are no cars, hurry doesn't work, and maps tell lies. The art is not seeing San Marco but walking two bridges past it and losing yourself on a silent canal: there the washing sways, the gondola repairer works, and Venice still lives.

🗣️ Local Concept: Ombra (OM-brah)

'Shade': in Venetian it means a glass of wine — the wine sellers once moved their stalls with the bell tower's shadow. If you can say 'un'ombra e due cicchetti', you're no tourist in this city; you're a regular.

3 Unwritten Rules for Venice

1

Count getting lost as the plan

In Venice the GPS falters, lanes dead-end, bridges throw you onto other islands. Don't resist: the city's best moments happen on the wrong streets. Know only the last vaporetto's time and give the rest to the water.

2

Do the vaporetto maths up front

A single ride is dear; the 24/48/72-hour passes pay for themselves within a few trips. Think of your stay and buy the pass on day one — Murano, Burano and Giudecca ride on the same card.

3

Check the entry fee and the acqua alta

On peak days day-trippers may owe an access fee (contributo d'accesso); overnight guests are exempt but may need to register. In autumn and winter, download the acqua alta app — and till the tide drops, shelter in a bacaro.

Getting Around

Venice Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Venice has two means of transport: your feet and the boats. Neither likes to hurry.

The train reaches Santa Lucia station right on the Grand Canal — the world's best station exit. From the airport, the Alilaguna boat or a bus to Piazzale Roma; beyond that point, no wheels pass.

Line 1 crawls the Grand Canal stop by stop (a tour in itself); line 2 is the express. Ride on a pass, and open up the islands with the same card.

The city walks end to end in an hour; bridge steps are a suitcase's enemy. The yellow 'Per San Marco / Per Rialto' signs are a rough compass — the rest is happy deviation.

The shared gondola crossing the Grand Canal where bridges are missing: a two-minute true gondola experience for a few euros. The locals cross standing; you may choose to hold on.

Sleek, fast and expensive: comfort from airport to hotel carries a high price. Reasonable split among a group; otherwise the vaporetto does every job.

⚡ Pro Tip

Pick a hotel near the station or a vaporetto stop, or travel light: in Venice every bridge is a staircase with your suitcase's name on it.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Venice

'Hidden' is a relative idea in Venice; these six wait two bridges beyond the crowd.

Libreria Acqua Alta

The absurd-beautiful bookshop where books pile into gondolas, bathtubs and barrels: in the back courtyard a staircase of old books looks over the canal. The cats are regulars too.

The campanile of San Giorgio Maggiore

The tower of Palladio's church on the island facing San Marco: ride the lift and see the piazza head-on. The clever alternative to the Campanile queue — with a better view.

Torcello

The island older than Venice: golden mosaics in a thousand-year cathedral, 'Attila's throne' in the grass, and a deep hush. Ten minutes and a thousand years from Burano's colours.

Squero di San Trovaso

The still-working gondola yard in Dorsoduro: wooden skeletons, the smell of pitch, an alpine-hut workshop. Watched best from the far bank, ombra in hand.

Scala Contarini del Bovolo

The Renaissance tower with a spiral staircase tucked into a narrow courtyard: 'bovolo' is Venetian for snail. From its top, a sea of rooftops — without the crowds.

Sant'Erasmo

Venice's vegetable-garden island: its purple artichoke (castraure) is legend, bicycles rule, and the beach belongs to locals. The antidote to tourist Venice.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Venice

Buongiorno
(bwon-JOR-noh)
Good morning / good day — mandatory entering a shop
Grazie
(GRAH-tsyeh)
Thank you — short, clear, always
Per favore
(pehr fah-VOH-reh)
Please — add it to the end of your order
Un'ombra, per favore
(oon OM-brah pehr fah-VOH-reh)
'A glass of wine, please' — the magic sentence of the bacaro counter
Andemo!
(an-DEH-mo)
Venetian for 'let's go!' — andiamo in lagoon form; hear it and you're one of the house
