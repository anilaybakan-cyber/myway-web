Mykonos

One of the world's most expensive islands — but this is also where you find the spirit of the Aegean islands in its purest form

Overview

The Vibe of Mykonos

Mykonos is like two different islands. The first: some of the world's most expensive beach clubs, mega yachts, nightlife and Paradise Beach packed to bursting in July and August. The second: whitewashed walls, narrow labyrinthine streets, windmills and blue-domed Chora, hidden at the heart of the island. This second Mykonos shows itself in the early morning hours or out of season. The island is small — everything is reachable by vehicle or bus. But before coming to Mykonos, one thing needs to be accepted: everything here is expensive and in midsummer it is very crowded. If you come at peace with that reality, Mykonos won't disappoint.

💬 Chora
🗣️ /HO-ra/

Greek for 'town' or 'city' — the main town of every island in the Aegean is called Chora. In Mykonos, Chora is the white labyrinthine heart of the island. Narrow streets, whitewashed walls, blue doors, climbing plants. Deliberately designed like a maze — in the old days to confuse pirates. Today getting lost is just as easy and just as beautiful.

Insider Advice

Unwritten Rules for Mykonos

#1
If you want to come in July-August, prepare your budget and your patience In midsummer Mykonos fills with crowds from all over the world. Hotel prices are very high, restaurant reservations are essential, finding a spot on the beaches is difficult. June or September offers the same sunshine and sea, prices noticeably lower and crowds at a tolerable level.
#2
Explore Chora in the early morning hours During the day Chora's narrow streets get clogged with tourist crowds. But between 7am and 9am the streets are nearly empty — apart from cats and pigeons. At this hour it's possible to take photos, have a quiet coffee and get lost. Beautiful in the evenings too, but the early morning is something else entirely.
#3
Hire a vehicle — ideal for discovering the island in a day Mykonos is small but KTEL buses don't go everywhere. Hiring an ATV is the most practical way to reach the island's northern coves, Ano Mera village and hidden beaches. Small car or motorbike are also options. In midsummer, hire in the early morning — by afternoon the good vehicles are gone.
Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Mykonos

💎 HIDDEN GEM
Agios Sostis beach
A small cove on the northern part of the island, reached by vehicle or a long walk. No beach club, no sunbeds, no bar — just sand, rock and clean water. Kiki's Taverna alongside, no reservations and with a queue but both delicious and affordable. Even in midsummer, far from the crowds.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Delos Island
30 minutes by boat from Mykonos Old Port. The sacred island of ancient Greece — where Apollo and Artemis are said to have been born. The Terrace of the Lions, mosaic-floored houses, temple ruins. UNESCO World Heritage Site. Take the first morning sailing, bring a hat and water. Closed for visits in winter.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Paraportiani Church
At the northern end of Chora, on a slope facing the sea. A cumulative white structure made up of five separate chapels. Considered one of the world's most photographed churches. Nearly empty between 7am and 8am. In the afternoon, crowds with cameras at every corner. The church interior is small and plain but the atmosphere is very powerful.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Fokos beach and Fokos Taverna
In the north-east of the island, at the end of a steep road reached by vehicle. Completely natural, no facilities. The adjacent Fokos Taverna is known as Mykonos's finest fish restaurant — local produce, fresh fish, no reservations. Quiet even in midsummer. ATV or car essential. Plan a day trip that includes lunch here.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Cine Manto
In the centre of Chora, inside a garden. A tradition much loved in Greece: open-air cinema on summer nights, continuing since the 1960s. Old Hollywood classics and modern films under the pine trees. Watching a film at night with tsipouro (local distilled spirit) and meze is another experience altogether. Ticket €10-12, reservation recommended.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Ano Mera village
In the centre of the island, the island's only real inland village. The square around Panagia Tourliani Monastery, local cafés and tavernas. Very different from Mykonos Chora's white labyrinth — simpler, more local, quieter. A small market every day of the week. 15-20 minutes from Chora by car.
Food & Flavors

What to Taste

Mykonos is expensive — but if you go to the wrong place it's even more so. Taverna selection is critical — flee the harbourfront, step into the side streets.

🍴 MUST TASTE
Octopus — sun-dried
Mykonos's most iconic sight: sun-dried octopus hanging on a taverna balcony. Grilled and served with lemon and olive oil. Seeing octopus hanging on the balcony is a good sign for freshness. Portion at coastal tavernas €12-20.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Tiropita and spanakopita
Greek cheese pie (tiropita) and spinach pie (spanakopita) — thin filo, fresh cheese or spinach filling, baked in butter. Perfect for breakfast. From bakeries or small pastry shops. €2-3. The bakeries in Chora's back streets are the best — easy to find while walking around.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Souvlaki and gyros
Greece's fast food but quality versions exist in Mykonos. Souvlaki: grilled lamb or chicken on a skewer, pita, tomato, onion and tzatziki. Gyros: döner-style, thinly sliced meat. Small shops outside Chora's centre charge €5-8. Places on the harbourfront charge double.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Loukoumades
Hot dough balls, Greek honey and cinnamon. A few specialist loukoumades shops in Chora. Plain, chocolate or with ice cream versions. €4-6 a portion. Can be eaten sitting or walking. Morning or evening snack.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Fresh fish — at Fokos Taverna
The fish restaurants on Chora's harbourfront are expensive and inconsistent. For genuinely fresh fish go to Fokos Taverna — on the north coast, next to Fokos beach, not easy to reach but the fish is outstanding. Alternative: local tavernas in Ano Mera are both more affordable and fresher.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Meze platter and Greek wine
Greek meze sharing culture: tzatziki, tarama, fava (yellow pea purée), stuffed vine leaves, olives, white cheese. A leisurely meal with wine that stretches on. Santorini Assyrtiko is the best match. Glass €8-12, bottle €25-45.
Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

❌
Front-row sunbeds at the celebrated southern clubs rent for hundreds of euros; two coves over, the sea is the same blue.
✅ Better Alternative: Turn north: at Agios Sostis and Fokos there are no clubs and no speakers — lay your towel and take the sea for free.
❌
Most places waving menus in the narrow lanes sell frozen moussaka at view prices.
✅ Better Alternative: Sit where the locals sit: Ano Mera's square and the backstreet tavernas serve the same dish at half the price with twice the care.
❌
The island has a handful of taxis; waiting for one after the clubs takes hours and the haggling ends badly.
✅ Better Alternative: Use the SeaBus between beaches, the bus network is surprisingly good; if you rent wheels, decide who's driving at night before you start.
❌
Some bars charge extra for the window seat; but the sunset belongs to no one.
✅ Better Alternative: Sit on the wall by the windmills: same sunset, zero fee. If you're drinking anyway, one cocktail's price includes the view.
