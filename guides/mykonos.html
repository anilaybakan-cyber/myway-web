Mykonos

One of the world's most expensive islands — but this is also where you find the spirit of the Aegean islands in its purest form

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Mykonos

Mykonos is like two different islands. The first: some of the world's most expensive beach clubs, mega yachts, nightlife and Paradise Beach packed to bursting in July and August. The second: whitewashed walls, narrow labyrinthine streets, windmills and blue-domed Chora, hidden at the heart of the island. This second Mykonos shows itself in the early morning hours or out of season. The island is small — everything is reachable by vehicle or bus. But before coming to Mykonos, one thing needs to be accepted: everything here is expensive and in midsummer it is very crowded. If you come at peace with that reality, Mykonos won't disappoint.

🗣️ Local Concept: Chora (HO-ra)

Greek for 'town' or 'city' — the main town of every island in the Aegean is called Chora. In Mykonos, Chora is the white labyrinthine heart of the island. Narrow streets, whitewashed walls, blue doors, climbing plants. Deliberately designed like a maze — in the old days to confuse pirates. Today getting lost is just as easy and just as beautiful.

3 Unwritten Rules for Mykonos

1

If you want to come in July-August, prepare your budget and your patience

In midsummer Mykonos fills with crowds from all over the world. Hotel prices are very high, restaurant reservations are essential, finding a spot on the beaches is difficult. June or September offers the same sunshine and sea, prices noticeably lower and crowds at a tolerable level.

2

Explore Chora in the early morning hours

During the day Chora's narrow streets get clogged with tourist crowds. But between 7am and 9am the streets are nearly empty — apart from cats and pigeons. At this hour it's possible to take photos, have a quiet coffee and get lost. Beautiful in the evenings too, but the early morning is something else entirely.

3

Hire a vehicle — ideal for discovering the island in a day

Mykonos is small but KTEL buses don't go everywhere. Hiring an ATV is the most practical way to reach the island's northern coves, Ano Mera village and hidden beaches. Small car or motorbike are also options. In midsummer, hire in the early morning — by afternoon the good vehicles are gone.

Getting Around

Mykonos Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Mykonos is a small island. KTEL buses cover the main spots; an ATV or vehicle is essential for the rest.

✈️

From Athens to Mykonos

Flight from Athens approximately 45 minutes, ticket €40-120 depending on season and how far in advance. Ferry: fast ferry from Piraeus (Seajet, Golden Star) 2-2.5 hours, €50-80. From Rafina port 1.5-2 hours, slightly cheaper. Rafina is close to Athens Airport — KTEL bus 30-40 minutes, €4. Buy tickets in advance in summer — sells out.

🚌

KTEL buses — island transport

Mykonos has two bus terminals: Fabrika (south) and Old Port (north) — no connection between them but 15 minutes on foot through Chora. From Fabrika: Paradise Beach (~15 min) and the airport (~10 min). From Old Port: the new port and Agios Stefanos. Ticket €1-3, cash from the driver.

🏍️

ATV and vehicle hire

The most free-spirited way to explore the island. ATV daily €25-50, small car €40-70, motorbike €20-40. Essential for reaching the northern coves, Ano Mera village, Fokos and Agios Sostis beaches. Driving licence required. Hire in the early morning — by afternoon the best vehicles are gone. Return with a full tank.

⛵

Boat — beach and island excursions

Daily boat trips to Delos Island depart from Old Port pier — return approximately €20-25. Combined ticket with Rhenia €25-30. Private boat and catamaran hire also available. Water taxi between beaches: Platis Gialos to Paradise Beach by water taxi 10-15 minutes, €10-15.

🚶

Walking — sufficient within Chora

Chora's labyrinth is walkable — small and compact. From the windmills to Little Venice 10 minutes, Paraportiani 5 minutes. Getting out of Chora requires KTEL or a vehicle. Coastal walking path and Chora-Ano Mera trail for the more adventurous.

💡 Delos Island — 30 minutes from Mykonos, thousands of years back

Daily boat trips from Mykonos Old Port. Centre of the ancient Greek pantheon — Temple of Apollo, lion statues, mosaic-floored houses. UNESCO World Heritage Site. Archaeological museum on the island. Return boat ticket plus archaeological site entry combined total €30-35. Take the first morning sailing. Very hot in summer — hat and water essential.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Mykonos: Best Areas

Where you stay in Mykonos completely changes the experience — Chora is unmatched but expensive; the south coast is close to beaches but crowded.

Chora — the heart of the island

Most iconic

Mykonos's white labyrinthine town. Everything within walking distance. Windmills, Little Venice, Paraportiani Church and nightlife. Quiet in the morning hours, crowded in the afternoon. Hotel prices are high but the essence of the Mykonos experience is here.

📍 Windmills • Little Venice • Nightlife hub

Platis Gialos — south coast

Most practical

4 kilometres from Chora, a quiet beach village on the south coast. Connected to the Fabrika bus terminal. Water taxi to Paradise Beach and other beaches. Slightly more affordable than Chora. Suitable for families.

📍 South beach coast • Water taxi connections • Close to Chora

Ornos — quiet and local

Most peaceful

A quiet small bay on the south coast, next to Platis Gialos. Away from the Paradise Beach crowds. Small waterfront restaurants and cafés. 10 minutes from Chora by car.

📍 Quiet bay • Small waterfront restaurants • Away from crowds

Agios Ioannis — sunset village

Most romantic

Small village on the south-west coast. Known as the filming location of Shirley Valentine. Stunning sunset views facing Delos Island. Small beach and a few restaurants. 15 minutes from Chora by car. Quiet and authentic.

📍 Sunset facing Delos • Shirley Valentine location • Quiet and small

Around Ano Mera

Most local

In the centre of the island, the only real village. Panagia Tourliani Monastery, local tavernas and authentic Mykonos life. Far from the beach but close to the northern coves. Half the price of Chora. Car essential.

📍 Panagia Tourliani Monastery • Local tavernas • Close to northern coves
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Mykonos

Mykonos is a summer island — but the difference in price and crowds between midsummer and shoulder seasons is enormous.

Spring

Apr – Jun
Quietest and most affordable

April and May are quiet in Mykonos. Most venues aren't yet at full capacity but the weather is lovely and there are no crowds. June is the ideal balance point: temperatures 25-28°C, sea swimmable, venues open but far from the July-August crowds. Prices can drop to half those of midsummer.

Summer

Jun – Aug
Most lively but most expensive

July and August see Mykonos at its most lively but also its most crowded and most expensive. Paradise Beach parties, Little Venice evenings, Cavo Paradiso nightclub — all at their peak during this period. Everything requires booking far in advance. The only window to escape the crowds is in the very early morning hours.

Autumn

Sep – Oct
Second best time

September and October are Mykonos's most balanced period. Still 22-26°C, the sea is swimmable. Prices have noticeably dropped. Some nightclubs have closed but the island is still running. Chora in the mornings is genuinely empty and beautiful. The meltemi dies down.

Winter

Nov – Mar
Local Mykonos

From November to March Mykonos belongs largely to its local residents. Most restaurants, bars and boutiques are closed. Weather 12-16°C and windy. But what remains open is genuinely local and cheap. Chora in the morning hours is quiet and photogenic. For those who choose this period consciously, it means seeing the island's real face.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Mykonos

Mykonos is expensive — but if you go to the wrong place it's even more so. Taverna selection is critical — flee the harbourfront, step into the side streets.

🐙

Octopus — sun-dried

Mykonos's most iconic sight: sun-dried octopus hanging on a taverna balcony. Grilled and served with lemon and olive oil. Seeing octopus hanging on the balcony is a good sign for freshness. Portion at coastal tavernas €12-20.

⭐ Coastal tavernas · portion €12–20 · look for the one hanging on the balcony
🥐

Tiropita and spanakopita

Greek cheese pie (tiropita) and spinach pie (spanakopita) — thin filo, fresh cheese or spinach filling, baked in butter. Perfect for breakfast. From bakeries or small pastry shops. €2-3. The bakeries in Chora's back streets are the best — easy to find while walking around.

⭐ Chora back street bakeries · €2–3 · fresh in the morning
🍖

Souvlaki and gyros

Greece's fast food but quality versions exist in Mykonos. Souvlaki: grilled lamb or chicken on a skewer, pita, tomato, onion and tzatziki. Gyros: döner-style, thinly sliced meat. Small shops outside Chora's centre charge €5-8. Places on the harbourfront charge double.

⭐ Chora back streets · €5–8 · avoid the harbourfront
🍯

Loukoumades

Hot dough balls, Greek honey and cinnamon. A few specialist loukoumades shops in Chora. Plain, chocolate or with ice cream versions. €4-6 a portion. Can be eaten sitting or walking. Morning or evening snack.

⭐ Shops in Chora's streets · €4–6 · eat them hot
🐟

Fresh fish — at Fokos Taverna

The fish restaurants on Chora's harbourfront are expensive and inconsistent. For genuinely fresh fish go to Fokos Taverna — on the north coast, next to Fokos beach, not easy to reach but the fish is outstanding. Alternative: local tavernas in Ano Mera are both more affordable and fresher.

⭐ Fokos Taverna · Ano Mera tavernas · portion €15–25
🧀

Meze platter and Greek wine

Greek meze sharing culture: tzatziki, tarama, fava (yellow pea purée), stuffed vine leaves, olives, white cheese. A leisurely meal with wine that stretches on. Santorini Assyrtiko is the best match. Glass €8-12, bottle €25-45.

⭐ Local tavernas · meze platter €12–18 · try Assyrtiko wine
🍷 Ano Mera — eating at half Chora's price

8 kilometres east of Chora, the island's only real village. Tavernas around the square in front of Panagia Tourliani Monastery are both cheaper and far more local. A menu that costs €30-40 in Chora is €15-20 here. 15-20 minutes by car. Having lunch here and then heading to the northern beaches makes for a perfect day plan.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Mykonos

Mykonos's finest corners are far from the beach clubs — on the north coast, in quiet coves and in the early morning hours.

✨ Mykonos beach for those wanting to escape the crowds

Agios Sostis beach

A small cove on the northern part of the island, reached by vehicle or a long walk. No beach club, no sunbeds, no bar — just sand, rock and clean water. Kiki's Taverna alongside, no reservations and with a queue but both delicious and affordable. Even in midsummer, far from the crowds.

✨ Even older than Delos — the sacred island of the Aegean

Delos Island

30 minutes by boat from Mykonos Old Port. The sacred island of ancient Greece — where Apollo and Artemis are said to have been born. The Terrace of the Lions, mosaic-floored houses, temple ruins. UNESCO World Heritage Site. Take the first morning sailing, bring a hat and water. Closed for visits in winter.

✨ Paraportiani Church — one of the world's most photographed churches

Paraportiani Church

At the northern end of Chora, on a slope facing the sea. A cumulative white structure made up of five separate chapels. Considered one of the world's most photographed churches. Nearly empty between 7am and 8am. In the afternoon, crowds with cameras at every corner. The church interior is small and plain but the atmosphere is very powerful.

✨ An isolated beach experience in the northerly wind

Fokos beach and Fokos Taverna

In the north-east of the island, at the end of a steep road reached by vehicle. Completely natural, no facilities. The adjacent Fokos Taverna is known as Mykonos's finest fish restaurant — local produce, fresh fish, no reservations. Quiet even in midsummer. ATV or car essential. Plan a day trip that includes lunch here.

✨ Cine Manto — open-air cinema under the stars

Cine Manto

In the centre of Chora, inside a garden. A tradition much loved in Greece: open-air cinema on summer nights, continuing since the 1960s. Old Hollywood classics and modern films under the pine trees. Watching a film at night with tsipouro (local distilled spirit) and meze is another experience altogether. Ticket €10-12, reservation recommended.

✨ Ano Mera — non-touristy Mykonos

Ano Mera village

In the centre of the island, the island's only real inland village. The square around Panagia Tourliani Monastery, local cafés and tavernas. Very different from Mykonos Chora's white labyrinth — simpler, more local, quieter. A small market every day of the week. 15-20 minutes from Chora by car.

✨ The windmills — different in the morning and evening

The seven 16th-century windmills on the top of Chora are the symbol of Mykonos. Built to mill grain brought by the Aegean winds. Surrounded by photo crowds during the day. Between 7am and 8am in the morning or at sunset in the evening — both light and quiet are at their finest at those hours. The mill interiors are closed but the surroundings can be visited at any time.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Mykonos is a two-faced island: the bright shop window and the quiet back. Know these four and choose the one without the bill.

🛋️ TRAP

A sunbed at the famous beach clubs

Front-row sunbeds at the celebrated southern clubs rent for hundreds of euros; two coves over, the sea is the same blue.

💡 What to Do Instead: Turn north: at Agios Sostis and Fokos there are no clubs and no speakers — lay your towel and take the sea for free.
🍽️ TRAP

A 'Greek taverna' with a doorway inviter in Chora

Most places waving menus in the narrow lanes sell frozen moussaka at view prices.

💡 What to Do Instead: Sit where the locals sit: Ano Mera's square and the backstreet tavernas serve the same dish at half the price with twice the care.
🚕 TRAP

Counting on taxis

The island has a handful of taxis; waiting for one after the clubs takes hours and the haggling ends badly.

💡 What to Do Instead: Use the SeaBus between beaches, the bus network is surprisingly good; if you rent wheels, decide who's driving at night before you start.
🌅 TRAP

The 'sunset table' booking fee in Little Venice

Some bars charge extra for the window seat; but the sunset belongs to no one.

💡 What to Do Instead: Sit on the wall by the windmills: same sunset, zero fee. If you're drinking anyway, one cocktail's price includes the view.
🛡️ The golden rule

On Mykonos the wind (meltemi) picks your beach: north wind means calm southern coves, south wind means quiet northern beaches. Watch the wind, not the sunbeds.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Mykonos

Even three words of Greek get the ouzo flowing at the taverna.

🗣️ Yassas
Hello / goodbye — the formal, safe greeting
🗣️ Efharistó
Thank you — looks hard, lands by the second try
🗣️ Parakaló
Please / you're welcome — Greek's joker card
🗣️ Yamas!
Cheers! — ouzo glasses rise to this
🗣️ Opa!
The exclamation of joy — for breaking plates and starting dances alike
