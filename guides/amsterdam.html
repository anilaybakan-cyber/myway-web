Amsterdam

Freedom, canals, and more bicycles than people

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Amsterdam

The moment you step off the train, Amsterdam hits different. The houses lean sideways — yes, on purpose, they're 17th century and proud of it. The canals wind through the city like a maze. And somehow the whole place just feels free.

🗣️ Local Concept: Gezellig (heh-ZEL-ikh)

That warm, cozy, can't-quite-translate-it Dutch vibe — sitting by the water with a coffee, watching boats drift past. This single word explains the soul of Amsterdam. You'll feel it everywhere.

3 Unwritten Rules for Amsterdam

1

Never walk on the red bike paths

Cyclists own this city and they will not stop for you. Step onto a bike lane = instant danger. The number one tourist mistake in Amsterdam.

2

Amsterdam runs on cards, not cash

From tiny bakeries to street markets, most places are card-only. Don't be surprised by the 'Card Only' sign — just tap and go.

Getting Around

Amsterdam Public Transit & Airport Transfers

In Amsterdam, how you move is part of the experience. Get this right and the city opens up completely.

✈️ Airport → City Center

The simplest option is the train. Schiphol has its own station directly below the terminal — take the escalators down with your luggage and board. Trains to Amsterdam Centraal run every 10–15 minutes and take 15–20 minutes. Fast, cheap, and stress-free. Taxis and transfer services cost 4–5 times more and are subject to traffic.

🚲 Bicycle — the city's real lifeblood

Rent one and Amsterdam suddenly makes sense. Three golden rules: never walk on the red bike lanes; always use two locks — bike theft here is practically a local sport; never stop in the middle of a bike lane to take a photo. Other cyclists won't warn you, they'll just ride past.

🚋 Trams, Buses & Metro

Trams are the backbone of the city, gliding through narrow streets to reach everywhere. Tap your OV-chipkaart or phone when boarding AND when exiting. Missing the exit tap gets processed as a maximum fare by the system. Everyone makes this mistake at the beginning.

⛵ Free Ferries to Noord

Free ferries depart continuously from directly behind Centraal Station to Amsterdam Noord — free for pedestrians, cyclists and mopeds. Short, breezy, and a small experience in itself. Runs all day and into the night.

🚕 Taxi & Uber

Both options exist but cost 4–5 times more than public transport. Worth considering for late nights or special situations — not as daily transport. Uber works smoothly in Amsterdam.

💡 Shoe tip

Wear comfortable, flat shoes. Canal-side stones are uneven and slippery. When crossing any street — look for bikes first, cars second. The bicycle army is silent and fast.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Amsterdam: Best Areas

Your neighborhood shapes your whole trip. The Centrum is overpriced and loud — step one ring out and Amsterdam gets real.

Jordaan Most romantic

The most 'Amsterdam' neighbourhood that exists. Narrow alleys, ivy-covered canal houses, boutique hotels right on the water. Buildings are 400 years old — expect very steep stairs and tiny rooms. Bring a small suitcase.

Calm & central • Steep stairs • Small suitcase essential
De Pijp Best nightlife

Amsterdam's 'Latin Quarter.' Home to the Albert Cuypmarkt — the city's biggest open-air market — a buzzing student scene, and restaurants from every corner of the world. Slightly cheaper than center, way more alive.

Nightlife-friendly • Great food scene • Budget-friendly
Grachtengordel (Canal Belt) UNESCO listed

Living inside a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Grand, high-ceilinged canal houses that end up on every postcard. Walking distance to everything, but expect tourist crowds on weekends.

Most prestigious • Weekend crowds • Postcard-perfect
Oud-West Local favourite

The local's secret. Home to De Hallen — a gorgeous food hall and cinema in a converted tram depot — walking distance to Vondelpark. Best value-for-money area in Amsterdam. 10 minutes to center by tram.

Best value • Near Vondelpark • Safe & stylish
Amsterdam Noord Free ferry

Across the free ferry behind Central Station. Former shipyards turned cool hotels, massive street art murals, digital nomad hub. Completely different energy. Catch the NDSM flea market on weekends.

Free ferry access • Street art • Weekend flea market
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Amsterdam

Amsterdam isn't a 'one perfect time' city — it's a 'depends what you're after' city.

Spring Mar – May
Tulip Season

Keukenhof Gardens explodes with millions of tulips. Then April 27th — King's Day — the entire city turns orange, every street becomes a party, and the canals fill with boats. One of the biggest street celebrations in Europe. If you can only go once, make it spring.

Summer Jun – Aug
Long Days

Sun sets past 10 PM. Locals picnic on canal boats. Vondelpark buzzes with live music. But weather is unpredictable — warm morning? Bring a jacket anyway. Rain arrives without warning.

Autumn Sep – Nov
Moody & Golden

Canal trees turn amber and orange, the city gets its famous melancholic, moody beauty. October brings Amsterdam Dance Event (ADE) — the whole city becomes the world's electronic music capital. Gezellig spirit peaks: rainy evenings, warm brown cafes, locals actually home.

Winter Dec – Feb
Canal Magic

Quieter, mistier, and honestly magical. The Amsterdam Light Festival transforms the canals into an open-air art museum. And if you get very lucky — rare, but it happens — the canals freeze and locals ice skate on them.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Amsterdam

Dutch food is better than its reputation. Some of this you can only find here.

🍟 Vlaamse Frites + Patat Oorlog

Thick-cut, double-fried, in a paper cone. Order Patat Oorlog (War Fries): peanut sauce, mayo, and raw onion. Sounds chaotic. Tastes incredible.

⭐ Try this first
🧇 Stroopwafel — fresh, not packaged

Two thin waffle layers with caramel in the middle. Get one warm from Albert Cuypmarkt. The supermarket versions don't compare.

⭐ Albert Cuypmarkt only
🧀 Dutch Cheese (Kaas)

You're in Gouda country. Cheese shops offer free tastings — aged, young, smoked, herb-infused. Try Henri Willig or Old Amsterdam. Always free to taste.

⭐ Free tastings available
🍺 Bitterballen + Borrel

Fried crispy balls filled with creamy meat ragout, dipped in mustard. Always eaten alongside an afternoon beer — that's the Borrel, Amsterdam's after-work ritual. Any brown café.

⭐ The true local ritual
🐟 Haring (Raw Herring)

Hold by the tail, tilt back, lower it in — that's the traditional way. Too bold? Get a broodje haring with pickles and onion. Either way, try it once.

⭐ Rite of passage
🍛 Rijsttafel — Indonesian Feast

15–20 small Indonesian dishes served together — rich, spiced, completely unlike anything else in Amsterdam. The most delicious remnant of Dutch colonial history. Reserve an evening.

⭐ Reserve in advance
🥞 Poffertjes

Tiny, fluffy mini-pancakes dusted with powdered sugar and butter. A childhood favourite in the Netherlands. Find them at any street market.

⭐ Kids & adults love these
🍷 Late night secret

Find a FEBO automat — tap your card at a wall of little glass windows and pull out a hot kroket. Amsterdam's oldest fast-food tradition. Completely unique after midnight.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Amsterdam

The real Amsterdam is behind doors, down alleys, and across free ferries. Most tourists walk right past these.

Ons' Lieve Heer op Solder — Church in an Attic Extremely rare find

From the outside: a normal canal house. Inside, hidden on the top floor: a fully intact 17th-century Catholic church. Built secretly when Catholic worship was banned. Amsterdam's most powerful symbol of tolerance — almost nobody knows it's there.

Begijnhof — The Hidden Courtyard Medieval secret

A medieval courtyard behind an unmarked door on Spui square. Step through and the noise disappears entirely. Built in the 14th century. No sign on the door — look for a small wooden gate in the wall.

Tuschinski Theater Art Deco masterpiece

One of the most beautiful Art Deco buildings in the world. Even if you don't watch a film, walk into the lobby. Carpets, ceiling, chandeliers — a time machine to 1921 Amsterdam. Most tourists walk past and never go inside.

NDSM Werf + Weekend Flea Market Noord secret — weekends only

Old shipyard turned street art wonderland via the free ferry. Giant scrap metal sculptures, massive murals. On weekends: the NDSM flea market brings artists, food trucks, and vintage stalls. The Amsterdam of the future.

Hofjes — Secret Garden Courts Jordaan's best-kept secret

Amsterdam has dozens of hidden gardens behind unmarked wooden doors. Begijnhof is the famous one, but Jordaan has several others — Karthuizerhofje, Sint Andrieshofje. Follow small signs on walls. Step through and find a garden 400 years removed from the city outside.

Winkel 43 — The Apple Pie Pilgrimage Best apple pie debate settled

Thick, cinnamon-heavy, served warm with a mountain of fresh whipped cream (slagroom). Queue is worth every minute. Go for afternoon coffee on a rainy day.

✨ De Wallen rule

Do NOT photograph the windows in the Red Light District. Illegal, deeply disrespectful, hostile response guaranteed. Phone stays in pocket — no exceptions.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Amsterdam is not ruthless about tourist traps, but the center has a few classics. Know them, and spend your money and time on the real Amsterdam.

🍽️ TRAP

Photo-menu restaurants on Damrak and Leidseplein

The neon-lit places that greet you off the train, with a waiter at the door and a menu in 12 languages. Overpriced, mediocre, and where no Dutch person ever sits.

💡 What to Do Instead: Walk two streets in: in Jordaan or De Pijp the same money buys a meal twice as good.
🚢 TRAP

Giant glass-covered canal boats

The big boats hauling 70 people with a recorded audio guide show you the canals without letting you feel them. Even photos are hard through the glass.

💡 What to Do Instead: Pick a small open boat with a live skipper — and time it for sunset.
🧀 TRAP

Souvenir cheese and tulip shops on Damrak

Vacuum-packed 'Gouda' and tulip bulbs that will never bloom. Inflated prices, low quality, and much of it isn't even locally made.

💡 What to Do Instead: Buy cheese at a real kaas shop after tasting; get certified (customs-approved) bulbs at Bloemenmarkt stalls.
🚕 TRAP

Taxi from Schiphol to the city

You'll pay €60–70 and take twice as long as the train in traffic. The airport taxi queue is Amsterdam's most expensive waiting room.

💡 What to Do Instead: The train reaches Centraal in 15–20 minutes; the platforms are right below the terminal.
🛡️ The golden rule

If someone at the door is calling you inside, the place can't sell itself. Good spots in Amsterdam never call you — you find them.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Amsterdam

Everyone speaks English, but even a single word of Dutch changes faces.

🗣️ Dank je wel
Thank you — the word you'll use most
🗣️ Alsjeblieft
Please / here you go — also said when handing something over
🗣️ Proost!
Cheers! Don't forget the eye contact
🗣️ Lekker
Tasty / great — works for food and for the weather
🗣️ Doei!
Bye — cheerful, said twice: doei doei!
