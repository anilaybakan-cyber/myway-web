Ksamil

Ionian turquoise, lagoon mussels and the pearl of the Albanian Riviera

Overview

The Vibe of Ksamil

Ksamil is a small headland on the map, but the colour of its water could make a continent jealous: here the Ionian turns Caribbean, four little islands sit within swimming distance, and the silhouette of Corfu stretches across the strait. On one side lie Butrint's two-thousand-year-old stones, on the other the mussel lines of the lagoon. This is not a polished resort but a fast-growing fishing village — a little untidy, deeply warm-hearted, and still the most honestly priced clear water in the Mediterranean. Come avash avash: hurry has no place in the language of these coves.

💬 Avash avash
🗣️ /ah-vash ah-vash/

'Slowly, slowly': a phrase that passed from Ottoman Turkish into Albanian and sums up the tempo of the coastal towns. The waiter and the boatman give the same answer — avash avash. Adopt it and your holiday starts sooner.

Insider Advice

Unwritten Rules for Ksamil

#1
Into the sea early, into the shade at noon In July and August the coves fill after 11 and the sunbeds run out: between 8 and 10 the water is like glass and the beach half empty. Take a long lunch at midday and return for the late-afternoon swim — that is the island rhythm.
#2
Get used to two currencies The official money is the lek, but the euro also speaks fluently on this coast: clarify which currency a price is quoted in, and your change may come back in lek. Keep the exchange rate ready on your phone — confusion is the most expensive tip.
#3
Make Ksamil the base, collect the surroundings The magic here is in the radius: Butrint 10 minutes, the Blue Eye half an hour, Sarandë 20 minutes, Corfu across the water by ferry. Whoever never leaves the sunbed sees the riviera's richest corner only by half.
Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Ksamil

💎 HIDDEN GEM
The ancient city of Butrint
The UNESCO site at the lagoon's end: a Greek theatre, Roman baths, a Byzantine baptistery and a Venetian tower in the shade of one forest. Enter at the first hour — among the stones, just the tortoises and you.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Syri i Kaltër — the Blue Eye
An ice-blue spring surging from deep underground: its true depth has never been fully measured. The water is 10 degrees — diving is banned, but the spectacle is free. Go early and skip the midday queue.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Plazhi i Pasqyrave — Mirror Beach
A small cove hidden among the cliffs on the Sarandë road: the water so still it takes its name from a mirror. The stair descent filters the crowds — at an early hour it's nearly a private beach.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Pema e Thatë
The 'Dry Tree' beach: a long calm strand at Ksamil's southern tip, reached through pine and olive. Corfu straight ahead — the crowds far behind.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Sunset at Lëkurësi Castle
The Ottoman castle above Sarandë: the bay, the Ksamil headland, Corfu and the Greek mountains behind, all from one terrace. Go up for sunset with a glass in hand — this is the riviera's balcony.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
The four islands by kayak
The finest way to Ksamil's islets is a kayak: pass cove by cove, land on deserted pebble strips, snorkel the rocks. No engine noise, no queue — the reward of 8 am.
Food & Flavors

What to Taste

The Ksamil table draws from two sources: the Butrint lagoon's mussels and the Ionian's daily catch — with the village bakery and raki alongside.

🍴 MUST TASTE
Butrint mussels
The lagoon's mussels are small, sweet and intense: steamed in white wine is the classic, served near-raw with lemon the brave choice. The price per kilo is a friendly surprise — order until you're full.
🍴 MUST TASTE
The day's fish on the grill
Sea bass, bream, or whatever the morning net brought: chosen by the kilo, grilled over coals, served with olive oil and lemon. Ask the price before the scales — that is the custom.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Grilled octopus
Octopus sun-dried and finished over coals is the pride of the shore restaurants: smoky outside, tender inside. With rocket and a cold white — the crowned plate of a beach afternoon.
🍴 MUST TASTE
A byrek morning
Hand-rolled pastry with spinach, cheese or minced meat: bought hot from the bakery, eaten standing. A byrek + dhallë (the local ayran) — the native formula for a pre-beach breakfast.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Fërgesë and the village plates
Peppers, tomatoes and curd cheese meeting in a clay dish: Albanian home cooking in summary. If you see tavë kosi (lamb baked in yoghurt), don't hesitate — the interior's classic has come down to the coast.
🍴 MUST TASTE
The raki ritual and local wine
Grape raki is the official language of the pre-dinner offering: in a small glass, with a 'Gëzuar!'. Albanian winemaking is rising too — ask, and try a local bottle.
Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

❌
In August the front-row sunbed sets ask serious euros, and once you've sat down the haggling is over.
✅ Better Alternative: Ask the price before lying down, or spread your towel in the free zone. Arrive before 9 am and on most beaches you'll take the front row at the normal price.
❌
Pay in euros, receive change in lek, and the rate gets 'rounded'; small differences add up to a plump invisible bill by day's end.
✅ Better Alternative: Pay in lek where possible, keep the rate on your phone, and count your change. An ATM's lek is usually better than the beachfront rate.
❌
Short hops are quoted at season prices; on routes like the Blue Eye a 'waiting fee' is added afterwards.
✅ Better Alternative: Fix the total — waiting included — before getting in, or use the furgon + scooter pair; both cost less than half.
❌
At midday the car park overflows, the path becomes a queue and you stand selfie-watch at the spring.
✅ Better Alternative: Go at the first hour or after 5 pm: the water just as blue, the forest cool, the platform empty. Stop at a village restaurant on the way back — real prices, no tourist menu.
