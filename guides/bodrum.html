Bodrum

White houses, bougainvillea purple and the birthplace of the Blue Voyage

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Bodrum

The Fisherman of Halicarnassus arrived in Bodrum as an exile and stayed as a lover — and this city's stories always run that way. Whitewashed houses are framed in bougainvillea purple, the castle stands watch between two bays, and the gulets weigh anchor here for the Blue Voyage. The Mausoleum, one of antiquity's Seven Wonders, stood on this soil; today's wonder is humbler and lovelier: the evening wind above the marina, dark-brewed tea, and lanes that walk down into the sea. The Bodrum peninsula is not one town but an archipelago opening bay by bay — don't hurry; the bays aren't going anywhere.

🗣️ Local Concept: Mavi Yolculuk (mah-vee yol-joo-look)

The 'Blue Voyage': the bay-by-bay gulet journey invented in the 1940s by the Fisherman of Halicarnassus and his friends, spread from Bodrum to the world. The route is loose, there is no clock, and the compass is pleasure — sooner or later it plants its dream in everyone who looks at the sea.

3 Unwritten Rules for Bodrum

1

Don't mistake the centre for the peninsula

Central Bodrum is the stage, but the real play is in the bays: Gümüşlük's quiet evening, Bitez's mandarin scent and Yalıkavak's wind are different worlds. Write at least two bays into the programme — the dolmuş minibuses shuttle all day.

2

Plan the sunset for Gümüşlük

The peninsula's western tip is the sunset's official box seat: the Gümüşlük quay and the windmills are painted orange first. Reserve the table ahead — this view has many regulars.

3

Don't skip the castle and what's under the sea

The Museum of Underwater Archaeology inside Bodrum Castle is among the world's best: thousand-year-old shipwrecks, glass treasures and the story of the Carian princess. Give one beach morning to history — the castle already waits between the two bays.

Getting Around

Bodrum Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Bodrum's transport is woven from dolmuş minibuses: one leaves the centre for every bay, boats complete the picture from the sea, and those who know the summer traffic leave early.

Milas-Bodrum airport is 45 minutes from the centre: Havaş buses run to the bus station and transfers fan out to the bays. On summer nights, plan the return to your flight with a generous margin.

Minibuses run all day from the bus station to Gümüşlük, Bitez, Yalıkavak and Türkbükü: cheap, frequent and used to beach bags. Saying 'müsait bir yerde' (anywhere convenient) is the first sentence of peninsula citizenship.

Daily boat tours leave from below the castle and gulets set out on the Blue Voyage: Kara Ada, the Aquarium cove and Kisebükü are the classic route. The small-boat, few-seats formula always wins.

The castle, the marina, Bar Street and the ancient theatre are within walking distance: the shore strip is flat, the back lanes climb. The late-afternoon marina stroll is Bodrum's official paseo.

For bay hunting, two or four wheels mean freedom: the roads are good, but summer traffic and parking demand patience. Leave early for Gümüşlük and Yalıkavak evenings and never rush the return.

⚡ Pro Tip

Board the Gümüşlük dolmuş two hours before sunset: claim a quay table, order your fish, and watch the sun sink behind Rabbit Island. The return minibuses run late into the night — no hurry.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Bodrum

Everyone sees the castle; these six are the peninsula's hidden coves and paths.

Rabbit Island — Gümüşlük

The islet crossed on foot through knee-deep water along ancient Myndos's sunken causeway: at sunset it falls dead-centre in the silhouette. When the sea pulls back the path appears — carry your shoes and walk on history.

Karakaya Village

Old Bodrum on the peninsula's heights, with its stone houses and narrow paths: bougainvillea, stone and silence. A late-afternoon walk among the restored mansions shows the peninsula's pre-tourism face.

The ancient city of Pedasa

A Lelegian city hidden in the pine forest above Konacık: walls, tower ruins and a gulf view. Reached by a short hike — the peninsula's oldest layer, where the crowds never call.

The windmill hill

Old stone mills lined along the ridge between Bodrum and Gümbet: a free sunset box seeing two bays at once. The photographers come early; the wind never leaves.

Kisebükü (Paradise Cove)

A cove with Byzantine monastery ruins, hard to reach by land and an hour away by sea: pine shade, shallow turquoise and old stone walls. The Blue Voyage's classic pause — the daily boats call here too.

The glass hall of the Underwater Museum

The Serçe Harbour glass-wreck hall inside the castle museum is unique in the world: a thousand-year-old cargo of glass lifted from the sea into the light. The brightest gift of Bodrum's sponge-diving tradition.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Bodrum

Hoş geldin
(hosh gel-din)
The heart of the welcome — you'll hear it as you take your quay table
Müsait bir yerde
(moo-sah-eet beer yer-deh)
The dolmuş culture's key phrase: 'drop me anywhere convenient' — step one of peninsula citizenship
Afiyet olsun
(ah-fee-yet ol-soon)
The table's opening and closing — heard often after the meze cabinet
Şerefe!
(sheh-reh-feh)
Cheers! — the official word of the Gümüşlük quay towards sunset
Kolay gelsin
(ko-lie gel-sin)
To the fisherman, the shopkeeper, the captain — a courtesy to anyone at work, always returned with a smile
