Lesbos

Ouzo, olives and an island in no hurry

Overview

The Vibe of Lesbos

When the ferry docks at Mytilene, what greets you is not a tourist island but a living Greek province. Lesbos is Greece's third-largest island: eleven million olive trees, the world's best ouzo, hot springs, and villages that never surrendered to tourism. Nobody here tries to sell you anything; you sip your coffee, swim, and get pulled into the conversation at the next table. The island is big and unhurried — be unhurried too.

💬 Siga siga
🗣️ /SEE-gah SEE-gah/

Greek for 'slowly, slowly': the tempo of Lesbos and its official philosophy. Service running late, ferry delayed? Siga siga. Adopting these two words is the only way to tune into the island.

Insider Advice

Unwritten Rules for Lesbos

#1
Rent a car — the island is bigger than it looks Lesbos covers 1,600 km²: Mytilene to Molyvos takes over an hour. Buses are sparse; to really see the island you need wheels. The roads are good but winding — no rushing between the olive groves.
#2
Never drink ouzo without food Plomari ouzo is the world's finest, but it's drunk the Greek way: always with meze, ice and water. Everyone at the table can spot the tourist who downs the glass in one.
#3
Sort your ferry and passport logistics early Coming from Turkey, visa-on-arrival practice and ferry schedules change with the season. Book the Ayvalık–Mytilene crossing ahead and check your return sailing on day one.
Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Lesbos

💎 HIDDEN GEM
Sigri Petrified Forest
At the island's western tip, sequoia trunks sealed in volcanic ash 20 million years ago: a UNESCO geopark, an open-air museum and a landscape like the moon. Few places on Earth compare.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Skala Sikamnias
The north coast's tiny fishing harbour: the Panagia Gorgona chapel on its rock, tavernas shaded by a mulberry tree, and the atmosphere the writer Stratis Myrivilis carried into literature. Island calm, distilled.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Eftalou Hot Springs
Near Molyvos, an Ottoman-era stone-domed bathhouse above a pebble beach: healing water at 43 degrees, the cool Aegean two steps away. The hot-cold ritual costs a symbolic entrance fee.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Moni Limonos
The island's largest monastery: peacocks in the courtyard, manuscripts in the library, centuries in the silence. Just off the Kalloni road, a world most visitors never turn into.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Kalloni Salt Pans
Thousands of flamingos and over 250 bird species in spring and autumn: one of Europe's finest birdwatching sites. Bring binoculars, take a hide, wait.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Agiasos
A mountain village in the chestnut forest on the slopes of Mount Olympos: ceramic workshops, a centuries-old church of the Virgin, cool stone lanes. The island's freshest escape in high summer.
Food & Flavors

What to Taste

The cuisine of Lesbos rests on two pillars: olive oil and the sea. The third arrives at the table with the ouzo.

🍴 MUST TASTE
Kalloni sardines (sardeles pastes)
The famous sardines of Kalloni Bay are salt-cured for a few hours and served almost raw-fresh; counted among Greece's most celebrated meze. The star of the August sardine festival.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Plomari ouzo
The anise national drink finds its finest form on this island; the distilleries of Plomari and Mytilene export it to the world. It clouds over ice, opens with meze, ends in conversation.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Ladotyri — cheese aged in olive oil
The island's protected sheep's cheese matures in jars of olive oil; hard, salty, full of character. Grilled as saganaki it becomes a feast of its own.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Olives and olive oil
On an island of eleven million trees, the oil is fresh enough to drink. Buy a bottle of cold-pressed from a village market; it will be the most precious gift in your suitcase.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Grilled octopus and calamari
The octopus drying in the sun outside the harbour tavernas is the evening's menu: char-grilled, with lemon, by the water. Molyvos and Skala Sikamnias harbours are the classic addresses.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Baklava and amygdalota
The Ottoman-heritage sweet tradition is alive and well: pistachio baklava, almond amygdalota and kadayif with ice cream. The patisseries of Mytilene's bazaar are a century old.
Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

❌
The Ayvalık line fills up in summer and cancels in wind; hearing 'no seats tomorrow' at the counter is the classic end to a holiday.
✅ Better Alternative: Buy the round trip up front and confirm the time a day before returning; stay flexible — the island deserves an extra night anyway.
❌
Small on the map, the island is a real journey on the ground; attempting Molyvos + Plomari + Sigri in one day is a holiday spent at the wheel.
✅ Better Alternative: Split the island into regions: one day north (Molyvos–Petra), one day south (Plomari–Agiasos), one day west (Kalloni–Sigri).
❌
The fancy bottles at the airport and gift shops are usually the island's ordinary line; you're paying for packaging.
✅ Better Alternative: Buy ouzo from a distillery's own shop or a village grocer; tasting and choosing in Plomari is half the fun.
❌
Staying at the hotel beach because it's easy means missing the island's coastal variety: every cove has a different character.
✅ Better Alternative: Try a new cove daily: the clarity of Agios Isidoros, the sand of Skala Eresou, the red pebbles of Tsonia — the car is already rented.
