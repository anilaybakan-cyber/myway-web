Florence

The Renaissance was born here — and the city reminds you of this on every corner, without ever becoming tiresome

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Florence

Florence is a small city — the historic centre is compact enough to walk. Inside it: Botticelli, Michelangelo, Leonardo, Brunelleschi; the Uffizi, Accademia, Bargello; a church on every corner, a fresco in every church. The only way to deal with this density is to set priorities: if you try to see everything at once, you won't really see any of it. Florence's dusty stone buildings, narrow streets, morning markets and evening aperitivo come together in a formula found nowhere else. The Arno River divides the city in two; on the far bank, the Oltrarno neighbourhoods — where most tourists don't cross, where workshops and local bars carry on, where the real Florence lives.

🗣️ Local Concept: Cucina povera (ku-CHI-na PO-ve-ra)

The poor man's kitchen. Florence's best dishes came from here: lampredotto (beef tripe), ribollita (stale bread soup), pappa al pomodoro (tomato and bread porridge). Cheap ingredients, long cooking times, deep flavour. Bistecca alla Fiorentina is expensive and showy — cucina povera is the real face of the city.

3 Unwritten Rules for Florence

1

Don't go to the Uffizi without a reservation

The Uffizi and the Accademia (Michelangelo's David) are Florence's most visited museums. Queues at the door can last hours — you might not get in at all. Book tickets in advance from the official websites — there's a booking fee but it's worth every minute saved in the queue. The first hours of the morning (9-10am) are the least crowded.

2

Tuscan bread is unsalted — be ready for this

Pane sciocco (unsalted bread) is a centuries-old Tuscan tradition. It might seem strange on the first bite. But dipped into cured meats, cheese or sauces it makes sense — it's designed to absorb the salt from the food beside it, not to be eaten on its own. Schiacciata, however — the flat bread with olive oil and salt — is salty and delicious.

3

Know what real gelato looks like

High-piled gelato in big windows, neon coloured, is a tourist trap. Real good gelato looks the opposite: simple, understated, naturally coloured. Pistachio is yellowish not green, strawberry is pinkish not red. Because there's no colouring — the colour comes only from the natural ingredients.

Getting Around

Florence Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Florence is small and the historic centre is walkable. Bus and tram are the second option; taxis are expensive.

The T2 Vespucci tram takes you from the airport to Santa Maria Novella train station in about 25 minutes. Departures every 5 minutes, ticket €1.70. From February 2025 the line was extended to Piazza San Marco. Fixed-price taxi around €22-25. The tram is easily the most practical option.

City buses and trams run on the same ticket system. Single fare €1.70, valid for 90 minutes, free transfers. Tickets available at tabacchi (tobacconist) shops, machines at bus stops or tram stops. You can't buy a ticket after boarding a bus — buy in advance and validate it when you get on. Contactless bank card payment of €1.70 is also accepted.

Line T1 runs from Scandicci to Santa Maria Novella; line T2 runs from Vespucci Airport via Santa Maria Novella to Piazza San Marco. Both are useful for reaching the city centre. Same ticket as the bus, €1.70.

Florence's historic centre is largely pedestrianised — vehicles are restricted. Bike rental is available but take care on the narrow and busy streets. The route along the Arno is bike-friendly.

From the Duomo: Uffizi 10 minutes, Ponte Vecchio 15 minutes, Santa Croce 15 minutes, Mercato Centrale 10 minutes. The historic centre is almost entirely walkable. To cross to Oltrarno: 5 minutes on foot over Ponte Vecchio.

⚡ Pro Tip

Florence's historic centre is a ZTL zone (Zona a Traffico Limitato) — restricted to vehicle traffic. Unauthorised vehicles are detected by automatic cameras and face heavy fines. If you've arrived by rental car, don't enter the historic centre. The best solution is to park outside and walk or use public transport.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Florence

Florence's quietest and most unexpected corners are usually overshadowed by the famous museums.

Bargello Museum

On Via del Proconsolo, 5 minutes' walk from the Uffizi. The most important collection of Renaissance sculpture is here: Donatello's David, Michelangelo's early works, Ghiberti and Verrocchio. A fraction of the Uffizi's visitor numbers. The first-floor courtyard — when afternoon light passes through it — is one of Florence's most beautiful and most silent spaces. Opens at 8:30am and is usually quiet until midday.

Laurentian Library

On the upper floor of San Lorenzo Basilica, with a separate ticket. The entrance vestibule and its dramatic, wavy staircase designed by Michelangelo — one of the earliest examples of Mannerism. Around 11,000 Medici manuscripts are held here. Open Monday-Saturday 10am-1pm.

Caffetteria delle Oblate

On Via dell'Oriuolo, 3 minutes' walk from the Duomo. A small café on the rooftop of the city library. The rear façade of the Duomo and a city panorama in front of you with your coffee or aperitivo. Limited seating — check before you go. The library itself is free to enter — you can sit inside too.

Piazza Santo Spirito

In Oltrarno, 15 minutes' walk from Ponte Vecchio. Quiet during the day — but after 6pm Florentines gather here. Bars and osterias all around, a fountain in the centre, people sitting on the steps. A small market runs every morning except Monday. Sunday brings an organic and craft market.

San Miniato al Monte

Just above Piazzale Michelangelo, 10 more minutes of walking. An 11th-century basilica above the city. A fraction of Piazzale's visitor numbers. The Tuscan marble floor and apse mosaic inside are among Florence's least-known art treasures. Go before sunset: first San Miniato, then down to Piazzale. Both are free.

Ponte Vecchio — early morning

Between 7:30 and 8:30am, before the jewellery shops open, the bridge is almost empty. At this hour you can stand on it, watch the Arno and see the Vasari Corridor passing above. Note the open gap in the middle of the bridge: left deliberately open for the bust and the view of the Arno.
