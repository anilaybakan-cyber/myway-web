Florence

The Renaissance was born here — and the city reminds you of this on every corner, without ever becoming tiresome

Overview

The Vibe of Florence

Florence is a small city — the historic centre is compact enough to walk. Inside it: Botticelli, Michelangelo, Leonardo, Brunelleschi; the Uffizi, Accademia, Bargello; a church on every corner, a fresco in every church. The only way to deal with this density is to set priorities: if you try to see everything at once, you won't really see any of it. Florence's dusty stone buildings, narrow streets, morning markets and evening aperitivo come together in a formula found nowhere else. The Arno River divides the city in two; on the far bank, the Oltrarno neighbourhoods — where most tourists don't cross, where workshops and local bars carry on, where the real Florence lives.

💬 Cucina povera
🗣️ /ku-CHI-na PO-ve-ra/

The poor man's kitchen. Florence's best dishes came from here: lampredotto (beef tripe), ribollita (stale bread soup), pappa al pomodoro (tomato and bread porridge). Cheap ingredients, long cooking times, deep flavour. Bistecca alla Fiorentina is expensive and showy — cucina povera is the real face of the city.

Insider Advice

Unwritten Rules for Florence

#1
Don't go to the Uffizi without a reservation The Uffizi and the Accademia (Michelangelo's David) are Florence's most visited museums. Queues at the door can last hours — you might not get in at all. Book tickets in advance from the official websites — there's a booking fee but it's worth every minute saved in the queue. The first hours of the morning (9-10am) are the least crowded.
#2
Tuscan bread is unsalted — be ready for this Pane sciocco (unsalted bread) is a centuries-old Tuscan tradition. It might seem strange on the first bite. But dipped into cured meats, cheese or sauces it makes sense — it's designed to absorb the salt from the food beside it, not to be eaten on its own. Schiacciata, however — the flat bread with olive oil and salt — is salty and delicious.
#3
Know what real gelato looks like High-piled gelato in big windows, neon coloured, is a tourist trap. Real good gelato looks the opposite: simple, understated, naturally coloured. Pistachio is yellowish not green, strawberry is pinkish not red. Because there's no colouring — the colour comes only from the natural ingredients.
Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Florence

💎 HIDDEN GEM
Bargello Museum
On Via del Proconsolo, 5 minutes' walk from the Uffizi. The most important collection of Renaissance sculpture is here: Donatello's David, Michelangelo's early works, Ghiberti and Verrocchio. A fraction of the Uffizi's visitor numbers. The first-floor courtyard — when afternoon light passes through it — is one of Florence's most beautiful and most silent spaces. Opens at 8:30am and is usually quiet until midday.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Laurentian Library
On the upper floor of San Lorenzo Basilica, with a separate ticket. The entrance vestibule and its dramatic, wavy staircase designed by Michelangelo — one of the earliest examples of Mannerism. Around 11,000 Medici manuscripts are held here. Open Monday-Saturday 10am-1pm.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Caffetteria delle Oblate
On Via dell'Oriuolo, 3 minutes' walk from the Duomo. A small café on the rooftop of the city library. The rear façade of the Duomo and a city panorama in front of you with your coffee or aperitivo. Limited seating — check before you go. The library itself is free to enter — you can sit inside too.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Piazza Santo Spirito
In Oltrarno, 15 minutes' walk from Ponte Vecchio. Quiet during the day — but after 6pm Florentines gather here. Bars and osterias all around, a fountain in the centre, people sitting on the steps. A small market runs every morning except Monday. Sunday brings an organic and craft market.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
San Miniato al Monte
Just above Piazzale Michelangelo, 10 more minutes of walking. An 11th-century basilica above the city. A fraction of Piazzale's visitor numbers. The Tuscan marble floor and apse mosaic inside are among Florence's least-known art treasures. Go before sunset: first San Miniato, then down to Piazzale. Both are free.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Ponte Vecchio — early morning
Between 7:30 and 8:30am, before the jewellery shops open, the bridge is almost empty. At this hour you can stand on it, watch the Arno and see the Vasari Corridor passing above. Note the open gap in the middle of the bridge: left deliberately open for the bust and the view of the Arno.
Food & Flavors

What to Taste

Florence is home to one of Italy's finest cuisines — but without pretension. Bistecca is expensive, lampredotto is cheap; both are Florentine.

🍴 MUST TASTE
Bistecca alla Fiorentina
Giant T-bone steak, Chianina beef, cooked over charcoal, red inside. €45-60 per kilo — shared between two people. It has to be 'al sangue' (rare). If you can accept that, this is one of Florence's most iconic experiences.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Lampredotto sandwich
The fourth stomach of the cow, boiled, sliced and placed into a simple semmel roll. Topped with salsa verde and chilli sauce. Ask for it 'bagnato' — they dip the top of the bread in the cooking broth, and the bread gets soaked — this is the critical difference. €4-5. At stalls in Mercato Centrale or Sant'Ambrogio Market. You'll either love it or not, but you can't leave Florence without trying.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Ribollita
A thick soup of white beans, cavolo nero (black kale), carrot, celery and stale Tuscan bread. The name means 'reboiled' because it's better the next day. A winter dish — October to March. Appears as a daily special at trattorias. Cheap and filling: €8-12.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Schiacciata sandwich
Tuscany's flat olive oil and salt bread (schiacciata), filled with prosciutto, pecorino or grilled vegetables. Florence's lunch-break food: quick, affordable, eaten standing. Fresh from bakeries from the morning onwards.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Real gelato
Metal containers, flat tops, natural colours: pistachio is yellowish-brown, strawberry pale pink. Those are real. Plastic containers, neon colours, high piled mounds — tourist trap. Gelateria dei Neri, Gelateria della Passera (Oltrarno), Bonchi.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Aperitivo culture
Between 6pm and 8pm. Order a Negroni or Aperol Spritz — small snacks come alongside. Some bars include a buffet with aperitivo. Oltrarno bars, Piazza Santo Spirito and Via San Niccolò are the right addresses for this ritual.
Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

❌
The 'skip-the-line' hawkers around the museum charge multiples of the official ticket; some tickets turn out invalid at the door.
✅ Better Alternative: Buy only from the official site, dated and timed; the calmest hours are the first slot and two hours before closing.
❌
Not everything labelled 'vera pelle' is genuine; glued soles and synthetic leather abound on the tourist stalls.
✅ Better Alternative: No flame test needed: check the stitching and the smell, and buy from workshops like the Scuola del Cuoio or long-established shops.
❌
Real bistecca alla fiorentina is sold by the etto (100 g) and served rare; the promise of bistecca 'per plate' is a thinned-out disappointment.
✅ Better Alternative: Go where the price is quoted per etto and accept it rare; sharing one between two is the tradition itself.
❌
Half an hour before sunset the terrace fills with coach convoys; the view is lovely, the experience a scrum.
✅ Better Alternative: Climb ten minutes more: the steps of San Miniato al Monte give the same view plus monks' chanting — and room to breathe.
