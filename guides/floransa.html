Florence

The Renaissance was born here — and the city reminds you of this on every corner, without ever becoming tiresome

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Florence

Florence is a small city — the historic centre is compact enough to walk. Inside it: Botticelli, Michelangelo, Leonardo, Brunelleschi; the Uffizi, Accademia, Bargello; a church on every corner, a fresco in every church. The only way to deal with this density is to set priorities: if you try to see everything at once, you won't really see any of it. Florence's dusty stone buildings, narrow streets, morning markets and evening aperitivo come together in a formula found nowhere else. The Arno River divides the city in two; on the far bank, the Oltrarno neighbourhoods — where most tourists don't cross, where workshops and local bars carry on, where the real Florence lives.

🗣️ Local Concept: Cucina povera (ku-CHI-na PO-ve-ra)

The poor man's kitchen. Florence's best dishes came from here: lampredotto (beef tripe), ribollita (stale bread soup), pappa al pomodoro (tomato and bread porridge). Cheap ingredients, long cooking times, deep flavour. Bistecca alla Fiorentina is expensive and showy — cucina povera is the real face of the city.

3 Unwritten Rules for Florence

1

Don't go to the Uffizi without a reservation

The Uffizi and the Accademia (Michelangelo's David) are Florence's most visited museums. Queues at the door can last hours — you might not get in at all. Book tickets in advance from the official websites — there's a booking fee but it's worth every minute saved in the queue. The first hours of the morning (9-10am) are the least crowded.

2

Tuscan bread is unsalted — be ready for this

Pane sciocco (unsalted bread) is a centuries-old Tuscan tradition. It might seem strange on the first bite. But dipped into cured meats, cheese or sauces it makes sense — it's designed to absorb the salt from the food beside it, not to be eaten on its own. Schiacciata, however — the flat bread with olive oil and salt — is salty and delicious.

3

Know what real gelato looks like

High-piled gelato in big windows, neon coloured, is a tourist trap. Real good gelato looks the opposite: simple, understated, naturally coloured. Pistachio is yellowish not green, strawberry is pinkish not red. Because there's no colouring — the colour comes only from the natural ingredients.

Getting Around

Florence Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Florence is small and the historic centre is walkable. Bus and tram are the second option; taxis are expensive.

✈️

Airport → City

The T2 Vespucci tram takes you from the airport to Santa Maria Novella train station in about 25 minutes. Departures every 5 minutes, ticket €1.70. From February 2025 the line was extended to Piazza San Marco. Fixed-price taxi around €22-25. The tram is easily the most practical option.

🚌

Autolinee Toscane — bus network

City buses and trams run on the same ticket system. Single fare €1.70, valid for 90 minutes, free transfers. Tickets available at tabacchi (tobacconist) shops, machines at bus stops or tram stops. You can't buy a ticket after boarding a bus — buy in advance and validate it when you get on. Contactless bank card payment of €1.70 is also accepted.

🚋

Tram — T1 and T2

Line T1 runs from Scandicci to Santa Maria Novella; line T2 runs from Vespucci Airport via Santa Maria Novella to Piazza San Marco. Both are useful for reaching the city centre. Same ticket as the bus, €1.70.

🚲

Bike and scooter

Florence's historic centre is largely pedestrianised — vehicles are restricted. Bike rental is available but take care on the narrow and busy streets. The route along the Arno is bike-friendly.

🚶

Walking — the best option

From the Duomo: Uffizi 10 minutes, Ponte Vecchio 15 minutes, Santa Croce 15 minutes, Mercato Centrale 10 minutes. The historic centre is almost entirely walkable. To cross to Oltrarno: 5 minutes on foot over Ponte Vecchio.

💡 ZTL — don't drive into the historic centre

Florence's historic centre is a ZTL zone (Zona a Traffico Limitato) — restricted to vehicle traffic. Unauthorised vehicles are detected by automatic cameras and face heavy fines. If you've arrived by rental car, don't enter the historic centre. The best solution is to park outside and walk or use public transport.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Florence: Best Areas

Location matters in Florence — the historic centre is close to everything but expensive; Oltrarno is more local and better value.

Centro Storico — Historic Centre

Most central

The Duomo, Uffizi, Ponte Vecchio, Mercato Centrale all within walking distance. The closest point to everything, but also the busiest and most expensive. Streets are relatively quieter in the early morning and late evening.

📍 Walking distance to everything • Most crowded • Most expensive

Oltrarno

Most local

On the far side of Ponte Vecchio. The Pitti Palace, Boboli Gardens and Bardini Gardens are on this side. Piazza Santo Spirito fills up in the evenings. Workshops, boutique restaurants and neighbourhood bars here. 10-15 minutes on foot to the historic centre. Prices slightly better, atmosphere much more local.

📍 Santo Spirito square • Pitti Palace • Local neighbourhood life

Santa Croce neighbourhood

Most balanced

Around the Santa Croce Basilica. On the eastern side of the historic centre, 15 minutes on foot to the Uffizi. Tourist pressure slightly less than the centre. Sant'Ambrogio Market is in this neighbourhood — where locals do their morning shopping.

📍 Sant'Ambrogio Market • Santa Croce Basilica • Slightly quieter

San Lorenzo neighbourhood

Most market

Around the Mercato Centrale and San Lorenzo Market. The liveliest neighbourhood in the mornings. The Medici Chapels and Laurentian Library are here. Market stalls and local cafés are active in the early part of the day.

📍 Mercato Centrale • San Lorenzo Market • Morning market culture

Near Santa Maria Novella

Close to the train station

Walking distance to Florence's main station, SMN. The airport tram (T2) passes through here. Practical for day trips around Tuscany — Siena, San Gimignano close to the train. Ten minutes on foot to the historic centre.

📍 SMN train station • T2 tram • Practical for day trips
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Florence

Florence can be visited year-round but summer crowds and heat reach their peak in July and August.

Spring

Apr – May
The best time

April and May are Florence's most balanced period. The weather is mild, tourists are around but far from summer levels. The wisteria at Bardini Gardens is in full bloom from mid-April to early May.

Summer

Jun – Aug
Hot and crowded

July and August are the busiest and hottest months — 35-38°C is normal. Museums, bridges and squares are full of tourists. Without reservations you may not get into anything. Before 8am and after 8pm the city is relatively quiet. The evening aperitivo culture and bars along the Arno are at their liveliest this season.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Second best time

September and October see the crowds thin out while the weather stays mild. The grape harvest in the Chianti region begins in September-October. Trattorias open with new seasonal menus: mushrooms, game, chestnuts. Rain increases from November but museums are nearly empty.

Winter

Dec – Feb
Empty museums, quiet city

Christmas markets and lights bring the city to life in December. January and February are Florence's quietest period — museums nearly empty, no Uffizi queue, trattorias accessible without reservations. Cold but snow is rare. Late February brings Carnevale season.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Florence

Florence is home to one of Italy's finest cuisines — but without pretension. Bistecca is expensive, lampredotto is cheap; both are Florentine.

🥩

Bistecca alla Fiorentina

Giant T-bone steak, Chianina beef, cooked over charcoal, red inside. €45-60 per kilo — shared between two people. It has to be 'al sangue' (rare). If you can accept that, this is one of Florence's most iconic experiences.

⭐ €45–60 per kilo · for two · served rare
🥪

Lampredotto sandwich

The fourth stomach of the cow, boiled, sliced and placed into a simple semmel roll. Topped with salsa verde and chilli sauce. Ask for it 'bagnato' — they dip the top of the bread in the cooking broth, and the bread gets soaked — this is the critical difference. €4-5. At stalls in Mercato Centrale or Sant'Ambrogio Market. You'll either love it or not, but you can't leave Florence without trying.

⭐ Mercato Centrale or Sant'Ambrogio · €4–5 · ask for 'bagnato'
🍲

Ribollita

A thick soup of white beans, cavolo nero (black kale), carrot, celery and stale Tuscan bread. The name means 'reboiled' because it's better the next day. A winter dish — October to March. Appears as a daily special at trattorias. Cheap and filling: €8-12.

⭐ Winter months · daily special at trattorias · €8–12
🥪

Schiacciata sandwich

Tuscany's flat olive oil and salt bread (schiacciata), filled with prosciutto, pecorino or grilled vegetables. Florence's lunch-break food: quick, affordable, eaten standing. Fresh from bakeries from the morning onwards.

⭐ Bakeries and delis · lunch break · €4–7
🍨

Real gelato

Metal containers, flat tops, natural colours: pistachio is yellowish-brown, strawberry pale pink. Those are real. Plastic containers, neon colours, high piled mounds — tourist trap. Gelateria dei Neri, Gelateria della Passera (Oltrarno), Bonchi.

⭐ Gelateria dei Neri or Oltrarno options · €1.50–2.50 per scoop
🍷

Aperitivo culture

Between 6pm and 8pm. Order a Negroni or Aperol Spritz — small snacks come alongside. Some bars include a buffet with aperitivo. Oltrarno bars, Piazza Santo Spirito and Via San Niccolò are the right addresses for this ritual.

⭐ 6–8pm · Oltrarno and Santo Spirito · Negroni or Spritz
🍷 Mercato Centrale — best in the early morning

Right next to San Lorenzo, a two-storey iron and glass building. Ground floor is the food market: butcher, fishmonger, cheesemonger, greengrocer — from 7am until early afternoon. Upstairs you can find pasta, pizza, lampredotto, bistecca at various stalls. Touristy but worth experiencing. The ground floor is local and real. Go down to the ground floor in the early morning and enjoy it. Head upstairs when you need a break.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Florence

Florence's quietest and most unexpected corners are usually overshadowed by the famous museums.

✨ An escape from Uffizi crowds — the silence of the Bargello

Bargello Museum

On Via del Proconsolo, 5 minutes' walk from the Uffizi. The most important collection of Renaissance sculpture is here: Donatello's David, Michelangelo's early works, Ghiberti and Verrocchio. A fraction of the Uffizi's visitor numbers. The first-floor courtyard — when afternoon light passes through it — is one of Florence's most beautiful and most silent spaces. Opens at 8:30am and is usually quiet until midday.

✨ Michelangelo's library — and that staircase

Laurentian Library

On the upper floor of San Lorenzo Basilica, with a separate ticket. The entrance vestibule and its dramatic, wavy staircase designed by Michelangelo — one of the earliest examples of Mannerism. Around 11,000 Medici manuscripts are held here. Open Monday-Saturday 10am-1pm.

✨ A better Duomo view than any café — and a library

Caffetteria delle Oblate

On Via dell'Oriuolo, 3 minutes' walk from the Duomo. A small café on the rooftop of the city library. The rear façade of the Duomo and a city panorama in front of you with your coffee or aperitivo. Limited seating — check before you go. The library itself is free to enter — you can sit inside too.

✨ Florence's real evening square

Piazza Santo Spirito

In Oltrarno, 15 minutes' walk from Ponte Vecchio. Quiet during the day — but after 6pm Florentines gather here. Bars and osterias all around, a fountain in the centre, people sitting on the steps. A small market runs every morning except Monday. Sunday brings an organic and craft market.

✨ The closest and least crowded view of Brunelleschi's dome

San Miniato al Monte

Just above Piazzale Michelangelo, 10 more minutes of walking. An 11th-century basilica above the city. A fraction of Piazzale's visitor numbers. The Tuscan marble floor and apse mosaic inside are among Florence's least-known art treasures. Go before sunset: first San Miniato, then down to Piazzale. Both are free.

✨ The bridge over the Arno — before the shops open

Ponte Vecchio — early morning

Between 7:30 and 8:30am, before the jewellery shops open, the bridge is almost empty. At this hour you can stand on it, watch the Arno and see the Vasari Corridor passing above. Note the open gap in the middle of the bridge: left deliberately open for the bust and the view of the Arno.

✨ Sant'Ambrogio Market — alternative to Mercato Centrale

In the Santa Croce neighbourhood, at Piazza Sant'Ambrogio. Smaller, less touristy, more local. Fresh vegetables, fruit, cheese, meat. Small trattoria stalls inside — lampredotto or daily special for lunch. Open 7am-2pm, closed Sundays. Much less crowded than Mercato Centrale and much more genuine.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Florence is small and tourist-dense; its traps are just as frequent. Know these four and tour the Renaissance in peace.

🎟️ TRAP

Street sellers of 'no-queue Uffizi'

The 'skip-the-line' hawkers around the museum charge multiples of the official ticket; some tickets turn out invalid at the door.

💡 What to Do Instead: Buy only from the official site, dated and timed; the calmest hours are the first slot and two hours before closing.
👜 TRAP

'Real leather' on the San Lorenzo stalls

Not everything labelled 'vera pelle' is genuine; glued soles and synthetic leather abound on the tourist stalls.

💡 What to Do Instead: No flame test needed: check the stitching and the smell, and buy from workshops like the Scuola del Cuoio or long-established shops.
🥩 TRAP

'Fixed-price bistecca' on a photo menu

Real bistecca alla fiorentina is sold by the etto (100 g) and served rare; the promise of bistecca 'per plate' is a thinned-out disappointment.

💡 What to Do Instead: Go where the price is quoted per etto and accept it rare; sharing one between two is the tradition itself.
🚌 TRAP

Reaching Piazzale Michelangelo at bus o'clock

Half an hour before sunset the terrace fills with coach convoys; the view is lovely, the experience a scrum.

💡 What to Do Instead: Climb ten minutes more: the steps of San Miniato al Monte give the same view plus monks' chanting — and room to breathe.
🛡️ The golden rule

Cross the river in Florence: in the Oltrarno prices drop, craft is real, and Florentines sit at the tables. The tourist centre is the shop window; the city is in the workshop.
