Madrid

Europe's city that never sleeps — being out at 2am here needs no explanation

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Madrid

Reset your clock before you land. Lunch starts at 2pm, dinner before 9:30pm is almost unheard of, and at 2am the streets are still full. This isn't an exaggeration — it's Madrid's actual rhythm. See the Prado, visit Reina Sofía, tour the Royal Palace — but if you're already planning what to eat after dinner, you're thinking like a Madrileño. The metro costs less than a café menu. The sun sets late. Tapas are eaten standing, talking, unhurried.

🗣️ Local Concept: Vermuteo (ber-moo-TAY-o)

The ritual of drinking vermouth before lunch. Between noon and 2pm, bars pour vermouth on tap, with crisps, olives, and a slice of tortilla on the side. Sunday after El Rastro, or weekday lunch breaks. 'Shall we do vermuteo?' is one of the most common sentences in a Madrileño's vocabulary.

3 Unwritten Rules for Madrid

1

Get into Madrid's rhythm — or the city closes on you

Breakfast is light and early. Lunch is between 2pm and 4pm — the most important meal of the day and the cheapest option: a full set menu of starter, main and dessert for 12-15 euros, usually with a drink included. Don't start dinner before 9pm. At 11pm tables are still filling up. Follow these hours and you eat cheaply in local places.

2

Avoid tourist traps — walk two blocks

The restaurants around Plaza Mayor, most of Gran Vía and the Mercado de San Miguel are touristy and overpriced. Two blocks away, prices drop and quality rises. If the menu has photos, someone's standing outside inviting you in, or the English menu is very prominent — keep walking.

3

Understand tapas culture

In Madrid, tapas don't come free with drinks — some bars put something small out but it's not the rule. Eat standing at the bar, visit a few bars, have something to eat and drink at each one. Small, light, shared: croquetas, patatas bravas, boquerones, a slice of tortilla.

Getting Around

Madrid Public Transit & Airport Transfers

The metro goes everywhere but the city centre is small enough to walk. Taxis are possible but not necessary.

✈️

Airport → City

Metro Line 8 to Nuevos Ministerios, 25 minutes, then transfer to the city centre. The ticket is the standard metro fare plus a €3 airport supplement — around €5 total. Fixed-price taxi from the airport: €33. Terminals T1-T2-T3 are connected; T4 is separate, 10 minutes by metro.

🚇

Metro — the city's backbone

13 lines, over 300 stations. Single ticket €1.50–2, depending on distance. The 10-trip ticket (MetroBus) is €7.30 — valid on both metro and bus, and can be shared between people (each person taps separately). You need to buy a Multi Card at a station machine first (€2.50). Runs 6am to 1:30am.

🚌

EMT buses — for late nights

Over 200 daytime routes, 35 night routes (Búho). Daytime runs 6am–11:30pm. Night buses depart from Cibeles — the only option once the metro closes. Pay by card directly or with the 10-trip ticket.

🚲

BiciMAD — electric bikes

Electric bike sharing stations across the city. €0.50 per 30 minutes or a monthly flat rate of €10. Ideal routes along Paseo del Prado, Retiro and Madrid Río.

🚶

Walking — the best option

The city centre is smaller than you think. From Sol: La Latina 15 minutes, Malasaña 20 minutes, Chueca 10 minutes. Safe at night. You can spend a full day without needing the metro.

💡 Tourist Pass or 10-trip ticket?

For a few days, the 10-trip MetroBus ticket (€7.30) is usually better value — valid on both metro and bus, and shareable between travellers. The Tourist Pass is €10 per day for Zone A, €42 for 7 days — unlimited rides including the airport with no supplement. If you're staying more than 3 days and plan to move around a lot, the Tourist Pass is worth considering.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Madrid: Best Areas

Location matters a lot in Madrid — the city lives at night and the neighbourhood's character shapes your evenings directly.

La Latina / Lavapiés

Most historic, most alive

La Latina is one of Madrid's oldest neighbourhoods. Cava Baja is lined with tapas bars and historic taverns. Plaza de la Paja is a quiet medieval square. El Rastro passes through on Sundays. Next door, Lavapiés is multicultural, artistic and constantly changing — the Mercado de San Fernando is here.

📍 El Rastro Sunday • Cava Baja tapas • Historic streets

Malasaña

Most alternative

The Movida Madrileña movement of the 1980s was born here. It still carries that spirit: street art, vintage shops, small bars and cafés. Plaza del Dos de Mayo fills up in the evening. Busy but authentic. Ten minutes' walk from Gran Vía but feels like another world.

📍 Street art • Vintage shops • Nightlife

Chueca

Most lively

Colourful, modern, with a strong nightlife scene. The Mercado de San Antón is here — three floors, with a rooftop bar at the top. Bodega de la Ardosa has been pouring vermouth straight from the barrel since 1892. Shares a border with Malasaña — five minutes between them.

📍 Mercado de San Antón • Bodega de la Ardosa • Nightlife

Chamberí

Most local, most quiet

The neighbourhood Madrid residents choose when they want to stay in Madrid. Families having morning coffee around Plaza de Olavide, elderly couples sharing newspapers. No tourist pressure. Andén 0 — the ghost metro station — is here. 15-20 minutes' walk to the city centre.

📍 Plaza de Olavide • Andén 0 • Quiet neighbourhood rhythm

Barrio de las Letras

Most cultural

Between the Prado and Puerta del Sol, where Lope de Vega and Cervantes lived. Quotes from writers are set into the pavement of Calle de las Huertas — look down as you walk. The Thyssen and Reina Sofía are within walking distance. It quietens early in the morning — that's when it's at its best.

📍 Calle de las Huertas quotes • Close to museums • Literary history
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Madrid

Madrid has a continental climate — summers are very hot, winters cold, spring and autumn are excellent.

Spring

Apr – Jun
The best time

April to June is Madrid's most balanced period. There's sun but the temperature is right for exploring. The rose garden Rosaleda in Retiro Park is in bloom in May. Fiestas de San Isidro in May — Madrid's biggest local festival, street musicians, traditional costumes, open-air concerts. Almond blossom at Quinta de los Molinos in late March.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Breathing after summer

From mid-September to November Madrid comes back to life. Museums open new exhibitions, restaurants launch new menus. Retiro Park turns golden in autumn — weekday mornings are its finest hours. Crowds are lower than summer.

Summer

Jul – Aug
Hot but the city never sleeps

July and August are Madrid's hottest months — 40°C is normal. Between 2pm and 7pm being outside is tough. But nightlife peaks. Veranos de la Villa: open-air concerts, film screenings and cultural events all summer. Picnics by the lake in Casa de Campo. Rooftop bars are at their liveliest this season.

Winter

Dec – Feb
Quiet but easy on the wallet

December: Navidad lights cover Gran Vía, and the Reyes Magos parade on the evening of 5 January. January and February are quiet — museums empty, restaurants without queues, hotels affordable. Cocido madrileño — the chickpea winter stew — is the dish of these months. Cold but snow is rare.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Madrid

Madrid is far from the sea but seafood is among the city's best. And everything here is eaten standing, at the bar, unhurried.

🦑

Bocadillo de calamares

Fried calamari in a bread roll, a squeeze of lemon — that's it. Bar La Campana is right behind Plaza Mayor, open since 1979. There's a queue but it moves fast. A beer alongside, eaten standing in Plaza Mayor. Eat this before you think about where else to go in Madrid.

⭐ Bar La Campana, behind Plaza Mayor · eat standing · every day
🍳

Huevos rotos

Fried eggs over crispy potatoes, with jamón or chorizo. The egg is broken so the yolk spreads over the potatoes — that's where the name comes from. Specific to Madrid. Casa Lucio is famous but crowded; you'll find the same dish at smaller bars in La Latina.

⭐ La Latina bars · lunch and dinner · best shared
🥘

Cocido madrileño

A stew of chickpeas, pork, chicken, morcilla and vegetables — served in three stages. First the chickpea broth, then the vegetables, then the meats. A winter dish, November to March. Taberna La Bola has been making it since 1870. Advance reservation essential.

⭐ Winter · lunch · Taberna La Bola since 1870 · reservation required
🍟

Patatas bravas

Crispy potatoes with two sauces — one spicy (brava), one garlicky aioli. In the Madrid version, the sauce is poured over the potatoes. Every tapas bar has its own recipe. Docamar on Alcalá is the city's most famous patatas bravas bar.

⭐ Every tapas bar · Docamar is the most famous
🍩

Churros con chocolate

At the end of a night out at 2am, or Sunday morning with bleary eyes. Chocolatería San Ginés has been open since 1894, right next to Puerta del Sol. Churros straight from the hot oil, chocolate thick and dark. Sitting here late at night is part of the Madrid experience.

⭐ Chocolatería San Ginés · since 1894 · next to Puerta del Sol · open until 2am
🍶

Vermut ritual

Sunday after El Rastro or weekday lunch breaks. Vermouth on tap, olives alongside. Bodega de la Ardosa in Chueca has been serving vermouth straight from the barrel since 1892. Casa Alberto in Barrio de las Letras since 1827. This isn't just a drink — it's a ritual of pausing.

⭐ Sundays after El Rastro or weekday lunch · Bodega de la Ardosa or Casa Alberto
🍷 How to eat at a tapas bar

Go in, find a spot at the bar, say what you want. Eating standing is better than at a table. Visit a few bars: croquetas at one, patatas bravas at the next, bocadillo after that. This is the right way to do tapas in Madrid. Wait a while before asking for the bill — there's no rush here.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Madrid

Madrid is a big city but its best corners appear as soon as you walk two blocks and turn.

✨ There's another metro hidden under the metro

Andén 0 — Ghost Station

Under Puerta del Sol metro station, an unused station dating from 1919. Converted into a museum in 2021. Original infrastructure from the construction of the metro preserved — old signs and vehicles. Ticketed; tours at specific times. The starting point of Spain's first metro line.

✨ Poetry set into the pavement — the literary quarter

Calle de las Huertas

In Barrio de las Letras, between Puerta del Sol and the Prado. Quotes from Cervantes, Lope de Vega and other Golden Age writers are set into the pavement of the street where they lived — look down as you walk. Before 10am, before tour groups arrive, there's no crowd. Lope de Vega's house is open as a museum.

✨ The city's most local hill for sunset

Cerro del Tío Pío — Siete Tetas

In the Vallecas neighbourhood, walking distance from Line 1 Buenos Aires stop. Seven small hills — known locally as 'Siete Tetas'. One of the least crowded spots in the city for Madrid's skyline and sunset. Bring a picnic blanket and food; sit with the local crowd in the late afternoon.

✨ Open since 1827 — on the same spot

Casa Alberto

In Barrio de las Letras, Calle de las Huertas 18. Open since 1827. Wooden bar, vermouth from the barrel, old Madrid photographs on the walls. Drink the vermouth on tap, order caracoles (snails) alongside — in season.

✨ Almond blossom in March — Madrid's most invisible festival

Quinta de los Molinos

On Alcalá, near the Ventas bullring. 1,500 almond trees — in late March they all blossom at once, ground and air turning white. Less known than Japanese cherry blossom but equally striking. Free, park always open. Go on a Sunday morning — neighbours walk here.

✨ Look out over the city from above Gran Vía

Círculo de Bellas Artes Rooftop

Alcalá 42, right at the corner of Gran Vía. Pay €5 and go up to the rooftop — Gran Vía, the Royal Palace, the Sierra de Guadarrama mountains visible 360 degrees. There's a bar. Inside there are art exhibitions too, often free. Most beautiful at sunset.

✨ El Rastro — the Sunday morning ritual

Every Sunday 9am–3pm in La Latina. Calle de la Ribera de Curtidores is the main street — second-hand goods, antiques, clothing, books, everything. El Rastro on Sunday, then vermuteo at lunch in La Latina — the standard Madrileño Sunday plan. Watch your pockets, it gets crowded. Best selection in the early morning hours.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Madrid is a generous city; its traps are squeezed into its three most touristic squares. Know these four — the rest is safe ground.

🍤 TRAP

Sitting down on Plaza Mayor

The square's terraces sell the historic view, not the kitchen: frozen calamares, double-priced sangria.

💡 What to Do Instead: Walk the square, take your photo; eat your bocadillo de calamares standing at the classic bars just outside it.
🥘 TRAP

Mistaking Mercado San Miguel for a 'real market'

The glass market is beautiful, but it's now a tourist showcase: small portions, big bills.

💡 What to Do Instead: For the real market experience go to Mercado de la Cebada or Antón Martín; same flavours, half the price, full of locals.
🎩 TRAP

Shell games and petitions at Sol

The cup game, the 'deaf association petition' and the rosemary-sprig fortune tellers around Puerta del Sol are the same crew in different costumes.

💡 What to Do Instead: Don't stop, smile, walk on; bag in front, phone in pocket. Even watching serves the crew's crowd trick.
💃 TRAP

Bus-tour flamenco 'galas'

The giant dinner-hall shows don't carry flamenco's soul; Madrid's tablao tradition is smaller and truer.

💡 What to Do Instead: Pick an established tablao, eat elsewhere, watch the show with a drink: cheaper and many times more moving.
🛡️ The golden rule

Madrid's rule is simple: walk two blocks from any tourist square and the price halves while the flavour doubles. The city is already telling you this; listen.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Madrid

Madrid is patient with English, but five Spanish words open the doors wide.

🗣️ Buenas
The short all-day greeting — entering any bar or shop
🗣️ Gracias
Thank you — short, clear, always
🗣️ Por favor
Please — add it to the end of your order
🗣️ ¡Salud!
Cheers! — vermut glasses rise to this
🗣️ Vale
OK / deal — Spain's most used word; said twice: vale, vale
