Madrid

Europe's city that never sleeps — being out at 2am here needs no explanation

Overview

The Vibe of Madrid

Reset your clock before you land. Lunch starts at 2pm, dinner before 9:30pm is almost unheard of, and at 2am the streets are still full. This isn't an exaggeration — it's Madrid's actual rhythm. See the Prado, visit Reina Sofía, tour the Royal Palace — but if you're already planning what to eat after dinner, you're thinking like a Madrileño. The metro costs less than a café menu. The sun sets late. Tapas are eaten standing, talking, unhurried.

💬 Vermuteo
🗣️ /ber-moo-TAY-o/

The ritual of drinking vermouth before lunch. Between noon and 2pm, bars pour vermouth on tap, with crisps, olives, and a slice of tortilla on the side. Sunday after El Rastro, or weekday lunch breaks. 'Shall we do vermuteo?' is one of the most common sentences in a Madrileño's vocabulary.

Insider Advice

Unwritten Rules for Madrid

#1
Get into Madrid's rhythm — or the city closes on you Breakfast is light and early. Lunch is between 2pm and 4pm — the most important meal of the day and the cheapest option: a full set menu of starter, main and dessert for 12-15 euros, usually with a drink included. Don't start dinner before 9pm. At 11pm tables are still filling up. Follow these hours and you eat cheaply in local places.
#2
Avoid tourist traps — walk two blocks The restaurants around Plaza Mayor, most of Gran Vía and the Mercado de San Miguel are touristy and overpriced. Two blocks away, prices drop and quality rises. If the menu has photos, someone's standing outside inviting you in, or the English menu is very prominent — keep walking.
#3
Understand tapas culture In Madrid, tapas don't come free with drinks — some bars put something small out but it's not the rule. Eat standing at the bar, visit a few bars, have something to eat and drink at each one. Small, light, shared: croquetas, patatas bravas, boquerones, a slice of tortilla.
Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Madrid

💎 HIDDEN GEM
Andén 0 — Ghost Station
Under Puerta del Sol metro station, an unused station dating from 1919. Converted into a museum in 2021. Original infrastructure from the construction of the metro preserved — old signs and vehicles. Ticketed; tours at specific times. The starting point of Spain's first metro line.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Calle de las Huertas
In Barrio de las Letras, between Puerta del Sol and the Prado. Quotes from Cervantes, Lope de Vega and other Golden Age writers are set into the pavement of the street where they lived — look down as you walk. Before 10am, before tour groups arrive, there's no crowd. Lope de Vega's house is open as a museum.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Cerro del Tío Pío — Siete Tetas
In the Vallecas neighbourhood, walking distance from Line 1 Buenos Aires stop. Seven small hills — known locally as 'Siete Tetas'. One of the least crowded spots in the city for Madrid's skyline and sunset. Bring a picnic blanket and food; sit with the local crowd in the late afternoon.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Casa Alberto
In Barrio de las Letras, Calle de las Huertas 18. Open since 1827. Wooden bar, vermouth from the barrel, old Madrid photographs on the walls. Drink the vermouth on tap, order caracoles (snails) alongside — in season.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Quinta de los Molinos
On Alcalá, near the Ventas bullring. 1,500 almond trees — in late March they all blossom at once, ground and air turning white. Less known than Japanese cherry blossom but equally striking. Free, park always open. Go on a Sunday morning — neighbours walk here.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Círculo de Bellas Artes Rooftop
Alcalá 42, right at the corner of Gran Vía. Pay €5 and go up to the rooftop — Gran Vía, the Royal Palace, the Sierra de Guadarrama mountains visible 360 degrees. There's a bar. Inside there are art exhibitions too, often free. Most beautiful at sunset.
Food & Flavors

What to Taste

Madrid is far from the sea but seafood is among the city's best. And everything here is eaten standing, at the bar, unhurried.

🍴 MUST TASTE
Bocadillo de calamares
Fried calamari in a bread roll, a squeeze of lemon — that's it. Bar La Campana is right behind Plaza Mayor, open since 1979. There's a queue but it moves fast. A beer alongside, eaten standing in Plaza Mayor. Eat this before you think about where else to go in Madrid.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Huevos rotos
Fried eggs over crispy potatoes, with jamón or chorizo. The egg is broken so the yolk spreads over the potatoes — that's where the name comes from. Specific to Madrid. Casa Lucio is famous but crowded; you'll find the same dish at smaller bars in La Latina.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Cocido madrileño
A stew of chickpeas, pork, chicken, morcilla and vegetables — served in three stages. First the chickpea broth, then the vegetables, then the meats. A winter dish, November to March. Taberna La Bola has been making it since 1870. Advance reservation essential.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Patatas bravas
Crispy potatoes with two sauces — one spicy (brava), one garlicky aioli. In the Madrid version, the sauce is poured over the potatoes. Every tapas bar has its own recipe. Docamar on Alcalá is the city's most famous patatas bravas bar.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Churros con chocolate
At the end of a night out at 2am, or Sunday morning with bleary eyes. Chocolatería San Ginés has been open since 1894, right next to Puerta del Sol. Churros straight from the hot oil, chocolate thick and dark. Sitting here late at night is part of the Madrid experience.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Vermut ritual
Sunday after El Rastro or weekday lunch breaks. Vermouth on tap, olives alongside. Bodega de la Ardosa in Chueca has been serving vermouth straight from the barrel since 1892. Casa Alberto in Barrio de las Letras since 1827. This isn't just a drink — it's a ritual of pausing.
Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

❌
The square's terraces sell the historic view, not the kitchen: frozen calamares, double-priced sangria.
✅ Better Alternative: Walk the square, take your photo; eat your bocadillo de calamares standing at the classic bars just outside it.
❌
The glass market is beautiful, but it's now a tourist showcase: small portions, big bills.
✅ Better Alternative: For the real market experience go to Mercado de la Cebada or Antón Martín; same flavours, half the price, full of locals.
❌
The cup game, the 'deaf association petition' and the rosemary-sprig fortune tellers around Puerta del Sol are the same crew in different costumes.
✅ Better Alternative: Don't stop, smile, walk on; bag in front, phone in pocket. Even watching serves the crew's crowd trick.
❌
The giant dinner-hall shows don't carry flamenco's soul; Madrid's tablao tradition is smaller and truer.
✅ Better Alternative: Pick an established tablao, eat elsewhere, watch the show with a drink: cheaper and many times more moving.
