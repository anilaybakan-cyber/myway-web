Seville

The most Spanish city in Spain — and the one that earned it

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Seville

Seville is the city that invented late nights and hosts the Holy Week procession that stops the entire country for a week. In July, the hottest city in Europe. In April, during Semana Santa and Feria de Abril, it transforms into something that has to be witnessed to be understood. The Alcázar is one of the most beautiful palaces ever built. The Cathedral is the largest Gothic structure in the world. But that is not the point. The point is the bar on Calle Gerona — where the bill is chalked on the wood and the same dish has been served since the Moors.

🗣️ Local Concept: Tapas (TAH-pahs)

In Seville, the tapas culture is specific: a small plate of food, ordered at the bar, eaten standing or perched on a high stool, accompanied by a caña (small beer) or a glass of fino sherry. The ritual is to move between bars — one drink and one tapa per bar, then on to the next. One drink, one plate, three or four bars, two streets, two hours. A Tuesday evening in Seville looks like this. No table, no reservation — just the bar counter and an evening passing by.

3 Unwritten Rules for Seville

1

Book the Alcázar and the Cathedral weeks ahead

The Alcázar and Cathedral both run timed-entry systems that fill weeks ahead in April and May. No walk-ins. Book online before you arrive — the Alcázar especially. Its Mudejar interior makes a strong case for most beautiful palace in Spain that isn't the Alhambra.

2

Lunch at 14:00, dinner at 21:00 — the city means it

Kitchens open at 14:00. They close at 16:00. Not as a suggestion. Dinner starts at 21:00 — that's the opening time, not the peak. The table next to you at 22:30 will have three generations on it, including a child who should be asleep. Between 17:00 and 20:30: slow walk, café stop, glass of manzanilla on a terrace. That's the hinge of the day.

3

In summer, the city moves at night

July and August: regularly 42°C. The city doesn't fight it — it rearranges around it. Empty streets at 3pm. Full streets at 11pm. Out by 9am, inside by 13:00, back out at 19:00, stay out until the city forces you home. Arrive expecting summer logic from home and Seville will be miserable. Follow the local pattern and it's extraordinary.

Getting Around

Seville Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Seville's historic centre is compact and flat. Most things are walkable. The bike network is the best in Spain. Use it.

EA airport bus to Torre del Oro (riverside): every 30 minutes, 35 minutes, pay onboard. No train link. Taxis are metered. That's it.

MetroCentro tram runs Plaza Nueva → Cathedral → Archivo de Indias → Nervión. €1.40 — machines at stops or tap contactless on the validator. Tarjeta Turista: 1-day or 3-day unlimited tram and bus. Runs until midnight weekdays, 2am Fri–Sat.

44 TUSSAM routes, red-and-yellow, cover everywhere the tram doesn't. €1.40, contactless works. C5 loops the historic centre. Night buses run until 5:30am at weekends — relevant when dinner starts at 21:30.

180km+ of separated cycle lanes. Completely flat. SEVIci bikes: stations every few hundred metres, 7-day subscription for unlimited 30-minute trips. Alcázar to Triana bridge along the river — under 20 minutes. For anything under 3km: the bike is faster than anything else.

Cathedral to Casa de Pilatos: 10 minutes. Triana bridge to Alcázar: 15 minutes. Alameda de Hércules to Plaza de España: 20 minutes. The patios, the orange trees, the Mudéjar tilework on ordinary corners — all between the monuments, not at them. Slow down.

⚡ Pro Tip

The Guadalquivir path south from Torre del Oro: city skyline on the left, river and herons on the right. An hour before sunset the stone turns a colour between copper and gold — specific to this latitude, this river, this hour. SEVIci bike: Torre del Oro to Triana bridge in 20 minutes. On foot: an hour. Both correct. Free at all hours.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Seville

The Cathedral queue is one of the longest in Spain. Twenty metres away, through a passage, is a 16th-century arcaded square that almost no one enters. This is how Seville works.

Casa de Pilatos — Santa Cruz

The Medinaceli family measured the distance from their door to a local chapel and found it matched Pontius Pilate's house to Calvary. Named accordingly. Mudejar tilework, Gothic vaulting, Renaissance sculpture — shouldn't cohere, entirely does. Lawrence of Arabia filmed here. The Crown filmed here. The Alcázar gets 90% of the visitors. Ticketed. Free Mondays 15:00–19:00 — queue at the door.

Archivo General de Indias — Cathedral Square

Between the Cathedral and the Alcázar. Not a church, not a palace — a Renaissance archive with 80 million pages on Spain's colonisation of the Americas. Columbus's letters: behind glass, in his handwriting. Designed by Juan de Herrera (El Escorial's architect). The visitors in the Cathedral queue next door largely don't know it exists. Free Tuesday to Sunday.

La Carbonería — Santa Cruz

No sign. Wooden door on Calle Levíes. Looks like a private courtyard — because it is. Inside: former coal yard, mismatched chairs, exposed brick, free flamenco every evening. Audience: mostly local. Performers: not on the tourist-tablao circuit. Informal — guitarist finishes, someone calls for another song, they agree. No ticket. No reservation. Arrive, order, sit, pay attention.

Palacio de las Dueñas — La Macarena

The Duchess of Alba lived here until 2014. 15th century, Mudéjar-Renaissance — orange-tree courtyards, jasmine-covered arches. Antonio Machado born here in 1875. Rooms hold the personal collections of the most decorated family in Spanish history: Goyas, Rubens, Roman sculpture. Ticketed. Free Mondays from 16:00 — book online same morning.

Plaza del Cabildo — Cathedral Square

South side of Cathedral square. Looks like a service entrance. Walk through — noise stops. 1572. A perfect semicircle of arches, designed for commerce, now a shortcut no one uses. Sunday mornings: a few stalls selling old coins and stamps. Otherwise: empty, quiet, surrounded by the highest tourist density in Andalusia. Seville in miniature. Free.

Metropol Parasol (Las Setas) — Plaza de la Encarnación

Six mushroom-shaped laminated timber parasols, 28 metres up, dividing Seville since 2011. Locals call it Las Setas. Most have made peace with it. Rooftop walkway: Giralda, Alcázar gardens, Guadalquivir, the white barrios south. Go an hour before sunset — the light on the Cathedral from up there. Rooftop ticketed. Ground floor market and Antiquarium Roman ruins: free.
