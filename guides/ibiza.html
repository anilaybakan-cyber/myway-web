Ibiza

The White Isle: turquoise coves, hippie markets and sunset rituals

Overview

The Vibe of Ibiza

Ibiza's fame was written by its nightclubs, but the island's real story is older: the Phoenicians built a port, ramparts rose against pirates, the hippies of the sixties settled the pine-fringed coves — and all of them left their mark on this white island. Today history lives in Dalt Vila's stone lanes, silence in the northern coves, a sunset rite opposite Es Vedrà, and at night the world's most famous dancefloors. There are two Ibizas — in what dose you mix them is entirely up to you.

🗣️ Local Concept: Eivissa (ey-vee-sa)

The island's Catalan and official name: the road signs say 'Eivissa', not 'Ibiza'. A local introduces themselves as 'eivissenc' — using this name shows you entered the island through its door, not its brochure.

Insider Advice

3 Unwritten Rules for Ibiza

1

Get to know both islands

South means night, north means silence: San Antonio and Playa d'en Bossa are party, the north around Portinatx and Benirràs is pine and cove. Whoever settles for one face has missed half the island.

2

Never buy the club ticket at the door

The big clubs' door prices are punishing: tickets are clearly cheaper online and in advance sales, and most hotels and bars pre-sell. As for the dream of two clubs in one night — that's budget and energy suicide.

3

Coves early, sunset in position

The famous calas fill by noon and the car parks overflow: 10 am is a cove's finest hour. For sunset it's the place, not the time, that matters — the cliffs opposite Es Vedrà or the west coast rocks need a plan.
