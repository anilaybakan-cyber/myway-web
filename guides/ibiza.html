Ibiza

The White Isle: turquoise coves, hippie markets and sunset rituals

Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Ibiza

Ibiza's fame was written by its nightclubs, but the island's real story is older: the Phoenicians built a port, ramparts rose against pirates, the hippies of the sixties settled the pine-fringed coves — and all of them left their mark on this white island. Today history lives in Dalt Vila's stone lanes, silence in the northern coves, a sunset rite opposite Es Vedrà, and at night the world's most famous dancefloors. There are two Ibizas — in what dose you mix them is entirely up to you.

🗣️ Local Concept: Eivissa (ey-vee-sa)

The island's Catalan and official name: the road signs say 'Eivissa', not 'Ibiza'. A local introduces themselves as 'eivissenc' — using this name shows you entered the island through its door, not its brochure.

3 Unwritten Rules for Ibiza

1

Get to know both islands

South means night, north means silence: San Antonio and Playa d'en Bossa are party, the north around Portinatx and Benirràs is pine and cove. Whoever settles for one face has missed half the island.

2

Never buy the club ticket at the door

The big clubs' door prices are punishing: tickets are clearly cheaper online and in advance sales, and most hotels and bars pre-sell. As for the dream of two clubs in one night — that's budget and energy suicide.

3

Coves early, sunset in position

The famous calas fill by noon and the car parks overflow: 10 am is a cove's finest hour. For sunset it's the place, not the time, that matters — the cliffs opposite Es Vedrà or the west coast rocks need a plan.

Getting Around

Ibiza Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Ibiza looks small but its coves are scattered: freedom is a rental car, practicality the main bus lines, pleasure a boat.

✈️

Airport to town

The airport is 10 minutes from Ibiza Town: bus 10 runs frequently in season, and taxis stay near a flat rate. Crossing to the San Antonio side takes half an hour.

🚗

A rental car — the key to the coves

Most hidden coves are reached down dirt tracks; a small car is the island's key. Book early in summer and reach beach car parks in the morning — by noon a space is down to luck.

🚌

The main bus lines

The Ibiza-San Antonio-Santa Eulària triangle and the airport line run regularly; at night the Disco Bus links the clubs. Services to the northern coves are sparse — check times beforehand.

⛵

Boats and ferries

The Formentera ferry leaves the port every half hour; coastal boats work like taxis on the Cala Comte-Cala Bassa line. The sunset cruise past Es Vedrà is the island's classic gesture.

🛵

Scooters and bikes

A scooter is practical for short hops and the harbour area; the inland roads are hilly and hot in summer — the bicycle is an early-morning vehicle.

💡 Formentera tip

Cross to Formentera on the first ferry, rent a bike at the quay and slip into Ses Illetes' Caribbean water before the crowds: sunburn and the regret of 'why didn't we stay a night' are standard on the ferry back.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Ibiza: Best Areas

In Ibiza the choice of area decides the kind of holiday: harbour and rampart shade, party beach, family cove or the pine silence of the north.

Ibiza Town (Eivissa) and Dalt Vila

Most atmospheric

Stone lanes inside the walls, the evening paseo along the harbour, marina bars: the centre of the history + nightlife balance. Lively and expensive in summer — the showcase of island life.

📍 Lanes in the walls • Harbour evenings • Access to the clubs

Santa Eulària des Riu

Most family friendly

A calm promenade, sandy beaches and restaurant variety: far from the party, firmly on comfort's side. The classic base for families and quiet holidaymakers.

📍 Calm promenade • Family beaches • Restaurant choice

San Antonio (Sant Antoni)

Party + sunset

The shore of the Sunset Strip and the young party crowd: the sunset bars are legend, night noise guaranteed. With budget hotels and the boat-trip harbour, the address of youth.

📍 Sunset Strip • Young energy • Budget hotels

The north — Portinatx and Sant Joan

Calmest

Pine forests, hidden coves and villages with a hippie inheritance: the island's slow face. A car is essential; the reward is silence and a starry sky.

📍 Hidden coves • Pine forest • Car needed

Inland — Santa Gertrudis and the fincas

Most characterful

White village squares and agroturismo fincas: equal distance to every cove, long tables on the village square at night. For those who want Ibiza as an island village.

📍 Agroturismo • Village square • Central position
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Ibiza

Read Ibiza's calendar not by the club season but by light and crowds: June and September are gold, July-August the peak, winter another island entirely.

Spring

Apr – Jun
Opening Season

The island turns its liveliest green with wildflowers, the sea becomes swimmable in May, and late May-June opens the season's gate with the clubs' opening parties. The coves are calm, prices still reasonable.

Summer

Jul – Aug
Peak Season

The world's DJ calendar moves to the island, the coves and roads fill, prices hit the ceiling. The energy is unmatched, but nobody should come unplanned: club ticket, restaurant table and car — all three in advance.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Closings and a Warm Sea

September is the island's best secret: the sea at its warmest, the crowds receded, and in early October the closing parties play the finale. With November the island returns to its locals and the hiking season opens.

Winter

Dec – Mar
The Locals' Island

The clubs sleep and the island breathes: in January and February the almond trees turn white, and the coastal paths belong to walkers. Many places close, but Dalt Vila and the village markets live on in their true form.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Ibiza

The island's cooking comes out of fishermen's pots: saffron fish stews, village-style meats and century-old liqueur recipes — the real Ibiza behind the beach club menus.

🐟

Bullit de peix — fish in two acts

First the saffron-poached fish and potatoes, then in act two the rice of that same broth, arroz a banda: the island's greatest gastronomic pride. In family restaurants of the fishing coves — with a booking if possible.

⭐ At a fishing-cove restaurant · two servings
🥘

Sofrit pagès — the village pot

The village classic where chicken, lamb, sausage and potatoes meet over slow fire: the heart of the winter table, on menus in summer too. Filling and honest — the signature of the agroturismo kitchens.

⭐ Inland · village style
🍮

Flaó and greixonera

Flaó: the fresh-cheese-and-mint tart, Ibiza's unchanged dessert of centuries. Greixonera is a cinnamon pudding made from yesterday's ensaimada — a masterpiece of the island's waste-nothing mind.

⭐ The dessert duo · the minted flaó is a must
🌿

Hierbas ibicencas

The anise liqueur steeped with dozens of island herbs — pine, fennel and mint among them: served over ice after dinner. Every family has its bottle and every restaurant its recipe — a classic gift, too.

⭐ After dinner · over ice
🥗

Ensalada payesa and peix sec

The village salad, Ibiza-style: potatoes, red peppers and sun-dried fish (peix sec). Salty, fresh, the lightest victory of a beach noon.

⭐ Beach lunch · with peix sec
🍹

The chiringuito sunset table

Grilled fish, sangria and feet in the sand at the beach bars: a simple menu, a magnificent stage. At the west coast chiringuitos, claim the table an hour before sunset.

⭐ On the west coast · before sunset
🍷 The booking rule

In summer a good fish restaurant is harder to get than a beach club: bullit de peix needs pre-ordering in most places. The 'authentic Ibiza' you're after is not at the beach club but in the family pot at the cove's end — call a day ahead.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Ibiza

Everyone knows the famous coves; these six are the island's concealed face.

✨ The island's mystic rock

The Es Vedrà lookouts

The uninhabited rock rising 380 metres from the sea is the centre of all the island's legends: sirens, magnetic fields, the home of Tanit. Watch at sunset from the cliffs above Cala d'Hort or the Savinar tower — the crowd on the beach, you up above.

✨ The drumming sunset cove

Benirràs

The north's pine-belted cove sees the sun off with a drum circle on Sunday evenings: the place where the hippie legacy still breathes. Go early — the cove is small, the ritual famous.

✨ An island market within the island

Las Dalias and the hippie markets

The legendary market at Sant Carles has been set up since 1954: handmade jewellery, the island's last true hippies and live music. Saturday is the main day — go in the morning, leave before the midday crowd.

✨ The salt flats' pink hour

The Ses Salines salt pans

The salt pools worked since the Phoenicians turn pink at sunset; flamingos wade the shallows. The beach's fame overshadows the pans' quiet beauty — tour them by bicycle.

✨ A pirate watchtower

Torre des Savinar (Torre del Pirata)

The 18th-century watchtower directly opposite Es Vedrà: a short, steep path leads up, and the reward is the island's most dramatic view. Be at the top an hour before sunset.

✨ The white village square

A Santa Gertrudis evening

The white village of the interior: tapas bars, galleries and tables under the trees around the square. An evening's break from the coastal noise — village Ibiza with a plate of jamón and a cold white.

✨ Cove tip

Every cove written large on the map has a 'little neighbour': if Cala Comte is full walk to Cala Escondida, if Cala Bassa overflows walk to Cala Gracioneta. A five-minute path means half the crowd.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

In summer Ibiza can be one of the world's most expensive islands per tourist; these four are the budget's main insurance.

🎟️ TRAP

Buying the club ticket at the door

The door price sits well above the advance sale; inside, even water is serious money, and the 'free entry' flyers usually hide a drinks condition.

💡 What to Do Instead: Buy the ticket early from official sales and plan one club per night. Enter knowing the drinks budget — an island night's true cost is the ticket plus three drinks.
🛏️ TRAP

The beach club sunbed's small print

The front-row beds' minimum spend reaches the price of a full lunch; once seated, there is no bargaining.

💡 What to Do Instead: Ask the minimum spend before sitting, or live the same sea for free on a towel on the public side. Choose a beach club as a deliberate experience, not a default.
🚕 TRAP

Expecting a taxi on an August night

Island taxis are few, and in peak season the night queues pass an hour; the 'we'll just grab a taxi' plan collapses at club exit.

💡 What to Do Instead: Learn the Disco Bus lines, arrange the return in advance or book a group transfer. If you rented a car, appoint the night's driver from the start.
⚓ TRAP

The 'all-inclusive' boat party

The cheap-looking boat parties run on a formula of crowded decks, restricted drinks and short anchor drops far from the good coves.

💡 What to Do Instead: Split a small-group sunset cruise or a private charter: let the route pass Es Vedrà and the stop be a real cove. Compare the price per boat, not per head.
🛡️ Golden rule

In Ibiza the most expensive thing is the lack of a plan: ticket, table, car and transfer — all four cost half to those who book early. And the island's finest things are still free: the cove, the path, the sunset and the sound of drums.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Ibiza

Spanish opens every door on the island, and a pinch of Catalan guarantees a smile.

🗣️ Hola / Bon dia
Hello / good morning — Catalan 'bon dia' is a gesture to the locals
🗣️ Gracias / Gràcies
Thank you — keep it ready in both languages
🗣️ Por favor
Please — valid when asking the sunbed price too
🗣️ ¡Salud!
Cheers! — as the hierbas glass rises
🗣️ Poc a poc
'Little by little, take it easy' — the island's tempo; smile when you hear it, then apply it
