Overview

The Vibe of Dubai

Dubai tells two stories at once: one is the glass-and-steel future gazing down from Burj Khalifa's 828 meters; the other is the past of the wooden abra crossing the Creek for one dirham. Fifty years ago this was a pearl divers' town; today it is the world's fastest-built showcase — but don't let the showcase make you skip the old part: Deira's spice-scented souks, the wind-tower courtyards of Al Fahidi and a glass of karak tea show where the city's heart still beats. The way to love Dubai is to visit both banks.

💬 Habibi
🗣️ /hah-BEE-bee/

Arabic for 'my dear': the word you'll hear a hundred times a day in Dubai. The taxi driver, the waiter, the souk merchant call everyone habibi — it is the common currency of warmth, bargaining and friendship. When someone says 'yes habibi' to you, smile: the Gulf's door has just opened.

Insider Advice

Unwritten Rules for Dubai

#1
Build the schedule around the air conditioning Dubai has two seasons: outdoors and indoors. November to March is an open-air paradise; in summer the street hits 45°C at noon — put outdoor plans before 10 a.m. and after sunset, and give the remaining hours to museums, malls and the aquarium.
#2
Don't skip Old Dubai The Burj and the marina are the city's showcase; the Creek is its soul: Al Fahidi's narrow lanes, the abra ride, the spice and gold souks fit into half a day — the city's most authentic half day. See new Dubai from the sky, old Dubai from water level.
#3
Respect the rules and the etiquette Dubai is a relaxed city, but it has its manners: dress modestly around mosques and government buildings, don't photograph people without permission, avoid rowdiness in public. If you come during Ramadan, eat and drink indoors by day — respect given is respect returned.
Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Dubai

💎 HIDDEN GEM
The abra crossing
You sit shoulder to shoulder on the wooden boat and glide to the far bank to the putter of the motor: spice scents, dhow silhouettes and minarets keep you company. Dubai's oldest line remains its cheapest and most authentic experience.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood
Houses of coral stone and gypsum, barjeel towers, shaded courtyards: old Dubai's restored quarter. Wander between the coffee museum and the small galleries — in the morning hours you'll have it nearly to yourself.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Alserkal Avenue
Galleries, an independent cinema and third-wave coffee in the Al Quoz warehouse district: the heart of Dubai's contemporary art scene. Saturday afternoons are its liveliest — the city's creativity beyond the showcase lives here.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary
Hundreds of flamingos at the foot of the skyscraper skyline: you watch the pink flock from free observation hides. Winter is peak guest season — Dubai's most unexpected postcard.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
The Deira souks at morning
The sack scents of the spice souk, the window blaze of the gold souk, and the dhow wharf's porters at the edge: old trading Dubai is most itself in the morning hours. Bargaining is the custom — accept the offered tea.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Kite Beach and the public beaches
Swim at the free public beach, walk the running track, watch the Burj Al Arab silhouette at sunset: Dubai beach life without a ticket. Grab something from the food trucks — the beach evening assembles itself.
Food & Flavors

What to Taste

The Dubai table is a port city's inheritance: the date-sweet generosity of Emirati cooking, the spices of the Indian quarter, and every cuisine on earth in one city.

🍴 MUST TASTE
Karak and chapati breakfast
Karak in a glass at an Old Dubai cafeteria, hot chapati and fried eggs alongside: a kingdom for a few dirhams. Join the morning workers' queue — this is the city's true breakfast salon.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Machboos and the Emirati table
Lamb or fish machboos over spiced rice, with dates and coffee as welcome: the generous classic of Emirati cooking. Try it at a traditional Emirati restaurant — the floor-cushion seating section is part of the experience.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Shawarma night
The Levantine masters' shawarma is the foundation of Dubai street food: garlicky toum, pickles and thin flatbread. A late-night queue outside a cafeteria is the best address marker in town.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Luqaimat
Fried sweet dough balls under date syrup and sesame: the calling card of Emirati dessert. Crisp outside, soft inside — the star of Ramadan, but good places serve it year-round.
🍴 MUST TASTE
The Friday brunch institution
The long brunch that opens the weekend is Dubai's social tradition: dozens of stations, a table that lasts hours. Options span every budget — booking is essential and appetite should be deployed strategically.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Thali at Meena Bazaar
Tray thalis, dosas and fresh tandoor bread in Bur Dubai's Indian quarter: the city's oldest migrant kitchen at its most honest prices. Join the local office crowd at noon — the tray comes full and the bill light.
Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

❌
A last-minute At the Top ticket at the counter means both a queue and peak pricing; sunset slots sell out weeks ahead.
✅ Better Alternative: Buy a timed ticket online days ahead; if sunset is gone, the first morning slots are calm and clear. Alternative: a cafe terrace facing the tower delivers the view at a fraction of the price.
❌
Cut-price safaris deliver a crowded convoy, rushed dune driving and a conveyor-belt camp evening; the experience bears no relation to the brochure photo.
✅ Better Alternative: Choose a licensed operator running small groups in the protected reserve; ask in advance about the sunset session and the camp menu. Slightly more money buys an entirely different desert.
❌
In Deira and the tourist zones the 'copy bag, copy watch' whisper invites you into back rooms: quality uncertain, bargaining loaded, the situation legally gray.
✅ Better Alternative: Smile and keep walking. Do your souk shopping in spices, dates, gold and fabric — their bargaining is legitimate and their quality in plain view. On gold, check the hallmark stamp.
❌
Between two points that look close on the map may lie a shadeless highway, an interchange and a construction site; a 'ten-minute walk' becomes an ordeal in summer.
✅ Better Alternative: Save walking for the neighborhoods (the Marina promenade, the Creek shore, Al Fahidi); connect the dots by metro and taxi. In summer, add at least fifty percent to any walking estimate.
