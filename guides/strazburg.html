Strasbourg

Capital of Europe, homeland of Christmas, one city of two cultures

Overview

The Vibe of Strasbourg

Strasbourg stands squarely between two worlds and absorbs them both: you buy a croissant from a French bakery in the morning and eat choucroute with a German appetite at noon. Everyone who sees the Gothic cathedral stops for a moment; the canal houses of Petite France, meanwhile, have arrived to meet your fairytale expectations. Add the glass palaces of the European Parliament and you get a city hard to describe and easy to explore.

💬 Stub
🗣️ /shtoob/

Alsatian for 'room, parlour': the root of winstub (wine parlour). Wood panelling, a tiled stove and strangers sharing your table — the stub is the architectural unit of Strasbourg's warmth.

Insider Advice

Unwritten Rules for Strasbourg

#1
Don't miss the cathedral's clock The astronomical clock's apostles parade daily at 12:30; be inside by noon for the short show. The tower's 332 steps spread the Alsatian plain out to the Black Forest — climb in the morning while the queue is short.
#2
Think of the city by bicycle Strasbourg is France's most bike-friendly city: 600 km of paths, dead-flat ground, Vélhop rentals on every corner. With two wheels, the European Quarter and the Orangerie are pedalling — not walking — distance.
#3
December is planned separately The Christkindelsmärik, held since 1570, is France's oldest Christmas market; in December the city transforms. Book hotel and train months ahead for that season.
Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Strasbourg

💎 HIDDEN GEM
The Barrage Vauban terrace
The roof terrace of the 17th-century dam-bridge looks over the covered towers of Petite France to the cathedral: the city's best panorama, free entry, and most visitors never hear of it.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
The apartments of Palais Rohan
The bishops' palace at the cathedral's foot hides three museums at once; the rococo apartments downstairs offer the air of Versailles without the queues.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
The Orangerie's stork nests
Storks, Alsace's emblem bird, have built a colony in the treetops of the Orangerie; in spring their bill-clattering is the park's background music.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
The BNU reading room
The domed reading room of the National University Library in Neustadt is the city's calmest masterpiece; you may enter and breathe even without a student card.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
The towers of the Ponts Couverts
The 'covered bridges' — named for wooden roofs long gone — and their medieval towers give the city's least-known postcard when the evening lights come on.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
The European Parliament bank
Walk or pedal the canal bank to the glass parliament: visits are possible in session periods, the architecture is open from outside always. The idea of Europe in concrete and glass.
Food & Flavors

What to Taste

The Strasbourg table is Alsatian cooking in its capital form: portions German, finesse French, appetite yours.

🍴 MUST TASTE
Tarte flambée — thin and from the embers
The thin dough with cream, onion and bacon is Alsace's pride; in Strasbourg the 'gratinée' (with cheese) and the sweet apple version also make the table. Broken by hand, gone in a minute.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Choucroute and baeckeoffe
The monumental fermented-cabbage plate reigns here too; baeckeoffe, the three-meat casserole slow-baked overnight in wine, is the secret hero of winter days.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Bretzel — warm
A warm bretzel from a street bakery is the official snack of the Strasbourg stroll; the buttered and cheese-topped ones are an invitation to sin.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Foie gras d'Alsace
Foie gras has centuries of history in Alsace; served with brioche and a sweet white (Gewurztraminer), it opens a feast.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Alsace glasses — Riesling to Crémant
You are at the gate of the Wine Route: from the winstub jug to a flute of Crémant, the whole range lives here. When the long green-stemmed Alsace glass reaches your hand, the ritual is complete.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Bredele and pain d'épices
Bredele, the Christmas cookie army, fills every December window; spiced honey bread pain d'épices is the city's scent all year round. Buy a tin — it won't last.
Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

❌
Some square-view terraces sell frozen choucroute at tourist prices; an injustice to Alsatian cooking.
✅ Better Alternative: Walk two streets: in the winstubs of Krutenau and the back lanes the same dish arrives at half the price, in its true form.
❌
Among 300 chalets, imported ornaments and factory goods slip in; same sparkle, no story.
✅ Better Alternative: Pick the producers' chalets: you'll know the Alsace pottery, wood carving and local bredele sellers by their labels and their conversation.
❌
Batorama queues run long at noon, and 70 minutes in a covered boat under the midday sun is the tour at its dullest.
✅ Better Alternative: Book the first morning or early-evening sailing online: beautiful light, no queue.
❌
A rubbery bretzel waiting on a tourist stand is the most unnecessary crime this city can witness.
✅ Better Alternative: Take it warm from a bakery; you can't go wrong where the window says 'artisan boulanger'.
