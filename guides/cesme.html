Cesme

A peninsula of wind, mastic and stone streets

Overview

The Vibe of Cesme

The Cesme peninsula is the westernmost tip of the Turkish Aegean, and here the wind is not weather — it's identity: it turned the old windmills, it snaps the surf flags, it cools Alacati's stone streets in summer. On one side, bougainvillea-draped stone houses and wild-herb mezes; on the other, the Caribbean-colored shallows of Ilica and a marina in the shadow of a castle. Across the water sits the silhouette of the Greek island of Chios — half an hour by ferry. This peninsula is small, but its days are long: morning market, noon cove, afternoon sea breeze, night on the stone streets.

🗣️ Local Concept: İmbat (im-BAHT)

The cool afternoon sea breeze of the Aegean: Cesme summer's invisible air conditioning. Ask a local 'is there imbat today?' — the answer is both a weather report and an evening plan. When the imbat blows, the stone streets cool down and the tables move outdoors.

Insider Advice

3 Unwritten Rules for Cesme

1

Make the wind your planning partner

On this peninsula the wind is the boss of the day: the poyraz that thrills surfers will flip a beach umbrella. Choose your cove by the wind — on a windy day the Alacati surf bay is a spectacle, and sheltered Pasalimani is your refuge.

2

Live Alacati at morning and night, flee to a cove at noon

The stone streets belong to crowds at noon, photographers at dawn, and dinner tables at night. When everyone is at the coves in the midday heat, be there too — Alacati shows its best face early and late.

3

Meet the herbs and the mastic where they live

Cesme's kitchen starts at the wild-herb stall and ends with mastic: don't leave without seeing şevketibostan or tasting mastic pudding. April's Alacati Herb Festival is this cuisine's official holiday — if your dates align, build your program around it.
