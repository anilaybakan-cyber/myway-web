Nice

The French Riviera's real city — not the postcard, the place

Overview

The Vibe of Nice

Nice isn't Monaco. It isn't a film set, a yacht show, or a luxury showcase. It's a real Mediterranean city — Italy-flavoured, sun-drenched, and entirely itself. The fifth largest city in France and the capital of the French Riviera, Nice has been seducing visitors since the 18th century when English aristocrats wintered here to escape the cold, eventually building the seafront promenade that still bears their name. But the Nice worth discovering is older and quieter than any promenade: Vieux Nice, the ochre and terracotta old town, with its Baroque churches, flower market, and lanes so narrow they block out the sky. The socca seller who's been at the same corner for forty years. The midday cannon that has fired from Castle Hill every day since 1861. This city has its own language, its own food, and a pride that has nothing to do with the people who come here in summer.

🗣️ Local Concept: Socca (SOH-kah)

Nice's street food, its soul food, its oldest food. A thin, crispy pancake made from chickpea flour, olive oil, and water — baked in a wood-fired oven on a huge copper plate, cut into irregular wedges, served hot with black pepper. Nothing else. No cheese, no toppings, no variations. Eaten standing at a market stall, in paper, while still too hot. Chez Thérésa at Cours Saleya has been making it since the 1920s. This is where you start.

Insider Advice

3 Unwritten Rules for Nice

1

The beach is pebbles — and the locals love it that way

Nice's famous beach is made of smooth, rounded pebbles, not sand. There is no sand anywhere on the main beach. This surprises almost every first-time visitor. Bring beach shoes, rent a mat from one of the private beach operators, or do what locals do and find a flat stone. The upside: the water is extraordinarily clear and the beach never feels gritty.

2

Nice is not Provence — and not fully French

Nice was Italian until 1860, formally ceded to France by Napoleon III. The old town architecture, the food, the dialect (Niçard), and the character of the place all carry that Italian imprint. The local word for Nice in the old dialect is Nissa. This is not a French city that happens to be near Italy; it's a Mediterranean city that is both, and entirely neither.

3

Castle Hill — always on foot, always free

Colline du Château rises 92 metres above the city and offers the best view in Nice: the curve of the Baie des Anges, the orange rooftops of Vieux Nice, the Alps in the distance. There's a lift, but walk up — the staircase through the rock is part of the experience, and the descent through Vieux Nice rewards you immediately. Free, always. One of the great free views in Europe.

Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Nice

Hidden Gem

To Catch a Thief locations — the Grande Corniche

Hidden Spot

Hitchcock filmed To Catch a Thief (1955) on the French Riviera — the Promenade des Anglais, the mountain roads of the Grande Corniche, and the gardens of Monaco. The film starred Cary Grant and Grace Kelly, and the chemistry between them reportedly led to Grace Kelly meeting Prince Rainier of Monaco shortly afterwards at the Cannes Film Festival. She flew out of Nice airport as a commoner for the last time in 1956. The Corniche road Hitchcock used is still there — same curves, same views, same light.

Hidden Gem

Coco Beach — east of the Port

Hidden Spot

Just past the port, east along the coast, a rocky path leads to Coco Beach — a small, sheltered cove of flat rocks and extraordinarily clear water. No facilities, no beach clubs, no hawkers. Just Niçois who have been coming here for generations. The coastal path continues from here, hugging the cliff face for 1.4km with views out over the Baie des Anges. In winter the path catches the low sun and is one of the most beautiful walks in the city.

Hidden Gem

Cathédrale Saint-Nicolas — Avenue Nicolas II

Hidden Spot

Six onion domes, colourful mosaics, and the largest Russian Orthodox cathedral in Western Europe — standing in a quiet garden ten minutes from the Promenade. Built in 1912 with the support of Tsar Nicholas II, for the large Russian aristocratic community that wintered in Nice. The Romanovs had strong connections to the city; Tsarevich Nicholas died here in 1865. The interior is extraordinary and almost unknown to most visitors. Free entry.

Food & Flavors

What to Taste

🍴 Socca — start here, always

Chickpea flour, olive oil, water, salt. Baked in a wood-fired oven at very high heat on a huge copper plate until the edges are crisp and the centre yielding. Cut into wedges, served immediately, eaten with black pepper. Chez Thérésa at Cours Saleya has been making it since the 1920s — arrive before 10am or queue. Socca is unique to Nice and this area; it doesn't exist anywhere else in France.

🍴 Pan Bagnat — Nice's great sandwich

A round country bread soaked in olive oil, then filled with tuna, hard-boiled egg, raw vegetables, anchovies, olives, and black pepper. The bread must absorb the oil for at least an hour before eating — it is not a fast food item but a patient one. Chez TinTin near Cours Saleya is consistently voted the best in the city. The perfect lunch to take to Castle Hill.

🍴 Salade Niçoise — the real one

Raw vegetables only: tomato, cucumber, radish, spring onion, green pepper, broad beans. Hard-boiled egg. Good tuna in oil. Anchovy fillets. Black olives. Dressed with olive oil. No cooked vegetables, no lettuce, no green beans unless very young and raw. The Nice Tourism Board has a registered recipe. Any restaurant serving it with cooked green beans is lying to you.

🍴 Pissaladière — the original pizza

Thick, soft bread dough topped with a deeply caramelised onion compote, black olives, and anchovy fillets — nothing else. Sold by the slice in boulangeries throughout Vieux Nice. Eaten standing up, warm, with a coffee. The onions must cook for at least an hour; the result is sweet, savoury, and entirely addictive. The predecessor of all pizza.

Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

Avoid

At the private beaches on the Promenade, sunbed plus umbrella plus towel adds up to hotel money — and the pebbles are the same pebbles.

Do this instead: Use the public beach: lay your towel, swim the same sea. If you want comfort, ask for the price list before sitting down.
Avoid

Some restaurants on the tourist side of the flower market run on photo menus and frozen 'Niçoise' classics.

Do this instead: Buy from the market itself, or walk two streets in: real pissaladière waits in the small eateries of Vieux Nice.
