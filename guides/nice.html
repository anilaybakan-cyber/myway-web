Nice

The French Riviera's real city — not the postcard, the place

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Nice

Nice isn't Monaco. It isn't a film set, a yacht show, or a luxury showcase. It's a real Mediterranean city — Italy-flavoured, sun-drenched, and entirely itself. The fifth largest city in France and the capital of the French Riviera, Nice has been seducing visitors since the 18th century when English aristocrats wintered here to escape the cold, eventually building the seafront promenade that still bears their name. But the Nice worth discovering is older and quieter than any promenade: Vieux Nice, the ochre and terracotta old town, with its Baroque churches, flower market, and lanes so narrow they block out the sky. The socca seller who's been at the same corner for forty years. The midday cannon that has fired from Castle Hill every day since 1861. This city has its own language, its own food, and a pride that has nothing to do with the people who come here in summer.

🗣️ Local Concept: Socca (SOH-kah)

Nice's street food, its soul food, its oldest food. A thin, crispy pancake made from chickpea flour, olive oil, and water — baked in a wood-fired oven on a huge copper plate, cut into irregular wedges, served hot with black pepper. Nothing else. No cheese, no toppings, no variations. Eaten standing at a market stall, in paper, while still too hot. Chez Thérésa at Cours Saleya has been making it since the 1920s. This is where you start.

3 Unwritten Rules for Nice

1

The beach is pebbles — and the locals love it that way

Nice's famous beach is made of smooth, rounded pebbles, not sand. There is no sand anywhere on the main beach. This surprises almost every first-time visitor. Bring beach shoes, rent a mat from one of the private beach operators, or do what locals do and find a flat stone. The upside: the water is extraordinarily clear and the beach never feels gritty.

2

Nice is not Provence — and not fully French

Nice was Italian until 1860, formally ceded to France by Napoleon III. The old town architecture, the food, the dialect (Niçard), and the character of the place all carry that Italian imprint. The local word for Nice in the old dialect is Nissa. This is not a French city that happens to be near Italy; it's a Mediterranean city that is both, and entirely neither.

3

Castle Hill — always on foot, always free

Colline du Château rises 92 metres above the city and offers the best view in Nice: the curve of the Baie des Anges, the orange rooftops of Vieux Nice, the Alps in the distance. There's a lift, but walk up — the staircase through the rock is part of the experience, and the descent through Vieux Nice rewards you immediately. Free, always. One of the great free views in Europe.

Getting Around

Nice Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Nice is compact, walkable, and well-connected. The tram handles most of what you need; your feet handle the rest. The city is also the perfect base for the entire French Riviera by train.

✈️

Airport (NCE) → City Centre

Tram line 2 connects the airport directly to the city centre in about 10 minutes — one of the best airport connections in France. Runs frequently, stops at Place Masséna and Vieux Nice. Taxis are significantly more expensive and traffic-dependent. The tram is always the right choice.

🚃

Trams — the city's main network

Nice has two tram lines. Line 1 runs east-west through the city centre connecting the main neighbourhoods. Line 2 connects the airport to the port and city centre. A single ticket is valid for 74 minutes on all tram and bus lines — validate on board every time. A 24-hour pass is good value if you're making three or more trips. Buy tickets at machines at tram stops.

🚂

Train — for day trips along the Riviera

Nice Ville station is one of France's best-connected coastal stations. Monaco is 25 minutes. Antibes 30 minutes. Cannes 40 minutes. Menton and the Italian border 45 minutes. Trains run frequently and are the most scenic way to see the Riviera coast — the line hugs the coast and the views alone are worth the ticket. Far cheaper and more reliable than driving.

🚲

Vélo Bleu — city bike share

Nice's public bike-share scheme has stations throughout the city. A short-term subscription gives access to the bikes; the first 30 minutes is included. The Promenade des Anglais has a dedicated bike lane running the full 7km — one of the best urban cycling routes in France. Best for the Promenade, the port area, and the flat parts of the city.

🚶

On foot — the only way for Vieux Nice

Vieux Nice cannot be explored any other way — the lanes are too narrow for vehicles, and half the best things are unmarked. The old town is 15 minutes' walk from Place Masséna. Castle Hill is 20 minutes from the old town on foot. The Promenade des Anglais is 7km end to end and completely flat. Nice rewards walking above all else.

💡 Day trip strategy

Nice makes an exceptional base. Use the train freely: Monaco on Tuesday for the market; Antibes on a quiet afternoon; Èze village at sunset by bus then train back. The Riviera is compact — a different town every day is entirely possible without ever moving your bag.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Nice: Best Areas

Nice's neighbourhoods are small, walkable, and very different in character. The right one depends entirely on what kind of city you want.

Vieux Nice (Old Town)

Most atmospheric

The historic quarter — baroque churches, the flower market, narrow lanes, great restaurants. The most beautiful place to stay in the city. Lively at night. Some streets can be noisy on weekends. But nowhere else puts you so completely inside what makes Nice worth visiting.

📍 Most beautiful • Best market access • Lively at night

Promenade des Anglais

Most elegant

The seafront boulevard. Grand Belle Époque hotels, the legendary Negresco, the sea on one side and the city on the other. Expensive, iconic, and everything the French Riviera promises. Slightly removed from the city's daily life — more resort than neighbourhood — but the morning walk along the water is hard to beat.

📍 Seafront views • Belle Époque hotels • Resort feel

Le Port & Garibaldi

Most local

The eastern quarter around the port — colourful, genuinely local, and largely undiscovered by tourists. The best restaurants in Nice are concentrated here. Place Garibaldi is one of the city's most beautiful squares. Slightly further from the beach but connected by tram. Where Niçois actually go out to eat.

📍 Best restaurants • Local atmosphere • Colourful port

Musiciens Quarter & Libération

Most affordable

The residential neighbourhoods north of the train station — Belle Époque apartment buildings, local markets, excellent neighbourhood cafés, none of the tourist premium. Well connected by tram. Where Nice residents actually live. Libération market on Tuesday and Friday mornings is outstanding.

📍 Residential feel • Best value • Local markets
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Nice

Nice has over 300 days of sunshine per year. Every season offers something different — the real question is which crowds and which version of the city you want.

Spring

Mar – May
Perfect Season

April and May are when Nice is most itself. The light is extraordinary, the crowds haven't arrived, the markets are overflowing with spring produce, and every terrace is open. By April, the hills behind the city are green and the mimosa is in bloom. The sea is still too cold for swimming but the Promenade in the morning light is one of the great urban walks in Europe. By May the city is at its quietest and most beautiful — museums empty, restaurants bookable, the lanes of Vieux Nice belonging entirely to the people who live there.

Summer

Jun – Aug
Peak Riviera

July and August are when the world comes to Nice. The beaches are packed, the restaurants are full, the sea is warm and clear, and the city buzzes with an energy that's hard to find elsewhere. But it comes at a price: everything costs more, queues are longer, and the real Nice retreats behind the tourist season. July brings the Festival du Jazz de Nice in Cimiez park — one of France's great outdoor festivals, under the olive trees where Matisse is buried.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Best Kept Secret

September and October are the best-kept secret of the French Riviera. The crowds drop sharply after August, the sea stays warm enough to swim until mid-October, and the city returns to its daily rhythm. Restaurant prices normalise, tables are easier to book, and the light takes on a golden quality. November brings the first rains but also the first fires in the fireplaces of Vieux Nice's trattorias. The town belongs to itself again.

Winter

Dec – Feb
Mild & Quiet

Nice is genuinely mild in winter — average January temperatures around 13°C, and the sun appears regularly even in the coldest months. This is why the English came in the first place. The Promenade des Anglais on a sunny December morning, nearly empty, with the Alps visible behind the city, is one of the most quietly beautiful urban experiences in Europe. Prices drop significantly. Locals take over. The old town is at its most authentic.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Nice

Niçoise cuisine is one of France's most distinct regional traditions — Mediterranean, olive-based, deeply vegetable-driven, and largely ignored by the rest of the country. This is the food of a city that was Italian until 1860 and has never entirely forgotten it.

🥞

Socca — start here, always

Chickpea flour, olive oil, water, salt. Baked in a wood-fired oven at very high heat on a huge copper plate until the edges are crisp and the centre yielding. Cut into wedges, served immediately, eaten with black pepper. Chez Thérésa at Cours Saleya has been making it since the 1920s — arrive before 10am or queue. Socca is unique to Nice and this area; it doesn't exist anywhere else in France.

⭐ Chez Thérésa · Cours Saleya · arrive early
🥙

Pan Bagnat — Nice's great sandwich

A round country bread soaked in olive oil, then filled with tuna, hard-boiled egg, raw vegetables, anchovies, olives, and black pepper. The bread must absorb the oil for at least an hour before eating — it is not a fast food item but a patient one. Chez TinTin near Cours Saleya is consistently voted the best in the city. The perfect lunch to take to Castle Hill.

⭐ Chez TinTin · eat it overlooking the sea
🥗

Salade Niçoise — the real one

Raw vegetables only: tomato, cucumber, radish, spring onion, green pepper, broad beans. Hard-boiled egg. Good tuna in oil. Anchovy fillets. Black olives. Dressed with olive oil. No cooked vegetables, no lettuce, no green beans unless very young and raw. The Nice Tourism Board has a registered recipe. Any restaurant serving it with cooked green beans is lying to you.

⭐ Raw vegetables only · no cooked green beans
🍕

Pissaladière — the original pizza

Thick, soft bread dough topped with a deeply caramelised onion compote, black olives, and anchovy fillets — nothing else. Sold by the slice in boulangeries throughout Vieux Nice. Eaten standing up, warm, with a coffee. The onions must cook for at least an hour; the result is sweet, savoury, and entirely addictive. The predecessor of all pizza.

⭐ Sold in every Vieux Nice bakery · warm, by the slice
🍷

Bellet wine — Nice's best-kept secret

Almost nobody outside Nice knows this exists: a tiny AOC wine appellation in the hills directly above the city, producing red, white, and rosé. Only about 50 hectares of vines. The rosé is exceptional and almost impossible to find outside the region. Look for it on wine lists throughout the city — it's local, it's rare, and it's very good.

⭐ AOC Bellet · local and rare · ask for it
☕

Coffee — the Italian way

Nice's Italian heritage shows in its coffee culture. Espresso is drunk standing at the bar, short and strong. A noisette is espresso with a dash of warm milk — the local standard. Cappuccino exists but is a morning-only drink. The cafés on Cours Saleya are tourist-priced; the cafés one street back in Vieux Nice are where locals go. The difference in price and quality is immediate.

⭐ Stand at the bar · noisette is the local order
🍷 Cours Saleya — morning ritual

The flower and food market at Cours Saleya runs Tuesday to Sunday until early afternoon. Get there before 9am: the light is extraordinary, the socca is fresh, the flower sellers are arranging their stalls, and you're sharing it with Niçois rather than tourists. Buy flowers. Eat socca. Drink a coffee. This is the best morning in Nice.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Nice

Nice hides its best things in plain sight — on a hillside everyone walks past, in a cove everyone misses, behind a church door that's always open. The rewards for the curious are extraordinary.

✨ Where Hitchcock filmed — and Grace Kelly changed her life

To Catch a Thief locations — the Grande Corniche

Hitchcock filmed To Catch a Thief (1955) on the French Riviera — the Promenade des Anglais, the mountain roads of the Grande Corniche, and the gardens of Monaco. The film starred Cary Grant and Grace Kelly, and the chemistry between them reportedly led to Grace Kelly meeting Prince Rainier of Monaco shortly afterwards at the Cannes Film Festival. She flew out of Nice airport as a commoner for the last time in 1956. The Corniche road Hitchcock used is still there — same curves, same views, same light.

✨ The locals' beach — no tourists, no crowds

Coco Beach — east of the Port

Just past the port, east along the coast, a rocky path leads to Coco Beach — a small, sheltered cove of flat rocks and extraordinarily clear water. No facilities, no beach clubs, no hawkers. Just Niçois who have been coming here for generations. The coastal path continues from here, hugging the cliff face for 1.4km with views out over the Baie des Anges. In winter the path catches the low sun and is one of the most beautiful walks in the city.

✨ A Russian cathedral in the middle of Nice

Cathédrale Saint-Nicolas — Avenue Nicolas II

Six onion domes, colourful mosaics, and the largest Russian Orthodox cathedral in Western Europe — standing in a quiet garden ten minutes from the Promenade. Built in 1912 with the support of Tsar Nicholas II, for the large Russian aristocratic community that wintered in Nice. The Romanovs had strong connections to the city; Tsarevich Nicholas died here in 1865. The interior is extraordinary and almost unknown to most visitors. Free entry.

✨ Matisse's grave — under olive trees, in a Roman park

Monastère de Cimiez — Cimiez Hill

Henri Matisse lived in Nice for the last 37 years of his life and is buried in the cemetery of the Monastère de Cimiez, alongside fellow artist Raoul Dufy. The monastery sits in a Roman archaeological park — the ruins of a Roman amphitheatre, an olive grove, and a public garden where locals play pétanque. The Musée Matisse is next door and houses the world's largest Matisse collection. Almost no tourist map connects all three. Walk up. Take time.

✨ Nice's secret vineyard — above the city

AOC Bellet — the hills of Saint-Roman-de-Bellet

In the hills directly above Nice, largely invisible from the city, a tiny wine appellation produces some of the most interesting wine in the south of France. AOC Bellet covers only about 50 hectares and produces red, white, and rosé — most of it consumed locally, almost none exported. The rosé is exceptional. Several domaines offer visits by appointment. Ask in any wine shop for Bellet — the reaction of the person behind the counter will tell you whether they know anything about wine.

✨ The cannon you won't hear coming

Le Canon du Midi — Castle Hill

Every day at exactly noon, a cannon fires from Castle Hill. It has done so every day since 1861. The story: a Scottish lord named Sir Thomas Coventry, impatient for his wife to return from shopping in town, persuaded the Nice city council to fire a cannon at midday so she would know to come home. The city kept doing it for over 160 years. Walk up to Castle Hill before noon — you can watch the firing from close range, and the view from the top is the best in Nice.

✨ Anyone can walk into the Negresco

Le Negresco has been the most iconic building on the Promenade des Anglais since 1913. You don't need to be a guest — walk in, order a drink at Le Versailles bar, and sit on the terrace overlooking the sea. The legendary interior dome and art-filled salons are free to admire. One of the world's great hotel interiors, no reservation required.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Nice is small but its tourism is big; the traps cluster along the seafront. Know these four and enjoy the Mediterranean like a local.

🏖️ TRAP

Beach clubs charging for everything

At the private beaches on the Promenade, sunbed plus umbrella plus towel adds up to hotel money — and the pebbles are the same pebbles.

💡 What to Do Instead: Use the public beach: lay your towel, swim the same sea. If you want comfort, ask for the price list before sitting down.
🥘 TRAP

'Menu touristique' on the edges of Cours Saleya

Some restaurants on the tourist side of the flower market run on photo menus and frozen 'Niçoise' classics.

💡 What to Do Instead: Buy from the market itself, or walk two streets in: real pissaladière waits in the small eateries of Vieux Nice.
🫓 TRAP

Socca that isn't served hot

Socca is eaten the moment it leaves the oven; pre-cooked, microwaved socca is an injustice to the city.

💡 What to Do Instead: Choose where it goes straight from wood fire to plate (Chez Pipo is the classic); lots of black pepper, eat with your hands.
🚗 TRAP

Driving the coast in summer

In July and August the corniche is a parking war plus traffic; a rental car is usually a burden, not freedom.

💡 What to Do Instead: Take the train: Villefranche, Èze, Monaco, Cannes are all on the coastal line. The view comes free.
🛡️ The golden rule

Most of Nice's beauty is free: the sea, the light, the Promenade, Colline du Château. Save your wallet for the food, not the view.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Nice

French courtesy words open doors in Nice; a pinch of Nissart wins smiles.

🗣️ Bonjour
Hello — the mandatory first word of every exchange; skip it and the service turns cold
🗣️ Merci
Thank you — stronger with beaucoup
🗣️ S'il vous plaît
