Naples

Chaotic, noisy, full of life — the city that serves the world's best pizza for the least money, wrapped in paper, eaten standing up

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Naples

Naples frightens you first, then won't let you go. Traffic with no rules, narrow streets, noisy crowds. But at the same time Italy's purest cuisine, the world's best pizza, Europe's most densely layered archaeological memory and the incomparable view of the Bay are all here. Vesuvius could wake at any moment — and Naples knows this. That's why the city holds tight to today: loudly, fast and with its whole heart. Stand in the middle of Spaccanapoli and the city pulls you in every direction. Let it.

🗣️ Local Concept: O' cafè (o KA-feh)

Naples' espresso. Italy's strongest, smallest, cheapest coffee. At the counter, in one sip, €1-1.50. The caffè sospeso tradition comes from here too: when someone has a coffee they leave one paid for someone who can't afford it — it stays recorded at the till. You can ask 'is there a sospeso?' Naples invented coffee as a form of sharing.

3 Unwritten Rules for Naples

1

Crowds and noise are normal — don't try to protect yourself from them

Naples looks disorganised because it is. Cars run red lights, scooters mount pavements however narrow they are, the concept of a queue exists in shops but isn't observed. No need to panic — the city functions within this chaos. Just be a little more careful, check your pockets and get into the city's rhythm.

2

Pizza is nowhere else this good — and they know it

Neapolitan pizza is on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list. Why it isn't the same elsewhere: Campania's San Marzano tomatoes, mozzarella di bufala, a wood-fired oven at 485°C and dough rested for at least 8 hours. Margherita or Marinara — that's all. No extra sauces, no toppings, no fillings. The skill is in the simplicity; adding extras is the mistake.

3

Half a day isn't enough for Pompeii — plan a full day

Pompeii is 30-40 minutes from Naples and most people grasp its real scale only when they're there: 66 hectares, 11,000 inhabitants, forum, theatre, baths, villas. Seeing it in half a day is possible but stays superficial. It opens at 9am and is most crowded at midday — getting in at opening is the best approach. Herculaneum is much smaller but far better preserved; if you're seeing both, go to Herculaneum first.

Getting Around

Naples Public Transit & Airport Transfers

The metro has become an art gallery, funiculars climb the hills, buses are chaotic but they work. Walking is the best option for the centre.

✈️

Airport → City

The Alibus (ANM) goes from the airport to Piazza Garibaldi (Naples Centrale train station) and Piazza Municipio. Ticket €5, departures every 15-20 minutes. Fixed-price taxi to the city centre: €25-35. Alibus is the most practical option — cheaper than a taxi. There's no direct metro connection from the airport; Alibus drops you at Piazza Garibaldi where you can connect to the metro.

🚇

Line 1 — Art Metro

One of Naples' most special experiences: Toledo, Università, Garibaldi and Municipio stations were designed by international artists. Toledo station was voted Europe's most beautiful metro station in 2012. Single fare €1.50, valid 90 minutes, funiculars included. Runs the full length of the historic centre.

🚡

Funiculars — the climb to Vomero

Four funicular lines climb Naples' hills: Funicolare Centrale from Piazzetta Augusteo to Vomero; Funicolare di Chiaia from Via del Parco Margherita to Vomero; Funicolare di Montesanto from Piazza Montesanto to San Martino; Funicolare di Mergellina from the Mergellina waterfront to the Posillipo hilltop. Same ticket system, €1.50.

🚌

Bus — chaotic but comprehensive

City buses run on the same ticket system. Necessary for reaching neighbourhoods the metro and funiculars can't access. Can run late during busy hours. Buy tickets in advance — not available on the bus. Available at stops or tabacchi shops.

🚶

Walking — and caution

Spaccanapoli, Via dei Tribunali, Quartieri Spagnoli and the historic centre are all walkable. Pavements are narrow, scooters come from every direction — be careful. Safe to walk at night but avoid isolated streets and don't carry valuables visibly. The centre isn't large: 15 minutes from Piazza del Gesù to Toledo.

💡 Campania ArteCard — museums and transport combined

The 3-day Naples ArteCard (€21) covers all city public transport (metro, bus, funicular) and gives free entry to the first two museums, discounted entry to others. Includes the Naples National Museum, Certosa di San Martino and Capodimonte. Worth considering if you're planning a museum-heavy itinerary. The 3-day card covering all of Campania is €32 — Pompeii and Herculaneum included.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Naples: Best Areas

Naples' neighbourhoods are very different from each other. The historic centre is close to everything, Vomero is quiet and panoramic, Chiaia is modern and on the waterfront.

Centro Storico — historic centre

Most central

Spaccanapoli, Via dei Tribunali, San Gregorio Armeno (the Christmas figurine street) are all in this neighbourhood. The neighbourhood itself, Naples' historic centre (Centro Storico), has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1995. Walking distance to everything but busy and noisy. Nightlife is lively, early morning is the quietest. Budget hotels and small family guesthouses (B&B) are most plentiful in this area.

📍 Spaccanapoli • UNESCO World Heritage • Close to everything

Quartieri Spagnoli

Most authentic

A grid of narrow streets built for Spanish soldiers in the 16th century. Laundry hangs from window to window, children play in the alleyways, a bar on every corner. Diego Maradona's mural portrait is here — on Via Emanuele De Deo. Touristy but still a real neighbourhood. Walking distance to Toledo metro and Via Toledo.

📍 Maradona mural • Narrow street grid • Local neighbourhood life

Chiaia

Most modern

Modern neighbourhood on the Bay waterfront, stretching along the Lungomare. High-quality restaurants, bars and shops. Mergellina harbour is nearby. An evening walk along the Lungomare is one of Naples' most enjoyable activities. Slightly further from the historic centre but easily reached by taxi or metro.

📍 Lungomare waterfront • Modern restaurants • Mergellina harbour

Vomero

Most quiet

The hilltop neighbourhood reached by funicular. Castel Sant'Elmo and Certosa di San Martino are here — both among the city's finest viewpoints. Away from the crowds of the historic centre, quieter and more local. Ten minutes to the centre by funicular. Good for morning local cafés and market shopping.

📍 Castel Sant'Elmo • Certosa di San Martino • Funicular access

Near Piazza Garibaldi

Close to the train station

Walking distance to Naples Centrale. The Circumvesuviana train (Pompeii, Herculaneum, Sorrento) departs from here. Practical for day trips. The neighbourhood itself is very touristy but the most central point for transport. Prices are relatively reasonable.

📍 Naples Centrale • Circumvesuviana • Practical for day trips
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Naples

Naples can be visited year-round but summer brings higher temperatures and tourist density.

Spring

Apr – May
The best time

April and May are Naples' most balanced period. Temperatures around 18-24°C, the Bay at its most vivid, tourists present but away from peak season. Maggio dei Monumenti (May Monuments Month): normally closed historic buildings and private spaces open for visits — follow this programme. Semana Santa (Easter) period brings religious processions and ceremonies through the city.

Summer

Jun – Aug
Hot, crowded and lively

July and August are the busiest and hottest months. The Amalfi Coast and Capri are at their peak — you won't get in without reservations. But Naples itself is something else on summer evenings: aperitivo on the Lungomare, fish restaurants in Posillipo, gelato in Mergellina. The city is at its most alive and bearable before 10am and after 8pm.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Second best time

September and October see the crowds thin while the weather stays mild. The sea is still warm enough to swim. The Amalfi Coast and Capri are much more comfortable to visit. Trattorias open with new seasonal menus — walnut pasta, courgette flowers, fresh fish. Rain increases in November but museums and historic sites are nearly empty.

Winter

Dec – Feb
Quiet and affordable

December Christmas preparations bring the city to life — San Gregorio Armeno's Christmas figurine street is at its peak this time of year. January and February are Naples' quietest period. Museums nearly empty, no queues at Pompeii, hotels cheap. Temperatures 10-15°C, some rainy days. During Carnevale (February), struffoli and sfogliatella are in season at pastry shops.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Naples

The Neapolitan pizza that entered UNESCO's list in 2017 — when King Umberto I and Queen Margherita visited Naples in 1889, pizzaiolo Raffaele Esposito prepared a pizza carrying the colours of the Italian flag: red tomato, white mozzarella, green basil. The pizza named after the Queen is known everywhere in the world today, but its real taste is still only here. Naples doesn't stop there: sfogliatella, cuoppo, pizza fritta — this city eats on the street, at the counter, standing up.

🍕

Neapolitan pizza — the real thing

Margherita (San Marzano tomatoes, mozzarella di bufala, basil) or Marinara (tomatoes, garlic, oregano — no cheese). No other options, no other options needed. L'Antica Pizzeria da Michele at Via Cesare Sersale 1 — since 1870, only these two varieties, cash payment, long queue. Sorbillo on Via dei Tribunali — shorter queue, same quality. Order another before you're halfway through the first.

⭐ Da Michele or Sorbillo · €5–8 · cash
🍕

Pizza fritta

During World War II Naples was bombed, the wood-fired ovens were destroyed but frying oil was still available — so pizza began to be cooked in hot oil rather than an oven. Pizza fritta was born from these difficulties. The dough is filled with ricotta, cicoli (fried pork skin) and provola cheese and puffs up in hot oil. Sophia Loren fried this pizza in a famous film scene. €3-5, a full meal on its own. The pizzeria called 1947 Pizza Fritta is at Via Pietro Colletta 29-31.

⭐ 1947 Pizza Fritta, Via Pietro Colletta 29-31 · €3–5
🥐

Sfogliatella

A shell-shaped ricotta pastry — layered and crispy outside, filled with cinnamon ricotta and candied fruit inside. Two versions: riccia (crispy layered) and frolla (covered in short pastry on the outside, softer). The fresh version comes out of the oven in the early morning hours. La Sfogliatella Mary is inside Galleria Umberto I. An espresso alongside — this is the authentic Neapolitan morning.

⭐ La Sfogliatella Mary, Galleria Umberto I · fresh in the morning · €1.50–2
🍟

Cuoppo

Mixed fried food in a paper cone: the seafood version (cuoppo di mare) has prawns, squid, mussels; the land version (cuoppo di terra) has courgette flowers, aubergine, potato chips. Around Pignasecca Market or Piazza Monteoliveto. €4-6. Eat while walking — no seating.

⭐ Around Pignasecca Market · €4–6 · eat while walking
🍝

Ragù napoletano

Pork and tomato sauce cooked for hours — sometimes 5-6 hours — on the lowest heat. Monday and Thursday are the traditional days; your chance of finding it at trattorias is highest on those days. Served with spaghetti, rigatoni or ziti. Crusco bread alongside, the meal is complete. Trattoria da Nennella in Quartieri Spagnoli.

⭐ Trattoria da Nennella, Quartieri Spagnoli · Mon-Thu · €10–14
☕

Espresso — and caffè sospeso

Neapolitan espresso: strong, small and €1-1.50. Drink at the counter, standing, fast — this is Naples coffee culture. The caffè sospeso (suspended coffee) tradition: an extra coffee paid into the till, for someone without means later. To take part, say 'sospeso' and pay for two coffees, leaving one behind. One of Naples' most humane rituals.

⭐ Any bar · €1–1.50 at the counter · join the sospeso tradition
🍷 Pignasecca Market — 7am to 1pm

Right next to Quartieri Spagnoli, on Via Pignasecca. Naples' most lively open-air food market. Fresh fish, vegetables, fruit, cheese, street food. Open 7am-1pm, closed Sundays. Inside the market: cuoppo stalls, mozzarella sellers and breakfast bars. Not touristy, a genuine neighbourhood market. Prices low, quality high.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Naples

Naples' most interesting corners are usually just one street away from the crowds — some in the city's underground, some on its hilltops.

✨ Europe's most beautiful metro station — and an art gallery

Toledo Metro Station

Toledo station on Line 1 won the award for Europe's most beautiful metro station in 2012. Designed by Oscar Tusquets: blue-purple mosaic surfaces that give a cave-like feeling as you descend deeper, themes of sea and light. No extra charge for entry — included in a standard metro ticket. Add Municipio, Università and Garibaldi stations: each is the work of a different artist. This metro line alone is worth making the journey for.

✨ WWII shelter, Bourbon tunnel and abandoned cars

Galleria Borbonica

On Via Morelli, near Chiaia. Built in the 19th century as an escape tunnel for King Ferdinand II. Later became a hospital, police depot and World War II air raid shelter. Inside, old cars and motorcycles from the 1940s-50s are still there. Different from Napoli Sotterranea: here it's not ancient Rome but recent history. Several different tour options available. Advance reservation essential.

✨ Vesuvius and Bay views — the city's finest viewpoint

Castel Sant'Elmo

A star-shaped castle on the Vomero hilltop, reachable by funicular. Dating from the 14th century. From its terraces: Vesuvius, the Bay, Capri and the whole city visible 360 degrees. The adjacent Certosa di San Martino (former Carthusian monastery) converted to a museum — the city's richest museum in terms of Neapolitan art and history. Both reachable by the same funicular.

✨ Maradona's home and a neighbourhood's memory

Quartieri Spagnoli murals

Diego Maradona's enormous portrait on Via Emanuele De Deo has been here since 1990 — painted, removed, painted again. Naples, which Maradona led to championships in 1987 and 1990, regards him like a saint; there are statues and shrines to him on the street. Walking through the neighbourhood you see not just Maradona but dozens of other murals: paintings telling of Naples' identity, history and politics. Free, walkable.

✨ An ancient Roman theatre under a house

Teatro Greco-Romano di Neapolis

In Spaccanapoli, on Via Anticaglia. A 2nd-century Roman theatre now buried under buildings. The old arches of the theatre are visible through the narrow streets between them. Literally the traces of a history built over by the city. Some sections can be visited on guided tours; consider combining with Napoli Sotterranea.

✨ Naples' quietest museum — and its most comprehensive collection

Museo di Capodimonte

In the north of the city, inside Capodimonte Park. Former Bourbon palace, now museum. Caravaggio, Titian, Raphael, Botticelli — and Andy Warhol's famous Vesuvius series. A fraction of the Naples National Museum's visitor numbers. The extensive park is also free to walk through. 30-40 minutes by bus from the city centre but a place where you could spend hours.

✨ Lungomare — evening walk and the Bay

The coastal road stretching from Castel dell'Ovo to Mergellina. Between 7pm and 9pm Neapolitans walk here, eat ice cream, sit. Views of Vesuvius and the Bay, Capri's silhouette across the water. Free entry, walking distance about 3 kilometres. One of Naples' most beautiful hours — you see the city's finest views without paying for anything.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Naples looks chaotic, but its rules are clear. Know these four and the city will open itself to you without fear.

🍕 TRAP

Burying hours in one pizzeria's queue

Da Michele is legend, but a two-hour tourist queue doesn't make the pizza better; every Naples neighbourhood has its own master.

💡 What to Do Instead: If the queue overflows, walk two streets: Sorbillo, Di Matteo, Starita or a nameless local oven — all in the same league.
🎫 TRAP

'Private guide' hunters at Garibaldi and Pompeii

The 'official guides' who approach at the station and the ruins' gate sell mediocre tours at inflated prices; some tickets turn out fake.

💡 What to Do Instead: Buy Pompeii tickets from the official site; if you want a guide, hire a licensed one inside — never deal on the Circumvesuviana.
🚕 TRAP

The 'broken meter' taxi

The 'meter's broken' classic in Naples taxis is the opening move of the tourist tariff.

💡 What to Do Instead: Ask for the meter before boarding or point to the fixed-fare route list; failing that, call an app — and the metro is the city's finest art gallery anyway.
📱 TRAP

Street bargains on 'brand-new' phones

Hand-sold 'sealed' phones and watches are the city's oldest game: a stone in the box, a hole in your pocket.

💡 What to Do Instead: Never buy electronics on the street; for a gift take a corno (the red horn) — cheap, local, and the city swears it brings luck.
🛡️ The golden rule

Safety in Naples is less about which street than about bearing: wave the phone less, wear the bag across, smile at the chaos. The city is kind to guests; panic is what marks the tourist.

Local Slang

Italian opens doors; Neapolitan wins hearts. With these five, the street is yours.

🗣️ Ciao
Hi / bye — any hour, to anyone
🗣️ Grazie
Thank you — short, clear, always
🗣️ Per favore
Please — add it to the end of your order
🗣️ Uè!
