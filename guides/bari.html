Bari

The capital of Puglia — and the street where grandmothers sit in their doorways making orecchiette (Puglia's handmade pasta) by hand

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Bari

Bari sits on the Adriatic coast in southern Italy, the capital of the Puglia region. It's different from northern Italy's polished cities — life here is lived on the street. In the labyrinthine alleys of Bari Vecchia, grandmothers roll orecchiette in their doorways, focaccia aromas drift from bakeries in the morning, and fishermen sell the morning catch at the harbour. The city is raw and alive, and that energy draws you in. Tourist pressure hasn't reached Venice or Rome levels yet — which means it still offers a genuinely local experience. It's also the perfect base for exploring Puglia: Alberobello, Matera and the Adriatic coves are all day-trip distance.

🗣️ Local Concept: Orecchiette (o-re-KYE-te)

Puglia's handmade pasta — the name means 'little ears' in Italian. Shaped by pressing with the thumb, producing small pasta pieces thin in the middle and thicker at the edges. The classic version served with cime di rapa (turnip tops), garlic, chilli and sometimes anchovies is the most representative dish of Puglia. Watching the local women on Strada delle Orecchiette make this pasta by hand — and buying it fresh — is both a meal and an experience.

3 Unwritten Rules for Bari

1

Enter Bari Vecchia in the early morning hours

Bari's historic old neighbourhood is completely different in the morning. Street vendors are setting up their stalls, bakeries are opening, grandmothers are beginning to roll orecchiette. Tourists and crowds grow in the afternoon. Between 8am and 10am is both the quietest and most authentic moment. For focaccia barese, getting it fresh from the bakery at this hour is essential.

2

Don't skip Strada delle Orecchiette

A narrow street inside Bari Vecchia, also known as Arco Basso. Local women have been rolling handmade orecchiette here in their doorways for centuries and selling it. Taking fresh orecchiette home or simply watching on the spot — this street is one of Bari's most authentic experiences. Most lively between 9am and 12pm. €3-5 per packet.

3

Use it as a base for exploring Puglia

From Bari: Alberobello 1 hour (by FSE bus), Matera 1.5 hours, Polignano a Mare 30-45 minutes, Lecce 1.5 hours. All these destinations are reachable by train or bus and ideal for day trip planning. Staying in Bari and exploring the surroundings is both practical and economical.

Getting Around

Bari Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Bari's historic centre is walkable. AMTAB buses cover the city; regional trains are ideal for all of Puglia.

✈️

Airport → Bari centre

Ferrotramviaria train from the airport to Bari Centrale in approximately 20 minutes, ticket around €5. Departures every 30-60 minutes. AMTAB bus also provides a connection but can take longer due to traffic. Fixed-price taxi approximately €25. The train is definitely the most practical option.

🚌

AMTAB — city buses

Bari's city bus network. Single fare €1-1.20, valid for 75 minutes. Tickets from tabacchi (tobacconists) or edicole (newsstands). When you board, don't forget to validate your ticket in the machine. Buses are used for the Lungomare and Pane e Pomodoro beach. The historic centre is within walking distance.

🚂

Regional train — for exploring Puglia

From Bari Centrale, connections to all of Puglia. Alberobello: ~1 hour by FSE bus (€5-8) or 1.5-2 hours by connecting train. Polignano a Mare: 30-45 minutes, €3-5. Lecce: 1.5 hours (regional train €10-15, fast train €15-35).

🚗

Car hire — for coastal exploration

Using a car within Bari city is actually unnecessary — traffic is heavy and parking is difficult. However, a car offers freedom for exploring the Puglia coast and reaching spots that trains don't serve. Plan the hire from outside Bari centre.

🚶

Walking — ideal for the historic centre

Bari Vecchia, Piazza Mercantile, Basilica di San Nicola, Castello Svevo, Lungomare and the Murat neighbourhood are all walkable — compact and close together. Comfortable shoes essential for Bari Vecchia's narrow streets. Best hours for walking on sunny days are morning and evening.

💡 Polignano a Mare — 30 minutes from Bari

A small town built on a rocky promontory jutting into the Adriatic. 30 minutes from Bari by train, €3-4. Cave beaches carved into the rock, crystal Adriatic water, narrow white streets. Birthplace of Domenico Modugno (composer of Volare). Very crowded in the afternoon in midsummer — go early in the morning or choose September-October. Free entry.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Bari: Best Areas

In Bari, Bari Vecchia or the Murat neighbourhood are the best locations — both are central but their characters are very different.

Bari Vecchia — the historic old town

Most authentic

Narrow stone streets, Baroque churches, focaccia aromas. Basilica di San Nicola and Castello Svevo within walking distance. Quiet and impressive in the morning hours. Some streets are still rough and unrestored but that's part of the authenticity.

📍 Basilica di San Nicola • Strada delle Orecchiette • Most local experience

Murat — modern centre

Most practical

Wide boulevards, close to Bari Centrale. Via Sparano shopping street. 10 minutes walk to Bari Vecchia. Comfortable hotels with good connections.

📍 Close to Bari Centrale • Via Sparano • Comfortable hotels

Around the Lungomare

For the sea

On the Adriatic coast. Close to the free Pane e Pomodoro beach. On the evening passeggiata route. 15-20 minutes walk to Bari Vecchia.

📍 Lungomare seafront • Pane e Pomodoro beach • Evening passeggiata

Japigia — local neighbourhood

Most local

To the south, a little further from the historic centre. No tourists but local trattorias and neighbourhood life. Budget-friendly. 15 minutes to the centre by bus.

📍 Local trattorias • Budget-friendly • Authentic neighbourhood life

Polignano a Mare

Puglia experience

30 minutes from Bari, a small town on an Adriatic cliff. Completely different when the midday crowds leave. Small hotels and B&Bs available.

Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Bari

Bari can be visited year-round — summer is hot but lively, spring and autumn are the most balanced.

Spring

Apr – Jun
The best time

April and May are Bari's golden period. Temperatures 18-24°C, no crowds, orecchiette season at its best with fresh vegetables. Puglia's olive groves and flowering countryside are unmissable views during this period. Ideal through to mid-June.

Summer

Jun – Aug
Hot and lively

July and August are hot but Bari Vecchia's narrow streets are shaded. The Lungomare fills with life in the evenings. Pane e Pomodoro beach is still breathable on weekday mornings. Polignano a Mare gets very crowded in the afternoon — go early. Southern Italy's summer evening culture is at its most vibrant during this period.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Second best time

September and October are excellent for Bari. Temperatures 22-26°C, the Adriatic is still swimmable. Puglia's olive harvest in October-November. The orecchiette con cime di rapa season begins — turnip tops are at their finest in autumn. November brings more rain but prices drop significantly.

Winter

Dec – Feb
Quiet and affordable

From December to February Bari is mild and quiet by southern Italian standards — the city doesn't really get cold, averaging 10-15°C. Some rainy days but plenty of sunny moments too. Bari Vecchia in the morning hours is empty and impressive. Winter trattorias serve tiella barese and bombette at their finest. Prices noticeably lower.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Bari

Food in Bari is both cheap and delicious — street food culture is strong, focaccia is the morning ritual, orecchiette is made by hand.

🍕

Focaccia barese

Bari's defining food. Panificio Fiore (Via Calefati) comes out fresh from the wood-fired oven at 8:30am — go early for this address. Alternative: small bakeries inside Bari Vecchia. Eat it hot — still delicious when cool but there's a difference. €2-4. Walking out of the shop and eating in the street is the true Barese way.

⭐ Panificio Fiore · Bari Vecchia bakeries · fresh in the morning · €2–4
🥟

Panzerotto

Bari's own fried dough — tomato and mozzarella filling inside. Puffs up in the frying oil and turns golden. Crispy outside, hot and yielding inside. Venezia 40 (on the Lungomare, facing the Adriatic) is the most famous address. Can have a queue from 6pm onwards. €3-5.

⭐ Venezia 40, Lungomare · €3–5 · crowded in the evening — go early
🍝

Orecchiette con cime di rapa

Puglia's most representative dish — handmade little-ear pasta, turnip tops, garlic and chilli. The essence of the dish is simple but ingredient quality changes everything. Turnip tops are at their finest in winter. €8-12 at local trattorias in Bari Vecchia and Murat.

⭐ Local trattorias · €8–12 · ask for freshly made
🟡

Sgagliozze

Fried polenta slices made from cornmeal — the evening street food of Bari Vecchia. A stall sets up and sells them at Largo Albicocca at certain hours. Crispy outside, soft inside, with salt on top. €1-2. Simple but addictive. Perfect alongside a glass of Primitivo at aperitivo hour.

⭐ Largo Albicocca, Bari Vecchia · €1–2 · midday and evening hours
🐚

Crudo di mare — raw seafood

Eating raw seafood at the Adriatic's edge is a Barese tradition. At the N'dranghet fish market near the old harbour, fresh mussels, sea urchins and octopus can be eaten straight from the morning catch. Fresh and cheap — it may take some courage. Also available at fish stalls within Bari Vecchia.

⭐ N'dranghet fish market, old harbour · early morning · fresh and cheap
🍰

Pasticciotto and sporcamuss

Puglia's pastry culture: pasticciotto (a small shortcrust tart with custard filling, originally from Lecce but everywhere), sporcamuss (a flaky pastry with sugar and custard — the name means 'dirty your mouth'). Both ideal for breakfast or an afternoon café break. €1.50-3 at pastry shops.

⭐ Bari pastry shops · €1.50–3 · morning or afternoon
🍷 Aperitivo culture — with Primitivo wine

Aperitivo hour in Bari (6pm-8pm) is the most enjoyable pause of the day. At bars and osterias, Puglia's Primitivo or Negroamaro red, Fiano white or an Aperol Spritz, alongside complimentary sgagliozze and taralli. Bars in the Murat neighbourhood and osterias near Bari Vecchia are the liveliest at this hour. A glass €4-7.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Bari

Bari's finest corners are in the labyrinth of Bari Vecchia, along the coastline and just beyond short train journeys.

✨ The street where grandmothers roll pasta in their doorways

Strada delle Orecchiette (Arco Basso)

A narrow street inside Bari Vecchia, known as Arco Basso. Local women have been rolling orecchiette, cavatelli and other handmade pasta here in their doorways for centuries. Ask permission before taking photos, buy fresh pasta. Most lively between 9am and 12pm. €3-5 per packet — the finest Puglia souvenir.

✨ Rarely visited — but Bari's most beautiful building

Basilica di San Nicola

An 11th-century Norman-Romanesque basilica in the heart of Bari Vecchia. It's believed that the relics of Saint Nicholas — who inspired Father Christmas — are kept here. Free entry. Downstairs to the crypt — dim light, columns and Saint Nicholas's sarcophagus. Go early for the morning service.

✨ From cliff to Adriatic — 30 minutes by train

Polignano a Mare

Cave beaches carved into Adriatic rock, crystal water, narrow white streets. 30 minutes from Bari by train. Very crowded in the afternoon — quiet between 8am and 10am. Lama Monachile cove is the finest photo spot. Free entry.

✨ Found nowhere else in the world — conical stone houses

Alberobello — Trulli village

1 hour 20 minutes from Bari by train. UNESCO World Heritage Site. White trulli houses with conical stone roofs — this architecture exists only here, in the Valle d'Itria. Rione Monti neighbourhood: hundreds of trulli side by side. Empty and impressive in the early morning hours. Easy day trip from Bari.

✨ Cave city — 9,000 years of settlement

Matera

About 1.5-2 hours from Bari by train and bus. UNESCO World Heritage Site and 2019 European Capital of Culture. Sassi (cave houses) carved into the rock face — people have lived here for 9,000 years. Rocky panorama facing the Gravina valley, cave churches, restaurants in stone streets. A day trip is possible from Bari but staying overnight is better.

✨ Castel del Monte — octagonal mystery

Castel del Monte

1 hour from Bari by car, near Andria on a hilltop. An octagonal castle from the 13th century — built by Holy Roman Emperor Frederick II. Why octagonal? The courtyard is octagonal, eight towers are octagonal, eight rooms on each floor. The answer is still debated. On the UNESCO list, a symbol of Puglia. The interior is relatively plain but the architecture and views are impressive. Ticketed.

✨ Piazza Mercantile — espresso at the heart of the morning

Bari Vecchia's main square. The city's trading and meeting point since the Middle Ages. Colonna della Giustizia (the Justice Column) stands in the centre. The cafés surrounding it fill with local businesspeople and shopkeepers between 7am and 9am. A €1 espresso, standing at the counter — this is Italy's most authentic morning ritual. Sit and watch: the square's energy captures Bari perfectly.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Bari hasn't yet surrendered to tourism; its traps are few, but the day-trip circuit has things worth knowing. These four suffice.

🍝 TRAP

Packaged 'handmade' orecchiette

The nonnas of Arco Basso truly roll pasta at their doorsteps; but some stalls sell factory pasta as 'handmade'.

💡 What to Do Instead: Buy from the stall where you watched it being made; fresh lasts two days refrigerated — ask for the dried kind to travel.
🦑 TRAP

Crudo di mare courage at a random spot

Raw seafood is a Bari tradition, but never from a stall waiting in the heat; don't let your stomach end the holiday early.

💡 What to Do Instead: Eat crudo at established addresses: the fishermen's own stand by the harbour or known ristorantes — fresh, on ice, safe.
🚌 TRAP

Arriving in Alberobello with the noon coach convoy

The trulli village becomes a tour-bus flood at midday; the fairytale set turns into a selfie queue.

💡 What to Do Instead: Take the first morning train or sleep there: at sunrise the trulli lanes are yours alone.
🚗 TRAP

Driving to Polignano, driving into the old town

Parking in Polignano is near-impossible in summer; steering a car into Bari Vecchia's lanes is a mistake in itself.

💡 What to Do Instead: The train to Polignano takes 20 minutes; in Bari, walk the old town — it's no bigger than your palm anyway.
🛡️ The golden rule

Bari lives with its doors open: the nonna's pasta table, the saint's basilica, the neighbours' street chat. Those who look for closed doors are tourists; those who walk through open ones become guests.
