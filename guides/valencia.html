Valencia

The birthplace of paella, a city shaped by oranges, and Spain's most underrated metropolis

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Valencia

Beach or history or architecture or food? Valencia is the city that doesn't make you choose. In a single day you can swim in the Mediterranean, get lost in medieval streets, find yourself surrounded by futuristic museums and sit down for lunch where the world's best paella was born. The Turia River running through the centre is the detail that completely changed Valencia's spirit: after the great flood of 1957, the riverbed was drained and turned into a 9-kilometre green park in the heart of the city. Today people cycle to work through it, run, spend time with their children here. Perhaps this is exactly what makes Valencia feel so good. Large but not exhausting, lively but not chaotic, and prices still feel reasonable compared to Spain's other big cities.

🗣️ Local Concept: L'Horta (lor-TA)

The fertile farmland surrounding Valencia. Orange groves, rice paddies, chestnut fields. Valencian paella comes from these lands: chicken, rabbit, fresh beans, olive oil and bomba rice (a short-grain local rice that absorbs liquid well). No seafood — real Valencian paella is the food of agricultural land. Knowing this distinction means eating the right thing in the right place in the city.

3 Unwritten Rules for Valencia

1

Paella is a lunch dish — not dinner

Valencians eat paella at lunchtime, between 2pm and 4pm. Restaurants serving paella in the evening are generally tourist traps — they're almost certainly reheating paella cooked that morning. Real paella preparation starts in the morning and takes an average of 40-50 minutes to cook. If you want paella, plan your lunch around it and warn the group early.

2

Don't drink orxata (horchata) from a supermarket

Orxata is the city's drink — a sweet, creamy cold drink made from chufa (tiger nuts similar to tubers). It has nothing in common with the pasteurised supermarket version. For fresh orxata, look for a horchatería with 'orxata artesana' on the sign. Horchatería Santa Catalina near the Mercado Central is a historic spot and one of the best. Order a farton alongside — a long, lightly sweet roll made for dipping.

3

Come in March for Las Fallas — but be ready for it

Las Fallas (12-19 March) is Valencia's UNESCO-listed festival — enormous sculpture groups are erected across the city and burned on the final night. Hotels charge two to three times normal prices during this period and the city is packed. But seeing Valencia this way is only possible in March — never at any other time.

Getting Around

Valencia Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Valencia is small and flat. Cycling is the best option for getting around. For metro and bus, use the SUMA card system.

Metro Line 3 or Line 5 takes you from the airport to the city centre in about 20 minutes. Ticket €5.60 — airport surcharge included. Taxi to the centre around €20-25. Metro is easily the most practical option. SUMA card is valid on the airport line but the airport surcharge is paid separately.

5 metro lines and 4 tram lines under the Metrovalencia network. SUMA card (card itself €1.10) valid on all lines. Single fare approximately €1.50, 10-trip SUMA for Zone A €8.90. Runs from 5am to 1am, 24 hours on Friday and Saturday nights. The centre, Ruzafa, beach and City of Arts and Sciences are all reachable by metro or tram.

Over 200 routes covering every corner of the city. Same SUMA card system applies. Cash payment on the bus is possible but slightly more expensive (€2). Night routes run after the metro closes. Bus is the most practical option for El Cabanyal and some coastal points.

275 stations and 2,750 bikes across the city. Annual subscription €25, daily €3.60. First 30 minutes free, hourly rate after that. Payment by contactless bank card at the station. Ideal for cycling along the Turia Gardens, to the beaches and around Ruzafa. The flat city makes it easy going.

Valencia's centre is small and walkable. From the Mercado Central: Cathedral 5 minutes, Turia Gardens entrance 10 minutes, Ruzafa 20 minutes. City of Arts and Sciences is 45 minutes on foot from the centre — long but a beautiful route through the Turia Gardens. Choose shaded routes on hot summer days.

⚡ Pro Tip

24 hours €15, 48 hours €20, 72 hours €25. Unlimited transport plus free entry to municipal museums. City of Arts and Sciences has a 15% discount — not fully free. Worth it if you're visiting several museums and moving around a lot. If you're only using the metro, the 10-trip SUMA card makes more sense.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Valencia

Valencia's best corners are usually two streets away or just outside the city.

Estación del Norte

A Modernist train station built in 1917 — one of Valencia's most important architectural buildings, yet most visitors walk past without going inside. Stained glass windows, ceramic panels and ironwork decorated with orange blossom, orange trees and Valencian symbols. Valencia's agricultural culture is embedded in this building's walls. Go inside even if you're not catching a train.

Jardines del Turia

After the 1957 flood disaster, the Turia riverbed was drained and turned into a 9-kilometre green corridor running right through the heart of the city. Cycling paths, walking trails, playgrounds and Gulliver Park all along this corridor. Valencians run here, cycle, have picnics. Completely free.

Albufera Lake

20 kilometres south of Valencia, a freshwater lake separated from the Mediterranean by natural sand barriers. A 21,000-hectare natural park. El Palmar village sits in the middle of the lake and can be explored by wooden boat. At sunset the lake turns red — a spectacular sight. Just 40 minutes by bus from the city centre.

El Carmen neighbourhood

Narrow streets enclosed by medieval walls inside Ciutat Vella. Artist studios, alternative cafés and street art by day; bars and live music venues by night. Torres de Serranos offers one of the city's finest panoramas — free. Quietest in the early morning hours; nightlife peaks on Friday and Saturday.

La Lonja de la Seda (Silk Exchange)

Built between 1482 and 1548, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. One of Spain's finest examples of late Gothic architecture. The spiral columns and vaulted ceiling of the Salon de Contratación tell the story of Valencia's golden age of trade. 3 minutes' walk from the Mercado Central, with a fraction of the crowds.

Bioparc Valencia

At the western end of the Turia Gardens. Not a traditional caged zoo — all animals are in open areas that replicate their natural habitats, at the same observation level as visitors. Gorillas, chimpanzees, elephants and hippos observed this way is a different experience altogether. Valencia's finest activity for children, though adults are equally captivated.
