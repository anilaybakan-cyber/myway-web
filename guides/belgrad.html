Belgrade

The Balkan capital where two rivers meet and nights never end

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Belgrade

Belgrade wins you over with character, not beauty: from Kalemegdan's ramparts you watch the Danube and Sava merge, accordion music rises from Skadarlija's cobblestones, and on the river's floating splavovi the night runs into morning. This city has been destroyed forty times and rebuilt forty-one; it doesn't hide its weariness, but it never hosts a guest halfway either. In the kafana the rakija arrives unbidden, the stories stretch, the bill comes in lower than you feared — Belgrade is one of Europe's least polished and most generous capitals.

🗣️ Local Concept: Merak (MEH-rak)

Spelled like the Turkish word, but in Serbian it means something else entirely: the deep, unhurried pleasure taken in small things. Savoring a coffee, a conversation, a long look at the river — all of it is merak. A Belgrader lives 'uz merak': slowly, fully, without show. It is the key word for understanding this city.

3 Unwritten Rules for Belgrade

1

Climb Kalemegdan at sunset

The fortress is open at all hours and free, but its real performance is at dusk: from beneath the Pobednik statue, the meeting of the two rivers turns crimson. Bring something along and sit on the ramparts — Belgrade's best 'ticket' costs nothing.

2

Honor kafana etiquette: never hurry, accept the offer

A kafana is not a restaurant but an institution: the table is yours for hours, and if the music is live, requesting a song is allowed. If rakija is offered, don't refuse — say 'živeli' and make eye contact. The custom is taking turns paying, not splitting the bill.

3

Know both banks: the old town + Zemun

Belgrade is not only Stari Grad: Zemun on the Danube carries the soul of a separate town — the Gardoš Tower, fish restaurants, quayside swans. Give Zemun an afternoon; the city's Austro-Hungarian past lives there.

Getting Around

Belgrade Public Transit & Airport Transfers

The center is walkable; trams weave the city and app taxis are cheap — and the riverbanks are the loveliest 'lines' of all.

✈️

Arriving from the airport

From Nikola Tesla Airport the A1 minibus reaches Slavija Square in half an hour; bus 72 is cheaper but slower. For a taxi, take a voucher from the official desk inside the terminal — ignore the freelancers at the door.

🚋

Trams and buses

The red trams stitch together the old town and the riverbanks: line 2 loops around Kalemegdan. Sort tickets via the app or the driver — inspections are rare, but it's the custom.

🚶

A walkable center

From Knez Mihailova to Kalemegdan, Skadarlija to Republic Square, everything is steps apart. Dorćol's grid streets are flat and Kalemegdan a gentle climb — comfortable shoes are enough.

📱

CarGo and taxis

App-based CarGo is Belgrade's Uber: cheap, haggle-free, everywhere. In a street taxi, watch that the meter runs — for late-night returns the app is always the cleaner option.

🚲

Riverside walking and cycling

The Sava promenade runs from Beton Hala to Ada Ciganlija; Zemun's quay stretches for kilometers along the Danube. Renting a bike and tracing both rivers in one loop is Belgrade's finest sport.

💡 Zemun tip

Reach Zemun by bus 83 or the riverside walk: climb the Gardoš Tower in the late-afternoon light, then have fish soup on the quay below. Save the return for evening — sunset over the Danube turns the trip itself into a gift.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Belgrade: Best Areas

In Belgrade the choice is a choice of atmosphere: the old town's walkability, Dorćol's bohemian rhythm, or Zemun's small-town calm.

Stari Grad (Old Town)

Most central

Around Knez Mihailova and Republic Square: Kalemegdan, museums and cafes within steps. The natural base for a first visit — you fan out everywhere on foot.

📍 Walk to the fortress • Pedestrian street • Cafe density

Dorćol

Bohemian rhythm

Third-wave coffee shops, design stores and night bars along grid-planned streets: Belgrade's young, creative face. Neighboring the river and the fortress — the quarter for atmosphere seekers.

📍 Coffee streets • Night bars • Near the river

Around Skadarlija

Kafana side

Surrounding the cobbled bohemian lane: accordion, tambura and kafana tables in the evenings. The deep end of atmosphere for night owls — light sleepers should drift one street in.

📍 Kafana tables • Live music • Bohemian history

Savamala and the quay

The new face

The transforming Sava riverfront: modern residence hotels, a waterfront promenade and gallery venues. Near the bus and train stations — for those curious about the new Belgrade.

📍 Waterfront walk • Modern hotels • Near transport

Zemun

Small-town calm

The old Austro-Hungarian town on the Danube: the slopes of Gardoš, fish restaurants, quayside mornings. Fifteen minutes to the center by bus — serenity and character in one.

📍 Danube quay • Gardoš slopes • Fish restaurants
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Belgrade

Belgrade holds kafana warmth in every season; spring and autumn belong to river walks, summer to splav nights, winter to rakija conversations.

Spring

Apr – Jun
Kalemegdan Spring

Kalemegdan park turns green, the riverside cafes throw their tables outdoors, and the city sheds its winter introversion. The weather is made for walking and the crowds are thin — the window for unhurried decoding of Belgrade.

Summer

Jul – Aug
Splav Season

Summer Belgrade moves to the water: Ada Ciganlija's beach by day, the Sava's splavovi by night. Hot noons belong to kafana shade, evenings to the quays. The city doesn't leave for vacation — it vacations on its rivers.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Golden Kafana Light

Kalemegdan turns gold, chestnut smoke drifts down Knez Mihailova, the kafanas move indoors. September extends summer on the riverbanks; October and November are the city at its most photogenic and most honest.

Winter

Dec – Mar
Rakija Warmth

When snow falls, Kalemegdan grows still, kafana windows fog over, and conversation and rakija thicken. A Belgrade winter can be harsh, but the indoors are always warm — the city's hospitality shines brightest in this season.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Belgrade

The Serbian table is built on generosity: grill smoke, kajmak, fresh bread and rakija that never leaves the table — portions are taken seriously.

🥩

Pljeskavica

The giant Serbian take on the grilled patty: in lepinja flatbread with kajmak and ajvar. The 'punjena' version, stuffed with cheese, is a feast — this is Belgrade's answer to the hamburger, and the answer wins on weight.

⭐ Grill classic · kajmak + ajvar
🍲

Kafana classics

Karađorđeva schnitzel (a kajmak-filled roll), sarma, prebranac baked beans: kafana cooking is home food at monumental scale. Start with the soup of the day — the portions welcome sharing.

⭐ Kafana table · serious portions
🥧

Burek and yoghurt morning

Meat or cheese burek from the pekara (bakery) counter, with drinkable yoghurt alongside: the cornerstone of a Belgrade breakfast. The morning tray is freshest — burek left past noon doesn't count as burek.

⭐ Pekara work · with drinkable yoghurt
🐟

Riblja čorba in Zemun

A dark, paprika-rich soup of Danube fish: the signature of the Zemun quay. Continue with fried carp or pike — the river view is the recipe's secret ingredient.

⭐ Zemun signature · at a quay table
🥃

The rakija ritual

Šljivovica from plums, dunja from quince, kajsija from apricots: rakija is Serbia's national opening and closing act. One glass before the meal, 'živeli' and eye contact — if you see 'domaća' (homemade), don't hesitate.

⭐ Šljivovica first · with živeli
🍰

Poslastičarnica sweets

Moskva šnit cake, krempita and tulumba: Belgrade's sweet shops are the confectionery alliance of Austria and the Ottomans. A slice with afternoon coffee — the city's most nostalgic pause.

⭐ Sweet-shop classic · with coffee
🍷 Table rule

In Belgrade the portions are built for two and the hospitality is sincere: order little and reorder if needed. Choose a kafana where the music is live and the tables are full of locals — a kafana with a tourist menu is not a kafana.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Belgrade

Everyone sees Kalemegdan; these six are Belgrade's river-scented, story-filled hidden pages.

✨ Tower over the Danube

Gardoš Tower (Zemun)

The millennium tower atop Zemun's cobbled slopes: the Danube's bend, tiled roofs and fishing boats at your feet. Climb in the late-afternoon light — Belgrade's calmest view lives here.

✨ The city's river beach

Ada Ciganlija

The peninsula-island on the Sava: a beach in summer, a running and cycling circuit year-round, lakeside cafes. This is the Belgrader's 'seaside' — half the city is here on weekends, so go midweek.

✨ Golden mosaics below ground

The Crypt of St. Sava

Beneath one of the world's largest Orthodox churches lies the crypt level: golden mosaics, chandeliers and candlelight. If the upper hall is grand, the lower one is a miracle — most visitors never learn there are stairs down.

✨ The last Ottoman mosque

Bajrakli Mosque and Dorćol

The Bajrakli Mosque of 1575 stands quietly in Dorćol's side streets: the last witness of Belgrade's Ottoman centuries. A walk through the surrounding quarter summarizes the city's layers in half an hour.

✨ The bohemians' last lane

Skadarlija at golden hour

The cobbled lane is a different kind of beautiful before the dinner crowds: painters' stalls, the fountain, the facades of old inns. Walk it at golden hour, stay for a kafana table at night — see both of its faces.

✨ The socialist skyline's crown

Genex Tower and Novi Beograd

The brutalist icon of the Genex Tower and the blocks of Novi Beograd: socialist Yugoslavia's concrete utopia. The city's other face for architecture lovers — pass by tram, hop off for photographs.

✨ River strategy

Belgrade's secret lies along its two riverbanks: coffee on the Sava quay in the morning, a swim or walk at Ada at noon, the Danube at Zemun in the afternoon, and the confluence from Kalemegdan at dusk. Follow the water — the city tells its story along the shore.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Belgrade is an honest city, but the airport door and the tourist street play the same games as everywhere; know these four.

🚕 TRAP

The airport pirate taxi

The men approaching with 'taxi, taxi' at the terminal exit charge three or four times the official tariff; the car is usually meterless.

💡 What to Do Instead: Take a zone-priced voucher from the official taxi desk inside the terminal, or call a CarGo; the A1 minibus is the cleanest budget option. Never negotiate at the door — the system with a queue always wins.
💱 TRAP

Exchange offices with creative rates

Some menjačnicas in tourist zones run rates that look attractive but hide a buried commission; the loss grows with the amount.

💡 What to Do Instead: Compare rates at a few offices and ask 'any commission?'; withdraw larger sums in dinars from a bank ATM. A shopkeeper offering to charge in euros is almost never doing you a favor.
🎻 TRAP

The Skadarlija music bill

The accordionists who come to your table expect payment after a few songs; the unknowing tourist meets a surprise beside the bill.

💡 What to Do Instead: If you want the music, set your tip willingly and up front (there's a per-song custom); if not, smile and thank them kindly. If the kafana has its own band, ask about a cover charge as you sit down.
🍽️ TRAP

The 'national restaurant' with a tourist menu

Some windows on Knez Mihailova sell a mediocre table at high prices under a 'national food' label; the kafana soul stays on the sign.

💡 What to Do Instead: Walk a street or two inland: choose the kafana full of locals, with a handwritten menu and a daily soup. The words 'domaća kuhinja' (home cooking) and full lunchtime tables are the most honest signs.
🛡️ Golden rule

In Belgrade sincerity is the strongest currency: a few Serbian words, a smile and an unhurried manner open every door. When in doubt, ask a local where they eat — advice is never withheld in this city, and it often returns to your table as rakija.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Belgrade

Serbian sounds tough but has a warm heart — and shares hundreds of Ottoman-era words with Turkish; these five open tables.

🗣️ Zdravo
Hello — the friendly greeting that works at any hour
🗣️ Hvala
Thank you — in the kafana, at the counter, on the tram; your most-used word
🗣️ Molim
Please / you're welcome / pardon — courtesy's multi-purpose key
🗣️ Živeli!
Cheers! — eye contact required as the rakija rises; the seal of the Serbian table
🗣️ Prijatno
Enjoy your meal — said to anyone sitting down; the greeting of kafana etiquette
