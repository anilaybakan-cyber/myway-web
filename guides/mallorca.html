Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Mallorca

Mallorca is Spain's largest Balearic Island and one of Europe's most visited holiday destinations. But those numbers only apply to the coastline — inland there's a completely different Mallorca. The UNESCO-listed villages of the Tramuntana mountains, stone roads among olive groves, terraced orange orchards. Palma is a city in its own right: La Seu Cathedral, historic neighbourhoods, Santa Catalina's market and gastronomy. The island is large and getting around without a car becomes difficult — but with the right planning it's possible to see beach, villages and mountains on the same trip.

🗣️ Local Concept: Ensaimada (en-sa-i-MA-da)

Mallorca's emblem — a spiral-shaped soft pastry made with lard (saïm). Available plain, with honey or filled with various fillings. Fresh from the oven every morning. Coming to Mallorca without eating an ensaimada is like going to Paris without a croissant. Found fresh at Palma's historic bakeries or at Mercat de l'Olivar. Also sold in large gift boxes to take home — but the best version is warm, straight from the oven.

3 Unwritten Rules for Mallorca

1

Hire a car — without one the island is only half-seen

Palma is manageable by public transport but the island's finest places — Tramuntana villages, hidden coves, inland villages — are very hard to reach without a car. Car hire is affordable with early booking. Last-minute bookings in midsummer are both expensive and may find no availability.

2

In July-August flee the coast and go inland

In midsummer, resort areas and popular beaches are very crowded. But at the same time, the Tramuntana villages, Palma's historic centre and inland towns are much quieter. If you want beach, head there very early in the morning or try the lesser-known coves on the east coast.

3

Explore Palma too — it's not just about the beach

Many visitors settle in a resort and leave without ever visiting Palma. This is a great loss. With La Seu Cathedral, Bellver Castle, Santa Catalina neighbourhood, Mercat de l'Olivar and its historic streets, Palma is a city well worth visiting in its own right. Set aside at least half a day, ideally a full day.

Getting Around

Mallorca Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Palma is manageable by public transport. For the island TIB buses and trains are available, but car hire is the most flexible option.

EMT bus Line A1 runs from the airport to Palma centre (Plaça d'Espanya) every 15 minutes, about 20 minutes, €5. Fixed-price taxi approximately €25. TIB AeroTib buses provide direct connections to the island's other resort areas (Alcúdia, Magaluf).

Palma's blue buses cover every point in the city. Single fare €1.50 (Line 1 port route €3), paid on board or by card. 10-trip card €10. Bellver Castle, Es Baluard Museum, Santa Catalina and Passeig Marítim are all reachable by EMT. Palma metro line M1 goes to the university — limited tourist use.

From Palma's Plaça d'Espanya, SFM trains go to Inca, Sa Pobla and Manacor. The Sóller Train (since 1912) is separate and historic — breathtaking mountain crossing between Palma and Sóller, return approximately €20. Train is a good option for day trips to the island's interior. Ideal for the large Sunday market in Sineu.

Tramuntana villages, hidden coves, Cap de Formentor — all require a car. Affordable with early booking, expensive last-minute in summer. No need to drive in Palma itself; hiring a car is most practical when leaving Palma. Navigation is essential — some roads are narrow.

Palma is cycle-friendly — the cycling path along Passeig Marítim is excellent. BiciPalma (city bike sharing) stations everywhere. Scooter hire is also widespread. Mallorca is also a paradise for professional cyclists.

⚡ Pro Tip

Historic wooden train from Palma to Sóller: 10:10am or 10:50am departures are recommended. From Sóller, the tram down to Port de Sóller. Lunch at Port de Sóller, then a short walk through Sóller's orange groves on the return. Buy tickets online in advance — sells out in summer. Sóller Train: €20 return. Tram separately ~€2.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Mallorca

Mallorca's finest corners are not far from the coastal resorts — just a car and a little curiosity are enough.

Sóller Train

Historic wooden train from Palma to Sóller — the same rails and carriages since 1912. It crosses the Tramuntana mountains through 13 tunnels, leaping across viaducts. About 1 hour. After arriving in Sóller, the tram running since 1913 takes you down to Port de Sóller. Buy tickets in advance — sells out. Return approximately €20. One of the island's most unforgettable experiences.

Deià

A small village in the Tramuntana mountains, looking down to the Mediterranean. Where British poet and author Robert Graves lived from 1929 until his death. His house is now a museum. The village itself is small, stone-streeted and panoramic. Quiet in the early morning hours. A small path leads to nearby Cala Deià cove — descend carefully, a beautiful cove awaits.

Sa Calobra

On the north coast, a legendary 13km winding road descending from the Tramuntana mountains. The road itself is an experience — winding, narrow, breathtaking views. At the bottom, a small cove and Torrent de Pareis (a deep rock canyon). Very crowded in midsummer afternoons — go early in the morning or late afternoon. Limited car parking; coming by bike is possible but demanding.

Fornalutx

4 kilometres above Sóller, one of the Tramuntana's most impressive villages. Honey-coloured stone houses, stepped streets, lemon and orange groves. Population 600. Tourism but peaceful. It's also possible to walk from Sóller — about 1 hour. Lunch at a small restaurant here is one of Mallorca's most local and finest moments.

Cala Mondragó Natural Park

On the south-east coast, near Santanyí. Two small coves within a natural park — much quieter and more sheltered than crowded beach areas. Walking trails, pine forests and clear turquoise water. Reachable by bus or car. Paid car park. An early start is recommended in midsummer. Nearby Cala Figuera fishing village is ideal for fresh fish.

Bellver Castle

Just west of Palma city centre, on a pine-forested hill. A circular plan from the 14th century — one of Europe's rare round castles. Houses the Palma History Museum inside. But the real reason: from the terrace, Palma bay and the whole city are visible 360 degrees. Ticketed but affordable. Reachable by EMT bus. Open-air concerts on summer evenings.
