Mallorca

Behind the crowded beaches, villages, olive groves and mountains that have kept their authenticity are still hiding

Overview

The Vibe of Mallorca

Mallorca is Spain's largest Balearic Island and one of Europe's most visited holiday destinations. But those numbers only apply to the coastline — inland there's a completely different Mallorca. The UNESCO-listed villages of the Tramuntana mountains, stone roads among olive groves, terraced orange orchards. Palma is a city in its own right: La Seu Cathedral, historic neighbourhoods, Santa Catalina's market and gastronomy. The island is large and getting around without a car becomes difficult — but with the right planning it's possible to see beach, villages and mountains on the same trip.

💬 Ensaimada
🗣️ /en-sa-i-MA-da/

Mallorca's emblem — a spiral-shaped soft pastry made with lard (saïm). Available plain, with honey or filled with various fillings. Fresh from the oven every morning. Coming to Mallorca without eating an ensaimada is like going to Paris without a croissant. Found fresh at Palma's historic bakeries or at Mercat de l'Olivar. Also sold in large gift boxes to take home — but the best version is warm, straight from the oven.

Insider Advice

Unwritten Rules for Mallorca

#1
Hire a car — without one the island is only half-seen Palma is manageable by public transport but the island's finest places — Tramuntana villages, hidden coves, inland villages — are very hard to reach without a car. Car hire is affordable with early booking. Last-minute bookings in midsummer are both expensive and may find no availability.
#2
In July-August flee the coast and go inland In midsummer, resort areas and popular beaches are very crowded. But at the same time, the Tramuntana villages, Palma's historic centre and inland towns are much quieter. If you want beach, head there very early in the morning or try the lesser-known coves on the east coast.
#3
Explore Palma too — it's not just about the beach Many visitors settle in a resort and leave without ever visiting Palma. This is a great loss. With La Seu Cathedral, Bellver Castle, Santa Catalina neighbourhood, Mercat de l'Olivar and its historic streets, Palma is a city well worth visiting in its own right. Set aside at least half a day, ideally a full day.
Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Mallorca

💎 HIDDEN GEM
Sóller Train
Historic wooden train from Palma to Sóller — the same rails and carriages since 1912. It crosses the Tramuntana mountains through 13 tunnels, leaping across viaducts. About 1 hour. After arriving in Sóller, the tram running since 1913 takes you down to Port de Sóller. Buy tickets in advance — sells out. Return approximately €20. One of the island's most unforgettable experiences.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Deià
A small village in the Tramuntana mountains, looking down to the Mediterranean. Where British poet and author Robert Graves lived from 1929 until his death. His house is now a museum. The village itself is small, stone-streeted and panoramic. Quiet in the early morning hours. A small path leads to nearby Cala Deià cove — descend carefully, a beautiful cove awaits.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Sa Calobra
On the north coast, a legendary 13km winding road descending from the Tramuntana mountains. The road itself is an experience — winding, narrow, breathtaking views. At the bottom, a small cove and Torrent de Pareis (a deep rock canyon). Very crowded in midsummer afternoons — go early in the morning or late afternoon. Limited car parking; coming by bike is possible but demanding.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Fornalutx
4 kilometres above Sóller, one of the Tramuntana's most impressive villages. Honey-coloured stone houses, stepped streets, lemon and orange groves. Population 600. Tourism but peaceful. It's also possible to walk from Sóller — about 1 hour. Lunch at a small restaurant here is one of Mallorca's most local and finest moments.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Cala Mondragó Natural Park
On the south-east coast, near Santanyí. Two small coves within a natural park — much quieter and more sheltered than crowded beach areas. Walking trails, pine forests and clear turquoise water. Reachable by bus or car. Paid car park. An early start is recommended in midsummer. Nearby Cala Figuera fishing village is ideal for fresh fish.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Bellver Castle
Just west of Palma city centre, on a pine-forested hill. A circular plan from the 14th century — one of Europe's rare round castles. Houses the Palma History Museum inside. But the real reason: from the terrace, Palma bay and the whole city are visible 360 degrees. Ticketed but affordable. Reachable by EMT bus. Open-air concerts on summer evenings.
Food & Flavors

What to Taste

Mallorca's cuisine is the simple, delicious face of the Mediterranean — olive oil, vegetables, pork and fresh seafood. Avoid tourist restaurants, step into the local markets.

🍴 MUST TASTE
Ensaimada
Mallorca's spiral pastry, fresh from the oven every morning. Available plain, with honey or filled (apricot jam, sobrasada, whipped cream). At Palma's historic bakeries or Mercat de l'Olivar for breakfast, €1.50-3. Brushed with egg yolk, dusted with icing sugar — as beautiful to look at as it is to eat.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Sobrasada
Mallorca's soft pork sausage paste — raw sausage cured with red pepper, salt and spices. Eaten with pa amb oli, spread on bread or cooked. Available everywhere across the island. Quality varies greatly between producers — much tastier from small local producers or village markets.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Tumbet
Mallorca's take on ratatouille but different — aubergine, courgette, potato and tomato sauce baked in layers in the oven. Fried vegetables, olive oil and garlic. Served both as a main and as a side dish. Most authentic in local restaurants in inland villages.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Pa amb oli
Bread rubbed with tomato, olive oil and salt — Mallorca's simplest and most satisfying dish. Sobrasada or cheese added on top. The island's cheapest and most local flavour. The quality difference is immediately apparent — fresh tomato and good olive oil change everything.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Fresh fish — in Santa Catalina
Palma's Santa Catalina neighbourhood is the address for the island's best fresh seafood and fish restaurants. Fish stalls at Mercat de Santa Catalina in the mornings. The small restaurants nearby are both fresh and reasonably priced. Dorado (sea bream), llampuga (mahi-mahi, September-November season) and fresh prawns are the island's standout choices.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Mallorca olive oil and wine
The island produces small quantities of high-quality olive oil and wine. Red wines from the Binissalem and Pla i Llevant DO areas are particularly good — the local Manto Negro grape. For olive oil, go to inland estates or Mercat de l'Olivar. Wine tasting at small bodegas around Manacor and Felanitx is possible.
Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

❌
The party strips are the island's exception, not its shop window; knowing Mallorca from there is like knowing a country from its airport.
✅ Better Alternative: Point your route at the Tramuntana villages, Palma's old town and the southern coves; that's where the island actually is.
❌
At seaside restaurants the 'catch of the day' sells by weight; fish ordered without asking becomes a surprise on the bill.
✅ Better Alternative: Ask the weight and price before ordering; or choose the locals' fish quarter around Santa Catalina.
❌
One of the world's twistiest roads becomes a bus convoy and parking war at noon.
✅ Better Alternative: Go at first light, or approach by boat from Port de Sóller: the same fjord, zero hairpin stress.
❌
The mass-produced last-minute ensaimada is not even a shadow of the one from the oven.
✅ Better Alternative: Buy it fresh from a historic Palma bakery; if it's travelling home, the bakeries provide travel boxes too.
