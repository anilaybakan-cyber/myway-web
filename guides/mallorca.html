Mallorca

Behind the crowded beaches, villages, olive groves and mountains that have kept their authenticity are still hiding

Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Mallorca

Mallorca is Spain's largest Balearic Island and one of Europe's most visited holiday destinations. But those numbers only apply to the coastline — inland there's a completely different Mallorca. The UNESCO-listed villages of the Tramuntana mountains, stone roads among olive groves, terraced orange orchards. Palma is a city in its own right: La Seu Cathedral, historic neighbourhoods, Santa Catalina's market and gastronomy. The island is large and getting around without a car becomes difficult — but with the right planning it's possible to see beach, villages and mountains on the same trip.

🗣️ Local Concept: Ensaimada (en-sa-i-MA-da)

Mallorca's emblem — a spiral-shaped soft pastry made with lard (saïm). Available plain, with honey or filled with various fillings. Fresh from the oven every morning. Coming to Mallorca without eating an ensaimada is like going to Paris without a croissant. Found fresh at Palma's historic bakeries or at Mercat de l'Olivar. Also sold in large gift boxes to take home — but the best version is warm, straight from the oven.

3 Unwritten Rules for Mallorca

1

Hire a car — without one the island is only half-seen

Palma is manageable by public transport but the island's finest places — Tramuntana villages, hidden coves, inland villages — are very hard to reach without a car. Car hire is affordable with early booking. Last-minute bookings in midsummer are both expensive and may find no availability.

2

In July-August flee the coast and go inland

In midsummer, resort areas and popular beaches are very crowded. But at the same time, the Tramuntana villages, Palma's historic centre and inland towns are much quieter. If you want beach, head there very early in the morning or try the lesser-known coves on the east coast.

3

Explore Palma too — it's not just about the beach

Many visitors settle in a resort and leave without ever visiting Palma. This is a great loss. With La Seu Cathedral, Bellver Castle, Santa Catalina neighbourhood, Mercat de l'Olivar and its historic streets, Palma is a city well worth visiting in its own right. Set aside at least half a day, ideally a full day.

Getting Around

Mallorca Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Palma is manageable by public transport. For the island TIB buses and trains are available, but car hire is the most flexible option.

✈️ Airport → Palma centre

EMT bus Line A1 runs from the airport to Palma centre (Plaça d'Espanya) every 15 minutes, about 20 minutes, €5. Fixed-price taxi approximately €25. TIB AeroTib buses provide direct connections to the island's other resort areas (Alcúdia, Magaluf).

🚌 EMT — Palma city buses

Palma's blue buses cover every point in the city. Single fare €1.50 (Line 1 port route €3), paid on board or by card. 10-trip card €10. Bellver Castle, Es Baluard Museum, Santa Catalina and Passeig Marítim are all reachable by EMT. Palma metro line M1 goes to the university — limited tourist use.

🚂 SFM train — into the island

From Palma's Plaça d'Espanya, SFM trains go to Inca, Sa Pobla and Manacor. The Sóller Train (since 1912) is separate and historic — breathtaking mountain crossing between Palma and Sóller, return approximately €20. Train is a good option for day trips to the island's interior. Ideal for the large Sunday market in Sineu.

🚗 Car hire — essential for the island

Tramuntana villages, hidden coves, Cap de Formentor — all require a car. Affordable with early booking, expensive last-minute in summer. No need to drive in Palma itself; hiring a car is most practical when leaving Palma. Navigation is essential — some roads are narrow.

🚲 Bicycle and scooter

Palma is cycle-friendly — the cycling path along Passeig Marítim is excellent. BiciPalma (city bike sharing) stations everywhere. Scooter hire is also widespread. Mallorca is also a paradise for professional cyclists.

💡 Sóller Train + Tram combination

Historic wooden train from Palma to Sóller: 10:10am or 10:50am departures are recommended. From Sóller, the tram down to Port de Sóller. Lunch at Port de Sóller, then a short walk through Sóller's orange groves on the return. Buy tickets online in advance — sells out in summer. Sóller Train: €20 return. Tram separately ~€2.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Mallorca: Best Areas

Location is critical in Mallorca — Palma is both urban and central, Tramuntana villages are authentic but require a car, coastal areas are crowded.

Palma — historic centre Most central

Walking distance to La Seu Cathedral, Bellver Castle, Mercat de l'Olivar and Santa Catalina neighbourhood. La Lonja and El Born neighbourhoods are Palma's liveliest spots. Ideal for exploring the city without a car. Boutique hotels in restored historic buildings are characterful but small.

Close to La Seu Cathedral • Santa Catalina neighbourhood • Everything walkable
Deià — the jewel of the Tramuntana Most authentic

A hilltop village in the Tramuntana mountains, looking out over the sea. Where Robert Graves lived and wrote. A magnet for artists, writers and musicians. Hotels and fincas are expensive but the atmosphere is unmatched. 30 minutes from Palma by car. Everything small-scale and authentic.

Robert Graves' village • Tramuntana atmosphere • Artists' village
Sóller — orange grove town Most characterful

A lovely town in the Tramuntana valley among orange groves. Connected to Palma by historic train, with a tram down to Port de Sóller. Compact and walkable centre. Close to Palma but peaceful. Ideal position for seeing both mountains and sea.

Historic train connection • Orange groves • Tram to the harbour
Alcúdia — north coast Family holiday

Versatile with both a walled historic town and a wide sandy beach. Playa de Muro and Alcúdia beaches are long and clean. The walled inner town is small but impressive. Somewhat quieter than the southern resorts. Sunday market is the largest open-air market in the area.

Walled historic town • Long sandy beach • Family-friendly
Santanyí — south coast Most elegant

An elegant small town in the south-east with golden stone buildings. Surrounded by Es Trenc beach (the island's finest sand) and Cala Mondragó natural park. Artistic market on Wednesdays and Saturdays. Away from crowds, quality restaurants and boutiques.

Close to Es Trenc • Wednesday–Saturday market • Golden stone architecture
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Mallorca

Mallorca can be visited year-round — summer is hot and crowded, spring and autumn are the most balanced.

Spring Apr – Jun
The best time

April and May are Mallorca's golden period: temperatures 18-24°C, no crowds, prices reasonable. Tramuntana walking trails are ideal. It starts filling up from mid-June but still far from the July-August crowds. The sea is a little cool but swimming is possible.

Summer Jun – Aug
Crowded but lively

July and August coastal resorts are packed. But the Tramuntana mountains and inland villages are still quiet at the same time. The sea is at its finest, evenings are long and lively. To see hidden coves in the very early morning hours, an early start is needed. June is more manageable.

Autumn Sep – Nov
Second best time

September and October see the crowds go, the weather stays warm and the sea is still swimmable. Olive and orange harvests begin. Autumn markets and fairs in inland villages. Rain increases in November but prices drop significantly. Tramuntana walking trails are excellent in autumn too.

Winter Dec – Feb
Quiet and green

In late January and early February, almond trees blossom in Mallorca's interior. The mountains are covered in pink and white. Well worth a special visit for this scenery. Temperatures 10-15°C, occasionally rainy. Most resorts are closed but Palma, Sóller and inland towns keep going. Prices very low.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Mallorca

Mallorca's cuisine is the simple, delicious face of the Mediterranean — olive oil, vegetables, pork and fresh seafood. Avoid tourist restaurants, step into the local markets.

🥐 Ensaimada

Mallorca's spiral pastry, fresh from the oven every morning. Available plain, with honey or filled (apricot jam, sobrasada, whipped cream). At Palma's historic bakeries or Mercat de l'Olivar for breakfast, €1.50-3. Brushed with egg yolk, dusted with icing sugar — as beautiful to look at as it is to eat.

⭐ Palma bakeries · Mercat de l'Olivar · fresh in the morning · €1.50–3
🥩 Sobrasada

Mallorca's soft pork sausage paste — raw sausage cured with red pepper, salt and spices. Eaten with pa amb oli, spread on bread or cooked. Available everywhere across the island. Quality varies greatly between producers — much tastier from small local producers or village markets.

⭐ Local markets and shops · everywhere · look out for quality
🥘 Tumbet

Mallorca's take on ratatouille but different — aubergine, courgette, potato and tomato sauce baked in layers in the oven. Fried vegetables, olive oil and garlic. Served both as a main and as a side dish. Most authentic in local restaurants in inland villages.

⭐ Local restaurants · inland villages · €10-16
🍞 Pa amb oli

Bread rubbed with tomato, olive oil and salt — Mallorca's simplest and most satisfying dish. Sobrasada or cheese added on top. The island's cheapest and most local flavour. The quality difference is immediately apparent — fresh tomato and good olive oil change everything.

⭐ Everywhere · morning–lunch · €4–8 · try the sobrasada version
🐟 Fresh fish — in Santa Catalina

Palma's Santa Catalina neighbourhood is the address for the island's best fresh seafood and fish restaurants. Fish stalls at Mercat de Santa Catalina in the mornings. The small restaurants nearby are both fresh and reasonably priced. Dorado (sea bream), llampuga (mahi-mahi, September-November season) and fresh prawns are the island's standout choices.

⭐ Santa Catalina neighbourhood · Mercat de Santa Catalina · lunch–dinner
🫒 Mallorca olive oil and wine

The island produces small quantities of high-quality olive oil and wine. Red wines from the Binissalem and Pla i Llevant DO areas are particularly good — the local Manto Negro grape. For olive oil, go to inland estates or Mercat de l'Olivar. Wine tasting at small bodegas around Manacor and Felanitx is possible.

⭐ Mercat de l'Olivar · bodegas · book ahead for tastings
🍷 Mercat de l'Olivar — Palma's kitchen

In Palma centre, at Plaça de l'Olivar. Fresh fish, meat, vegetables, fruit, cheese, charcuterie and prepared foods. Tapas bars and small restaurants inside too. Open 7am-2pm, closed Sundays. Go early — the fish stalls close towards midday. Ideal for both shopping and breakfast. Mercat de Santa Catalina is a younger, more modernised alternative nearby.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Mallorca

Mallorca's finest corners are not far from the coastal resorts — just a car and a little curiosity are enough.

Sóller Train The world's finest mountain train journey — since 1912

Historic wooden train from Palma to Sóller — the same rails and carriages since 1912. It crosses the Tramuntana mountains through 13 tunnels, leaping across viaducts. About 1 hour. After arriving in Sóller, the tram running since 1913 takes you down to Port de Sóller. Buy tickets in advance — sells out. Return approximately €20. One of the island's most unforgettable experiences.

Deià Robert Graves' village — touristy but still magical

A small village in the Tramuntana mountains, looking down to the Mediterranean. Where British poet and author Robert Graves lived from 1929 until his death. His house is now a museum. The village itself is small, stone-streeted and panoramic. Quiet in the early morning hours. A small path leads to nearby Cala Deià cove — descend carefully, a beautiful cove awaits.

Sa Calobra Sa Calobra — steep to reach, views like nowhere else

On the north coast, a legendary 13km winding road descending from the Tramuntana mountains. The road itself is an experience — winding, narrow, breathtaking views. At the bottom, a small cove and Torrent de Pareis (a deep rock canyon). Very crowded in midsummer afternoons — go early in the morning or late afternoon. Limited car parking; coming by bike is possible but demanding.

Fornalutx Fornalutx — one of Spain's most beautiful villages

4 kilometres above Sóller, one of the Tramuntana's most impressive villages. Honey-coloured stone houses, stepped streets, lemon and orange groves. Population 600. Tourism but peaceful. It's also possible to walk from Sóller — about 1 hour. Lunch at a small restaurant here is one of Mallorca's most local and finest moments.

Cala Mondragó Natural Park Cala Mondragó — cove in a protected natural park

On the south-east coast, near Santanyí. Two small coves within a natural park — much quieter and more sheltered than crowded beach areas. Walking trails, pine forests and clear turquoise water. Reachable by bus or car. Paid car park. An early start is recommended in midsummer. Nearby Cala Figuera fishing village is ideal for fresh fish.

Bellver Castle Bellver Castle — Palma's most overlooked viewpoint

Just west of Palma city centre, on a pine-forested hill. A circular plan from the 14th century — one of Europe's rare round castles. Houses the Palma History Museum inside. But the real reason: from the terrace, Palma bay and the whole city are visible 360 degrees. Ticketed but affordable. Reachable by EMT bus. Open-air concerts on summer evenings.

✨ Valldemossa — the monastery village where Chopin wintered

20 minutes by car from Palma. Frédéric Chopin and George Sand spent the winter of 1838-39 here — the cells of the former Carthusian monastery are now converted to a museum. The village is small and beautiful: stone streets, potted flowers, small cafés. Very crowded in midsummer but quiet in the early morning hours. Fine walking trails in the surrounding area. Valldemossa local arts festival takes place every summer.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Mallorca is two islands: the one in the brochure and the real one. Know these four and holiday on the second.

🍹 TRAP

Mistaking Magaluf and Arenal for 'Mallorca'

The party strips are the island's exception, not its shop window; knowing Mallorca from there is like knowing a country from its airport.

💡 What to Do Instead: Point your route at the Tramuntana villages, Palma's old town and the southern coves; that's where the island actually is.
🐟 TRAP

'Fresh fish' with the kilo price unasked

At seaside restaurants the 'catch of the day' sells by weight; fish ordered without asking becomes a surprise on the bill.

💡 What to Do Instead: Ask the weight and price before ordering; or choose the locals' fish quarter around Santa Catalina.
🚗 TRAP

Descending Sa Calobra at summer midday

One of the world's twistiest roads becomes a bus convoy and parking war at noon.

💡 What to Do Instead: Go at first light, or approach by boat from Port de Sóller: the same fjord, zero hairpin stress.
🥐 TRAP

An airport-box ensaimada

The mass-produced last-minute ensaimada is not even a shadow of the one from the oven.

💡 What to Do Instead: Buy it fresh from a historic Palma bakery; if it's travelling home, the bakeries provide travel boxes too.
🛡️ The golden rule

On Mallorca the distances are short and the differences huge: drive twenty minutes inland from the coastal crowds and the stone villages, almond groves and silence begin. That's what the car is for.
