Milano

Fashion, design, football and the best aperitivo on earth — in that order

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Milano

Milan doesn't try to charm you. It doesn't need to. This is a working city — Italy's financial and fashion capital, full of people with somewhere to be, performing for no one. But just beneath that cool surface, something else entirely is going on: Leonardo da Vinci's greatest masterpiece on a convent wall, a Gothic cathedral six centuries in the making, a colony of flamingos living in a private garden two blocks from the city centre, and an aperitivo culture so generous it amounts to a free dinner with every drink. Milan takes a moment to open up. Give it that moment.

🗣️ Local Concept: Aperitivo (ah-peh-REE-tee-voh)

Not just a drink before dinner — a social institution. Every evening from 6 to 9 PM, Milano's bars transform: order one drink and a spread of food appears. In some places it's a full buffet. In others, just a few bites. Either way, it's the city's great democratic ritual — the moment when finance directors and art students sit at the same bar and the evening properly begins.

3 Unwritten Rules for Milano

1

Book everything that matters in advance

Leonardo's Last Supper (Santa Maria delle Grazie) is one of the most regulated visits in the world — only 30 people allowed in at a time, 15 minutes maximum. Tickets sell out weeks, sometimes months ahead. Il Duomo rooftop, the Pinacoteca di Brera, Cenacolo Vinciano — book all of these before you arrive. Walking up on the day guarantees disappointment.

2

Aperitivo is not a snack — it is dinner

Milanese aperitivo begins at 18:00 and runs until around 21:00. Order a Spritz, Negroni or Campari Soda and a spread of food arrives — sometimes a full buffet, always substantial. This is how the city eats on weekday evenings. Don't eat before. Don't rush it. Sit, drink, graze, and watch Milano decompress after work.

3

Sunday shuts down — plan around it

More than most Italian cities, Milano observes Sunday. Shops close, many restaurants shut, the fashion district goes quiet. The upside: museums are less crowded, parks fill with locals, and the city shows a slower, more residential face. Plan your museum visits and park days for Sunday and your shopping for any other day.

Getting Around

Milano Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Milano has one of Italy's best public transport networks. The metro is clean, fast and logical. Between metro, trams and buses, almost everything is reachable without a taxi.

✈️

Linate Airport (LIN) → City Center

Linate is the closest airport — just 7 km from the city center. The metro Line 4 (M4, blue line) opened in 2023 and connects Linate directly to the center in about 30 minutes. Bus 73 is the budget alternative but slower. Taxi is expensive. If you're flying into Linate, the M4 is the obvious choice.

✈️

Malpensa Airport (MXP) → City Center

Malpensa is 50 km northwest of the city — further but handles more international flights. The Malpensa Express train runs directly to Milano Centrale (50 min) or Cadorna (40 min) every 30 minutes. It's the fastest and most reliable option. Shared shuttles exist but add significant time. Taxis from Malpensa are very expensive.

🚇

Metro — fast and logical

Milano's metro has four lines (M1 red, M2 green, M3 yellow, M4 blue) covering all major areas. It's fast, clean and runs until midnight on weekdays, later on weekends. Tap your contactless card or buy a single journey or day pass at any station. The day pass is excellent value if you're making more than three journeys. Validate before boarding.

🚃

Trams — the authentic way

Milano's historic orange trams are one of the city's most charming features — some have been running since the 1920s. They're slower than the metro but cover areas the underground doesn't reach and offer a moving view of the city. Line 1 from Piazza Castello to Piazza XXIV Maggio is one of the great urban tram rides in Europe. Same ticket as the metro.

🚲

BikeMi — city bikes

Milano's public bike share system with hundreds of docking stations throughout the city. Excellent for flat central areas, canal-side rides along the Navigli, and reaching the Parco Sempione from the center. Register online or via the app. Electric bikes also available at select stations.

💡 ATM day pass

If you're spending a full day in the city, the ATM day pass covers unlimited journeys on metro, trams and buses. Buy it at any metro station or tabacchi. Far better value than individual tickets if you're moving around — and you will be.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Milano: Best Areas

Milano's neighbourhoods are dramatically different in character. The right choice depends entirely on what kind of city you want to wake up in.

Brera

Most beautiful

Milano's most picturesque neighbourhood — cobblestone streets, art galleries, independent boutiques, the magnificent Pinacoteca di Brera. Central, walkable and effortlessly elegant. The aperitivo bars here are some of the best in the city. More expensive than other areas but genuinely one of the finest urban neighbourhoods in Italy.

📍 Pinacoteca di Brera • Art galleries • Best aperitivo

Navigli

Most atmospheric

The canal district. Bohemian, creative and alive at all hours. The Naviglio Grande and Naviglio Pavese canals are lined with bars, restaurants and studios. The aperitivo scene here is legendary — some of the most generous spreads in the city. Slightly further from Il Duomo but the metro brings you there in minutes. The last-Sunday flea market is unmissable.

📍 Canal-side bars • Aperitivo scene • Monthly flea market

Isola

Most local

Once a working-class island cut off by railway lines, Isola is now Milano's most interesting neighbourhood — independent restaurants, design studios, street art and the spectacular Bosco Verticale (Vertical Forest) towers. Genuinely local, genuinely creative, not yet overrun. The area around Piazza Garibaldi and Corso Como is one of the best in the city.

📍 Bosco Verticale • Independent restaurants • Design scene

Porta Romana & Tortona

Design district

The city's design and fashion heartland — particularly electric during Salone del Mobile in April. Former industrial buildings now house the city's most important galleries, showrooms and concept stores. The Fondazione Prada is here. Quieter outside design week but deeply interesting year-round.

📍 Fondazione Prada • Design galleries • Salone del Mobile

Città Studi

Best value

The university district east of the center. Young, affordable and genuinely local — good trattorias, neighbourhood bars, the wonderful Orto Botanico. Further from the tourist sights but well connected by metro and tram. The area around Piazza Leonardo da Vinci has a calm, studious energy that's very different from the fashion-district intensity.

📍 University energy • Affordable • Good trattorias
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Milano

Milano is a city of seasons — each one transforms its character completely. The fashion and design calendar also shapes the city's rhythm in ways worth knowing.

Spring

Mar – May
Design Week

April brings Salone del Mobile — the world's most important design fair — and Milano transforms entirely. The whole city becomes a design exhibition, with events, installations and parties spilling out of every corner. Outside design week, spring offers ideal weather, cherry blossoms in the Parco Sempione and a city in full energy. The most exciting time to be here.

Summer

Jun – Aug
Hot & Local

August is when Milanese leave for the coast or mountains and the city empties. Restaurants close, streets quiet down, and a strange calm descends on one of Europe's most frenetic cities. July is hot and humid but perfectly functional — fewer tourists, shorter queues, lower prices. The aperitivo scene continues regardless. Not the conventional peak season but has its own particular charm.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Fashion Week

September brings Milano Fashion Week — the city's other great transformation. The streets fill with editors, buyers and photographers; restaurants become impossible without reservations; the energy is electric. Outside fashion week, September and October offer golden light, harvest produce in the markets, and a city settling into its most liveable season.

Winter

Dec – Feb
Misty & Magical

Milano in winter is fog, candlelit trattorias, risotto milanese and the extraordinary La Scala opera season. The city is at its most intimate — locals only, tourists few, the fashion district quiet. Christmas markets in Piazza Duomo and along the Navigli. January is the quietest and most authentic month. February brings carnival and the first design-week energy begins building.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Milano

Milano's food is nothing like the rest of Italy — and Milanese are proud of that. Richer, slower-cooked, more influenced by the north. This is the city that invented risotto, cotoletta and panettone.

🍚

Risotto alla Milanese — the city's signature

Saffron-gold, bone-marrow-rich, finished with a generous hand of Parmigiano. The real thing takes 18 minutes from order to plate and arrives slightly liquid — all'onda, they say, like a wave. If it's stiff, it's been sitting. Osteria dell'Acquabella and Trattoria del Nuovo Macello are benchmarks. Order it as a primo, not a side.

⭐ 18 minutes · all'onda · primo course only
🥩

Cotoletta alla Milanese — not a schnitzel

Bone-in veal cutlet, breaded and fried in clarified butter until deeply golden. Thicker than Wiener Schnitzel, richer, and always served with the bone attached. The debate about whether the Viennese stole the recipe from Milano has been running for 200 years. Order it at a traditional trattoria, not a tourist restaurant. It should be crisp, substantial and arrive alone.

⭐ Bone-in · butter-fried · not a side dish
🫘

Cassoeula — winter in a bowl

The definitive winter dish: pork (ribs, sausage, trotters) slow-cooked with Savoy cabbage until everything collapses into something profound and deeply comforting. Only on menus from October to February — if you see it any other time, it's not real. Order it in a trattoria in the Navigli or Porta Romana. It's heavy, honest and deeply Milanese.

⭐ October–February only · trattoria only
🥐

Colazione Milanese — the breakfast ritual

Stand at the bar (never sit — it costs more and misses the point). Order a cappuccino and a cornetto (Italian croissant, filled with cream, jam or pistachio). The whole thing takes four minutes and costs very little. Repeat at any bar in the city. This is how 9 million journeys begin every morning in Lombardy.

⭐ Stand at the bar · cappuccino + cornetto · never before 7 AM
🍊

Aperitivo — the Milanese invention

The city that gave the world Campari (invented here in 1860) takes its aperitivo seriously. The classic order: Campari Soda, Negroni or Aperol Spritz. At most bars, one drink unlocks the food spread. In Navigli and Brera, the spreads can be extraordinary — pasta, rice dishes, bruschetta, charcuterie. This is dinner, priced as a drink.

⭐ 18:00–21:00 · one drink · dinner is included
🎂

Panettone — the original

Yes, panettone is from Milano. The original is nothing like the supermarket versions exported around the world — tall, domed, made with a natural yeast starter that takes days to develop. Pasticceria Marchesi (founded 1824) and Pasticceria Cova are the historic benchmarks. Even outside Christmas, good Milanese bakeries carry it year-round.

⭐ Marchesi or Cova · the original is nothing like the export version
🍷 Mercato Comunale di Porta Romana

Milano has exceptional food markets. The Mercato Comunale di Porta Romana is one of the best — a covered market where local producers sell cheese, charcuterie, fresh pasta, vegetables and wine. Go on Saturday morning, arrive hungry, and buy your lunch there. Far better than any restaurant at a fraction of the cost.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Milano

Milano rewards those who look beyond Il Duomo and the Galleria. The city's most extraordinary things are behind unmarked doors, inside converted factories and at the end of quiet courtyards.

✨ Leonardo's other masterpiece — almost no queue

The Last Supper (Cenacolo Vinciano) — Santa Maria delle Grazie

Everyone knows it exists. Almost no one prepares for how extraordinary it is in person. Painted directly onto the refectory wall between 1495 and 1498, it is 8.8 metres wide and nearly 5 metres tall. You stand in front of it for exactly 15 minutes with 29 other people in a climate-controlled room. The experience is unlike anything else in Italy. Book weeks ahead — but book.

✨ Secret garden inside a Renaissance courtyard

Università degli Studi — Cortile della Facoltà di Giurisprudenza

The central courtyard of Milano's historic university, built on the site of a 15th-century hospital designed by Filarete. Walk through the unmarked entrance on Via Festa del Perdono and find a perfectly preserved Renaissance courtyard where students have studied for 600 years. Almost entirely unknown to tourists. Free, open during university hours.

✨ The most beautiful bookshop in Italy

Libreria Bocca — Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II

Inside the Galleria, hidden from the luxury shops and tourist crowds, is Italy's oldest bookshop — open since 1775. The interior is magnificent: dark wood, glass cases, art books stacked floor to ceiling. Specialises in art, architecture and design. The staff have been recommending books since before most countries existed. Go in, even if you don't buy anything.

✨ Vertical forest — a building like no other

Bosco Verticale — Isola

Two residential towers in the Isola neighbourhood covered in 900 trees, 5,000 shrubs and 11,000 plants — a forest suspended above the city. Designed by Stefano Boeri and completed in 2014, they have become one of the most photographed buildings in the world. You can't go inside but they are extraordinary from street level. The surrounding neighbourhood of Isola is worth half a day in itself.

✨ Fondazione Prada — art in a distillery

Fondazione Prada

Miuccia Prada's extraordinary foundation occupies a former gin distillery in the Porta Romana area — a complex of old and new buildings designed by Rem Koolhaas's OMA. The permanent collection includes some of the most significant contemporary art in Italy. The gold-leafed Haunted House building alone is worth the visit. The Bar Luce, designed by Wes Anderson to look like a 1950s Milanese café, is exceptional.

✨ The oldest canal in Europe — for a sunset walk

Naviglio Grande at golden hour

Leonardo da Vinci worked on the canal system here in the late 15th century. The Naviglio Grande — stretching 50 km to the Ticino river — was once how marble arrived in Milano to build Il Duomo. Today it's lined with bars, restaurants and studios. At golden hour, the light on the water and the reflections of the old buildings create one of the most beautiful urban scenes in northern Italy. Walk from the Darsena west toward Corsico.

✨ Last Sunday of the month

On the last Sunday of every month, the Mercatone dell'Antiquariato fills the entire length of Naviglio Grande — one of Italy's greatest flea markets. Antique dealers, vintage clothing, art prints, furniture, ceramics. Arrive early. Bring cash. It runs from early morning until evening and is one of the most genuinely Milanese experiences available.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Milan is an elegant city, but the Duomo's surroundings hold Italy's record for trap density. Know these four and keep your style intact.

🕊️ TRAP

Corn and bracelets on the Duomo square

People pressing corn into your hand for pigeon photos or tying a 'gift' string on your wrist will insist on payment afterwards.

💡 What to Do Instead: Hands in pockets, no eye contact, smile and keep walking. Shoot your photo from the square's corner — it looks better anyway.
☕ TRAP

Sitting down for coffee in the Galleria

An espresso at a table in the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II costs several times the standing price. Most places sell the view, not the coffee.

💡 What to Do Instead: Do it the Milanese way: drink standing at the counter. For historic atmosphere, take the counter side at Camparino.
🍸 TRAP

'Free buffet' aperitivo traps

Some Navigli bars hunt tourists with neon cocktails and a stale buffet; the drink is mediocre, the buffet is a prop.

💡 What to Do Instead: Choose the bar where the crowd speaks Italian; a good aperitivo brings a small but careful plate with your drink.
👜 TRAP

Bargaining for 'designer' bags on the street

The 'Pradas' laid out near Montenapoleone are fake — and buying can be as punishable as selling.

💡 What to Do Instead: For style on a budget, hit the designer outlets or vintage shops; Milan's second-hand is first-class.
🛡️ The golden rule

In Milan, quality is quiet: the best places have small signs, plain windows, often a closed door. Avoid anything that shouts.

Local Slang

Thank you — short, clear, always
