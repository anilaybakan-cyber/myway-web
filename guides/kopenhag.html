Copenhagen

The capital of bicycles, harbour water and hygge

Overview

The Vibe of Copenhagen

Copenhagen doesn't show off with grandeur but with a well-built life: at eight in the morning a man in a suit glides past you on a bicycle, at noon people dive into the sea from the harbour steps, and in the evening someone chats in a candlelit window without once looking at their phone. This city is an elegant answer to the question 'how should one live'. Take your postcard shot of colourful Nyhavn, then get down to the real business: pedal, get in the water, sit down around a candle. Copenhagen isn't seen — it's lived.

💬 Hygge
🗣️ /hoo-gah/

Danish's gift to the world: that indefinable state of cosiness made of candlelight, good friends, a warm drink and the absence of hurry. If you see blankets and candles in a café, hygge is there — joining in is allowed.

Insider Advice

Unwritten Rules for Copenhagen

#1
Never step into the bike lane The bike lane is not a pavement, it's a motorway: step into a flow moving at 20 km/h and you'll hear a Danish warning before the bell. Cross it like an arrow, take your photos from the pavement.
#2
Respect the trust Denmark is a society of trust: prams sleep outside cafés, bikes are held by thin locks, the metro has no turnstiles. As this system's guest, buy your ticket and keep your astonishment to a smile.
#3
Swimming is free — with rules The harbour baths (havnebad) are free and the water is clean enough to swim in; swimming outside the marked areas and in the boat lanes is both forbidden and dangerous. Jump in where the locals jump.
Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Copenhagen

💎 HIDDEN GEM
Assistens Kirkegård
The cemetery where Andersen and Kierkegaard rest is also Nørrebro's park: locals sunbathe among the headstones, cycle through, read books. There are few places where death and life stand so amicably side by side.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
CopenHill
The roof of the city's waste-to-energy plant is an artificial ski slope, a hiking path and the world's tallest climbing wall: sport on top of clean energy. From the summit, the harbour and the wind turbines — a view that summarises Copenhagen.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Superkilen
Nørrebro's park assembled from objects from more than 60 countries: a Moroccan fountain, a Thai boxing ring, Soviet neon and the famous white-striped pink-and-black hills. A world-renowned classroom of urban design.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Islands Brygge Havnebad
A free five-pool harbour bath in the city's belly: a Copenhagener leaving the office loosens their tie, jumps in, and goes back to work. If you have the courage, the five-metre platform is waiting.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Cisternerne
The old water cistern beneath Frederiksberg hill is the city's most atmospheric art space: darkness, dripping water and giant site-specific installations. The park above is a bonus view.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Refshaleøen and La Banchina
The old shipyard island is the city's new creative quarter: street food, a sauna-and-sea combination, and tiny La Banchina, where wine is drunk on the jetty. Go by havnebus and meet the sunset at the jetty's edge.
Food & Flavors

What to Taste

You're in the capital of New Nordic cuisine, but don't be intimidated: the smørrebrød counter, the sausage cart and the cinnamon roll are every bit as sacred as the starred menus.

🍴 MUST TASTE
The smørrebrød ritual
The lunch classic: pickled herring on rye, veal with remoulade, or shrimp with egg. Eaten in century-old lunch rooms with white tablecloths and snaps — leave the ordering sequence to the waiter.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Pølsevogn — the sausage cart
A red sausage (rød pølse) with the bread on the side, plenty of mustard, chocolate milk to drink: Denmark's standing national menu. The organic sausage carts have carried the classic into a new generation.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Kanelsnegl and the truth about the Danish
What the world calls a 'Danish pastry' is called wienerbrød (Vienna bread) here — and the cinnamon snail, the kanelsnegl, is the city's morning religion. When you see a bakery queue, join the back of it.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Torvehallerne market
The glass-roofed gourmet market: smørrebrød counters, fresh fish, coffee, and Danish cooking portion by portion. The address for both the rainy-day plan and the sunny picnic shop.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Craft beer and the culture of skål
In Carlsberg's city the new wave is strong: Mikkeller and the micro beer bars draw pilgrims worldwide. When the glasses rise, meet everyone's eyes, say 'skål', then drink — the order matters.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Refshaleøen street food
Reffen, on the old shipyard island, is a giant street kitchen built from containers: world cuisines, harbour views, fire pits. Go by havnebus, stay for the sunset.
Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

❌
The world-famous statue is 1.25 metres tall with a fifty-person selfie queue in front; the disappointment is bigger than she is.
✅ Better Alternative: Make her one stop on a morning bike ride: pass through the star fortress of Kastellet, greet the statue in 30 seconds, and give your real time to the fortress and the waterfront path.
❌
The menus under the colourful façades add the view to the bill: mediocre fish at above-capital prices.
✅ Better Alternative: Have only a beer at Nyhavn (on the quay stones, from a shop — a legitimate local tradition); eat dinner in Kødbyen or Nørrebro.
❌
With the metro 15 minutes from the centre, a taxi does the same trip at ten times the price; add time on top if it hits traffic.
✅ Better Alternative: Metro M2 drops straight into the centre and the fare is a contactless tap at the gate. Even with lots of luggage, think metro first — the stations have lifts.
❌
Three restaurant meals a day will finish your budget in one day in Copenhagen; the city itself doesn't live that way.
✅ Better Alternative: Copy the local pattern: bakery in the morning, market counter or smørrebrød at noon, one chosen restaurant at night. The difference buys you a ticket to Louisiana.
