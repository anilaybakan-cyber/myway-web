Copenhagen

The capital of bicycles, harbour water and hygge

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Copenhagen

Copenhagen doesn't show off with grandeur but with a well-built life: at eight in the morning a man in a suit glides past you on a bicycle, at noon people dive into the sea from the harbour steps, and in the evening someone chats in a candlelit window without once looking at their phone. This city is an elegant answer to the question 'how should one live'. Take your postcard shot of colourful Nyhavn, then get down to the real business: pedal, get in the water, sit down around a candle. Copenhagen isn't seen — it's lived.

🗣️ Local Concept: Hygge (hoo-gah)

Danish's gift to the world: that indefinable state of cosiness made of candlelight, good friends, a warm drink and the absence of hurry. If you see blankets and candles in a café, hygge is there — joining in is allowed.

3 Unwritten Rules for Copenhagen

1

Never step into the bike lane

The bike lane is not a pavement, it's a motorway: step into a flow moving at 20 km/h and you'll hear a Danish warning before the bell. Cross it like an arrow, take your photos from the pavement.

2

Respect the trust

Denmark is a society of trust: prams sleep outside cafés, bikes are held by thin locks, the metro has no turnstiles. As this system's guest, buy your ticket and keep your astonishment to a smile.

3

Swimming is free — with rules

The harbour baths (havnebad) are free and the water is clean enough to swim in; swimming outside the marked areas and in the boat lanes is both forbidden and dangerous. Jump in where the locals jump.

Getting Around

Copenhagen Public Transit & Airport Transfers

In Copenhagen the king of transport is the bicycle; the metro is driverless and punctual, and the harbour boats are the 'bus' with the best view.

✈️

Airport to the centre

Metro line M2 reaches Kongens Nytorv in 15 minutes; the train reaches Central Station in about the same. Both share one ticket system — tap through with a contactless card.

🚲

The bicycle — the city's mother tongue

With a rental bike the city is yours in a day: wide lanes, bicycle bridges, bicycle traffic lights. The rules are serious: keep right, signal your turns, raise a hand when stopping.

🚇

The driverless metro

Lines M1–M4 run 24/7, and watching the tracks through the front window feels like a childhood dream. The Cityringen loop (M3) ties all the central districts together.

⛴️

The harbour boats

The yellow harbour buses (havnebus) run on a normal public transport ticket: go from Nyhavn to the Opera and on to Refshaleøen over the water — at bus fare, not tour-boat fare.

🚶

The centre on foot

From Strøget to Nyhavn and on to the palace square, the centre walks comfortably; but Copenhagen is a broad city — for Nørrebro and Refshaleøen trust the pedals or the metro.

💡 Havnebus tip

The yellow 991/992 boats are the city's cheapest 'cruise': the whole waterfront from the Royal Library to the Opera on a normal ticket. Time it for the sunset departure.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Copenhagen: Best Areas

Copenhagen's districts compete on character: the fairytale centre, bohemian Nørrebro, formerly-meat-now-cool Vesterbro, canal-laced Christianshavn.

Indre By (the centre)

Most central

A fairytale set between Strøget, Nyhavn and the palaces: walking distance to everything, morning coffee by the canal. Prices above even capital level — location is paid for in cash.

📍 Nyhavn two steps away • Walk to everything • High prices

Nørrebro

Most bohemian

The multicultural energy across the bridge: world kitchens, vintage shops, Superkilen park, picnics in the cemetery park. Young, alive, and home to the city's best coffee roasters.

📍 World kitchens • Vintage shops • Young energy

Vesterbro

Coolest

The bars of the old meatpacking district Kødbyen, third-wave coffee and design hotels: new life inside old fabric. Next to Central Station — practical with a suitcase too.

📍 Kødbyen bars • Design hotels • Near the station

Christianshavn

Most picturesque

Canals, boats and façades that recall Amsterdam: five minutes to the centre over the bridge, the sound of water in the evening. For those after canalside breakfasts and quiet nights.

📍 Canal views • Bridge to centre • Quiet evenings

Østerbro

Calmest

The district of embassies, lakes and wide avenues: the density of prams is the city's index of trust. Walking distance to the Little Mermaid; the quarter for families and seekers of calm.

📍 Lakeside • Family friendly • Broad parks
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Copenhagen

Copenhagen's summer is like a reward and its winter like a lesson: the reward is daylight, the lesson is hygge. Both are worth having.

Spring

Apr – Jun
The Awakening

Cherry blossoms open, café tables spill into the street, blankets are hung over chairs. The light stretches fast; in June it's still twilight at eleven at night.

Summer

Jul – Aug
Harbour Summer

The city moves outdoors: the harbour pools fill, the waterfronts become beaches, the street-food markets live into the night. The day lasts 17 hours and nobody wants to stay inside.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Golden Light

The trees turn, candles are lit early in the cafés, museums and concert halls open their seasons. On Kulturnatten in October, hundreds of venues keep their doors open until midnight.

Winter

Dec – Feb
Hygge Season

The day darkens at four, but the city answers the dark with candles: Tivoli becomes a winter garden, cafés hand out blankets, hygge peaks. Dress for the cold, look warmly on the soul.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Copenhagen

You're in the capital of New Nordic cuisine, but don't be intimidated: the smørrebrød counter, the sausage cart and the cinnamon roll are every bit as sacred as the starred menus.

🥪

The smørrebrød ritual

The lunch classic: pickled herring on rye, veal with remoulade, or shrimp with egg. Eaten in century-old lunch rooms with white tablecloths and snaps — leave the ordering sequence to the waiter.

⭐ Lunch classic · with a snaps
🌭

Pølsevogn — the sausage cart

A red sausage (rød pølse) with the bread on the side, plenty of mustard, chocolate milk to drink: Denmark's standing national menu. The organic sausage carts have carried the classic into a new generation.

⭐ On your feet · ristet + the works
🥐

Kanelsnegl and the truth about the Danish

What the world calls a 'Danish pastry' is called wienerbrød (Vienna bread) here — and the cinnamon snail, the kanelsnegl, is the city's morning religion. When you see a bakery queue, join the back of it.

⭐ From a morning bakery · a queue is a good sign
🏪

Torvehallerne market

The glass-roofed gourmet market: smørrebrød counters, fresh fish, coffee, and Danish cooking portion by portion. The address for both the rainy-day plan and the sunny picnic shop.

⭐ Covered market · lunch counters
🍺

Craft beer and the culture of skål

In Carlsberg's city the new wave is strong: Mikkeller and the micro beer bars draw pilgrims worldwide. When the glasses rise, meet everyone's eyes, say 'skål', then drink — the order matters.

⭐ Micro beer bar · eye contact + skål
🔥

Refshaleøen street food

Reffen, on the old shipyard island, is a giant street kitchen built from containers: world cuisines, harbour views, fire pits. Go by havnebus, stay for the sunset.

⭐ In summer · go by boat
🍷 Budget tip

Restaurant bills swell in Copenhagen; strike the balance like a local: smørrebrød or a market counter at noon, one good restaurant in the evening. Tap water is free and excellent everywhere — don't hesitate to ask.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Copenhagen

Everyone sees the Little Mermaid (and is surprised); these six are Copenhagen's real character.

✨ A picnic park with philosophers

Assistens Kirkegård

The cemetery where Andersen and Kierkegaard rest is also Nørrebro's park: locals sunbathe among the headstones, cycle through, read books. There are few places where death and life stand so amicably side by side.

✨ A power plant with a ski slope on its roof

CopenHill

The roof of the city's waste-to-energy plant is an artificial ski slope, a hiking path and the world's tallest climbing wall: sport on top of clean energy. From the summit, the harbour and the wind turbines — a view that summarises Copenhagen.

✨ A park like a world map

Superkilen

Nørrebro's park assembled from objects from more than 60 countries: a Moroccan fountain, a Thai boxing ring, Soviet neon and the famous white-striped pink-and-black hills. A world-renowned classroom of urban design.

✨ A free harbour beach

Islands Brygge Havnebad

A free five-pool harbour bath in the city's belly: a Copenhagener leaving the office loosens their tie, jumps in, and goes back to work. If you have the courage, the five-metre platform is waiting.

✨ An art cistern underground

Cisternerne

The old water cistern beneath Frederiksberg hill is the city's most atmospheric art space: darkness, dripping water and giant site-specific installations. The park above is a bonus view.

✨ Sunset on the shipyard island

Refshaleøen and La Banchina

The old shipyard island is the city's new creative quarter: street food, a sauna-and-sea combination, and tiny La Banchina, where wine is drunk on the jetty. Go by havnebus and meet the sunset at the jetty's edge.

✨ Morning tip

See Nyhavn at 8 am: colourful façades, an empty quay, coffee steam. The same frame becomes a flood of people by noon — the postcard is shot in the morning; the afternoon escapes to the Christianshavn canals.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Copenhagen is an honest city; its traps come less from bad faith than from the price tag.

🧜 TRAP

The Little Mermaid pilgrimage

The world-famous statue is 1.25 metres tall with a fifty-person selfie queue in front; the disappointment is bigger than she is.

💡 What to Do Instead: Make her one stop on a morning bike ride: pass through the star fortress of Kastellet, greet the statue in 30 seconds, and give your real time to the fortress and the waterfront path.
🍽️ TRAP

Dinner on the Nyhavn quay

The menus under the colourful façades add the view to the bill: mediocre fish at above-capital prices.

💡 What to Do Instead: Have only a beer at Nyhavn (on the quay stones, from a shop — a legitimate local tradition); eat dinner in Kødbyen or Nørrebro.
🚕 TRAP

Taking a taxi from the airport

With the metro 15 minutes from the centre, a taxi does the same trip at ten times the price; add time on top if it hits traffic.

💡 What to Do Instead: Metro M2 drops straight into the centre and the fare is a contactless tap at the gate. Even with lots of luggage, think metro first — the stations have lifts.
💸 TRAP

Trying to eat every meal in a restaurant

Three restaurant meals a day will finish your budget in one day in Copenhagen; the city itself doesn't live that way.

💡 What to Do Instead: Copy the local pattern: bakery in the morning, market counter or smørrebrød at noon, one chosen restaurant at night. The difference buys you a ticket to Louisiana.
🛡️ Golden rule

If something is expensive in Copenhagen, the locals found the cheap version long ago: ask, and copy. The city's best moments — harbour, park, bicycle, sunset — carry no price tag anyway.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Copenhagen

Danish pronunciation is an extreme sport, but everyone speaks English; these five words still open doors with a smile.

🗣️ Hej
Hello — say it twice (hej hej) and it becomes goodbye
🗣️ Tak
Thank you — 'tusind tak' means a thousand thanks
🗣️ Skål!
Cheers! — eye contact with everyone as the glasses rise is mandatory
🗣️ Hyggeligt
'That was lovely, so cosy' — the best summary of a get-together; smile when you hear it
🗣️ Undskyld
Excuse me / sorry — if you hear it with a bicycle bell, step aside fast
