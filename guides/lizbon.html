Lisbon

Seven hills, Atlantic light and the smell of freshly baked pastéis — the city rebuilds itself from scratch every morning

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Lisbon

Lisbon is built on seven hills — you'll understand this the moment you step into the city. The cobblestones are slippery, the streets are steep, but at the top of every hill there's a view. The city has made peace with this difficulty: trams climb, elevators help with the ascent, and people walk slowly. The Atlantic light hits here in a colour found nowhere else — not yellow, not amber, exactly Lisbon light. Azulejo tiles on every corner, fado seeping through a doorway, a pastelaria on every neighbourhood street. This city doesn't tell you to hurry.

🗣️ Local Concept: Saudade (saw-DA-djeh)

The word Portuguese has exported to the world. A longing for the past, for something distant, for something that perhaps never existed. Melancholic but not painful — a sweet sadness. Fado is Lisbon's own traditional music genre — songs performed with guitar accompaniment, exploring themes of grief, longing and fate. Its emotional foundation is saudade. Lisbon itself carries a little saudade: old neighbourhoods facing the Tagus, faded tiles, traces of an old empire.

3 Unwritten Rules for Lisbon

1

Eat at the counter, not the table — same place, half the price

In most pastelarias and cafés in Lisbon, eating and drinking at the counter costs less than sitting at a table — sometimes half the price. An espresso at the counter might be €0.80, at a table €1.50. The same rule applies to breakfast and light meals. Standing, quick, cheap: this is Lisbon's morning rhythm.

2

Tram 28 is a tourist trap — use the alternatives

Tram 28E runs a beautiful route but is now completely packed with tourists. Don't leave your pockets open and keep checking them. The crowds can be genuinely exhausting. Walk to Alfama or try tram 12E. Take the bus to Graça instead of 28E. The city's elevadores are a completely different story: Elevador da Bica, Elevador da Glória — these are still local and lovely.

3

Bacalhau every day different — but always the same fish

The Portuguese say they have 365 recipes for salt cod (bacalhau) — one for every day of the year. It's a legend but grounded in truth: bacalhau is at the heart of Portuguese cooking. When you see 'bacalhau' on a restaurant menu, look at how many versions they offer. Bacalhau à Brás — with egg, potato and olives — is the most classic and the right place to start.

Getting Around

Lisbon Public Transit & Airport Transfers

The metro is fast, trams are beautiful but slow, the ferry is scenic. And walking — in Lisbon, really walking is the most beautiful way to discover everything.

✈️

Airport → City

The red metro line (Aeroporto station) connects directly to Baixa-Chiado — about 25 minutes, no surcharge. Use your Viva Viagem card for a standard fare. Taxis and Uber are also available; 15-20 minutes to the centre, €10-15 depending on traffic. The metro is the most practical option: cheaper than a taxi, faster than a bus.

🚇

Metro — fast and reliable

4 lines (blue, yellow, green, red), 56 stations. Single fare €1.80. With zapping (pre-loaded credit), each journey is €1.47. Runs from 6:30am to 1:00am. Viva Viagem card costs €0.50 — bought once and reloaded repeatedly. The airport, Baixa, Chiado, Marquês de Pombal and Oriente are all on the metro lines.

🚋

Trams and elevators

Tram 28E has a beautiful route but is not recommended due to tourist crowds and pickpocket risk. Tram 15E goes to Belém — more modern, less crowded, the better choice. The elevadores are a completely different experience: Elevador da Bica, Elevador da Glória and Elevador de Santa Justa — valid with the Viva Viagem card.

⛴️

Tagus Ferry — the city's finest view

From Cais do Sodré pier to Cacilhas: just €1.30, 10 minutes. From the other side you see the Lisbon skyline, the Ponte 25 de Abril bridge and the Cristo Rei statue. Most beautiful at sunset.

🚶

Walking — and the reality of the hills

Lisbon has seven hills and they really are steep. Comfortable, non-slip shoes are essential — cobblestones become like an ice rink when wet. The centre is small: Baixa to Alfama on foot is 20 minutes, Chiado to Bairro Alto is 5 minutes. But budget time and breath for climbing to Graça or the heights of Alfama.

💡 Viva Viagem card — how to use it

Available at any metro station for €0.50. You can load a single fare, a 24-hour pass (€6.80) or zapping credit. Zapping is the most flexible: load credit in advance and pay €1.47 per journey — valid on metro, bus, tram, elevator and the Cacilhas ferry. The card is valid for one year; keep it if you're coming back to Lisbon.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Lisbon: Best Areas

Where you stay in Lisbon determines how many hills you'll climb. The centre is practical, Alfama characterful, Príncipe Real elegant.

Baixa / Chiado

Most central

The flat ground of the city. Baixa is busy with shopping streets and Praça do Comércio; Chiado right beside it is far more refined — bookshops, cafés, Livraria Bertrand. Close to the metro, trams and ferry. Walking distance to everything. Busy but the right choice if you want to be central.

📍 Close to everything • Chiado's cultural life • Metro connections

Alfama

Most characterful

Lisbon's oldest neighbourhood, dating from the Moorish period. Narrow streets, azulejo-clad buildings, laundry hanging from windows, cats on every corner. São Jorge Castle at the top. Fado lives here — at night music seeps through doorways. Touristic but still a living neighbourhood. Steep and tiring — but something different at every step.

📍 Heart of fado • São Jorge Castle • Historic fabric

Príncipe Real

Most elegant

Elegant buildings, tree-lined squares, design shops and good restaurants. An organic market sets up at Praça do Príncipe Real on Sundays. Walking distance to Bairro Alto and Chiado. Lisbon's quietest and most refined neighbourhood.

📍 Organic market • Design shops • Quiet and elegant

Mouraria / Intendente

Most authentic

Just below Alfama, the neighbourhood where the Moors settled after being expelled from Lisbon. Multicultural, noisy, colourful — Portuguese, African and Asian communities here. Fado has its roots here too — the first voices rose in these streets. Intendente square comes alive at night. Not touristic; real Lisbon life flows through these neighbourhoods.

📍 Multicultural • Roots of fado • Nightlife

Belém

Historic district

6 kilometres from the centre, by tram 15E or train. The Jerónimos Monastery, Tower of Belém and Monument to the Discoveries are here. The original Pastéis de Belém is here — Rua de Belém 84-92, since 1837. The queue is long but moves fast. If you want to try them in the city centre, Manteigaria (Chiado) is at least as good. Try to plan a separate day for a museum visit.

📍 Jerónimos Monastery • Pastéis de Belém • Historic monuments
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Lisbon

Lisbon can be visited year-round but is at its best when it's neither too cold nor too hot.

Spring

Mar – Jun
The best time

May and June are Lisbon's most beautiful months. Jacaranda trees burst into purple blossom in May — Avenida Fontes Pereira de Melo and Príncipe Real are the best places to start. The weather is mild, sunny but not overwhelming. In June, Festas de Santo António — the city's biggest festival, sardine grills, street music, processions.

Summer

Jul – Aug
Hot but breezy

July and August are the busiest months. Temperatures reach 30-35°C but the Atlantic breeze keeps it from feeling oppressive. Sardine season runs May to August. Museums are crowded, restaurants need bookings. For beaches, Cascais and Estoril are 40 minutes by train.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
Quiet and beautiful

September and October are Lisbon's most comfortable period. The summer crowds have gone, the weather is still mild, museums and restaurants are quieter. In autumn the sun hits at a lower angle — the light is soft, shadows are long, everything turns golden. Photographers' favourite season. Rain begins from November.

Winter

Dec – Feb
Wet but quiet

From December to February Lisbon is rainy but doesn't freeze. The least crowded, cheapest hotel prices, emptiest museums. The city is decorated with lights over Christmas. Fado evenings are at their best in winter — listening to fado on a cold, rainy night in a small venue carries a different meaning.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Lisbon

Lisbon is cheap and delicious — if you eat in the right places. Start at a pastelaria corner, move on to a tasca, finish with ginjinha.

🥐

Pastel de nata

An egg custard tart invented by monastery monks. The top is caramelised and lightly scorched, the pastry layered and crisp. Dust with cinnamon and icing sugar. At the counter, standing, with an espresso. Manteigaria (Chiado): when the bell rings, they've just come out of the oven. Pastéis de Belém is the original — since 1837, in Belém — but Manteigaria is at least as good and right in the city centre.

⭐ Manteigaria, Chiado · eat at the counter · early morning · €1.50
🥩

Bifana

Pork marinated in white wine, garlic and rosemary, stuffed into a papo seco roll. The bread absorbs the sauce — this is critical. If there's a yellow mustard bottle on the counter, that place knows what it's doing. As Bifanas do Afonso, Rua da Madalena 146 — standing, fast, cheap.

⭐ As Bifanas do Afonso, Rua da Madalena 146 · eat standing · €2.50–3.50
🐟

Bacalhau à Brás

Salt cod, thin matchstick fries, egg, onion, olives and parsley — all cooked together in one pan. The result looks simple but the flavour is layered. Said to have been invented in the 19th century by a tavern owner in Bairro Alto. Start your bacalhau journey in Lisbon here — the most approachable, most classic version for first-timers.

⭐ All traditional restaurants · year-round
🐟

Grilled sardines

Lisbon's seasonal dish from May to August. During the Santo António festival in June, the smell of grilled sardines fills every street corner — that smell is the scent of Lisbon's summer. With only lemon and bread. Fresh sardines are hard to find in winter; better not to order them in that season.

⭐ May–August · Alfama and festival streets
🍒

Ginjinha

Sour cherry liqueur, in a small glass, €1.50. Around 5pm there's a strange moment in Lisbon — lunch is over, evening hasn't arrived yet, nobody's hungry but everyone is stopping somewhere. This is exactly the moment for ginjinha. A Ginjinha has been at Largo de São Domingos since 1840 — go in, say what you want, take it, leave. A few doors along is Ginjinha Sem Rival. Try both; it takes ten minutes, the difference is yours to decide.

⭐ A Ginjinha, Largo de São Domingos · €1.50 · since 1840
🛒

Tasca lunch

Small, family-run, usually no printed menu or written on a chalkboard. Ask for the prato do dia (dish of the day), they'll tell you what's available, you take it. Fish, meat, a little wine — €8-12 all in. These kind of tascas are still going strong in Mouraria and Intendente. Zé da Mouraria is legendary: lunch service only, cash preferred. Reservation essential.

⭐ Mouraria and Intendente · lunch · €8–12 · cash
🍷 Time Out Market — how to use it

At Cais do Sodré, on the river. Good for trying several things at once — but a nightmare if you go at the wrong time. Between 1-2pm and 7-9pm, finding a seat is impossible. Sit first, then choose your food. Around €20-25 per person. Some of the city's best chefs have stalls here.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Lisbon

Lisbon's best places usually appear when you climb a staircase or turn into a narrow street.

✨ The viewpoint Lisbonites go to, not the one tourists photograph

Miradouro da Graça

Behind São Jorge Castle, in the Graça neighbourhood. Most tourists end up at Portas do Sol or Santa Luzia — Graça is less known, quieter, the view equally beautiful. Go in the early morning or before sunset. If you're going at sunset, don't forget to bring a beer. Sit, take a sip and enjoy the moment. In the view before you, the Tagus, Alfama and the whole city will be waiting.

✨ Portugal's book paradise — Europe's oldest bookshop

Livraria Bertrand

In Chiado, Rua Garrett 73. Open since 1732 — according to the Guinness Book of Records, the world's oldest bookshop still in operation. Small rooms, old wooden shelves, books in Portuguese and foreign languages. Even if you don't buy a book, go in, sit, stay a few minutes. You feel as though a whole history has settled over you.

✨ Lisbon by elevator — the descent of Bica

Elevador da Bica

A steep funicular tram connecting the Bica neighbourhood to Cais do Sodré. Running since 1892. Not for the photograph — ride it because this is genuinely the way to travel this route. Your Viva Viagem card is valid here. At the bottom, walking distance to Mercado da Ribeira. At the top, get lost in Bica's narrow streets.

✨ Tinned fish — Lisbon's most beautiful souvenir shops

Conserveira de Lisboa

At the entrance to Alfama, Rua dos Bacalhoeiros 34. Open since 1930. Inside, dozens of sardine, tuna and octopus tins — in colourful cans with designs from another era. A wonderful option for putting memories in your suitcase before you leave or bringing gifts for loved ones, at reasonable prices.

✨ Cross the Tagus and come back — Lisbon's skyline for €1.30

Cacilhas Ferry

From Cais do Sodré pier to Cacilhas: 10 minutes, €1.30. As the ferry moves, the Lisbon skyline, the Ponte 25 de Abril bridge and the Cristo Rei statue on the hill gradually come into sharper view. The finest view is during the crossing. Once you arrive in Cacilhas, you can eat fresh fish at the small restaurants on the waterfront while looking back at the city. Try to return at sunset — Lisbon takes on the most beautiful shade of orange at that hour.

✨ Portugal's history set into the pavement

Calçada Portuguesa — Rossio Square

Portugal's distinctive handmade black-and-white cobblestone mosaic. The finest example is in Rossio Square: wave-like curves simulating the sea, every stone placed by hand. The Portuguese took this technique to Brazil too. That's why it's no coincidence that Rio de Janeiro's famous pavements show the same craftsmanship. To see the mosaic's full pattern you need to look from slightly above; from the balcony of a café at the edge of the square, or from nearby steps, the whole design reveals itself.

✨ Feira da Ladra — Lisbon's oldest market

Every Tuesday and Saturday, above Alfama at Campo de Santa Clara. The name means 'Thief's Market' — second-hand goods, antiques, records, books, jewellery. Tuesday mornings are quieter; Saturdays busier but more colourful. Prices accept haggling. Free entry. Miradouro da Graça is within walking distance — you can fit both into the same morning.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Lisbon is a sweet city, but the tourism wave brought a few classic traps. Know these four and live the saudade for real.

🚋 TRAP

Queueing for tram 28 at midday

The legendary tram is now a pickpocket bus at noon: an hour's queue, then a ride packed like sardines.

💡 What to Do Instead: Ride the same line before 8 am or try the 12E; better still, walk the route — Alfama exists to be walked.
🍞 TRAP

Mistaking the couvert for a gift

The bread, olives and cheese that land on your table are not free; touch them and they're on the bill.

💡 What to Do Instead: If you don't want it, smile and send it back: 'não, obrigado' is enough — nobody minds, and the bill stays clean.
🛺 TRAP

Haggling a tuk-tuk city tour

The hills are the excuse and the tariffs are wild; a half-hour tour can cost three times a taxi.

💡 What to Do Instead: The funiculars (Bica, Glória) and the tram's quiet hours handle the hills; if a tuk-tuk it must be, lock the price before boarding.
🧁 TRAP

Hunting the nata only in the Belém queue

Pastéis de Belém is historic and lovely, but it isn't the only truth; an hour's queue is not required.

💡 What to Do Instead: Take a warm nata from the counter at Manteigaria, dust the cinnamon, be happy in thirty seconds; if you go to Belém, go at opening time.
🛡️ The golden rule

In Lisbon, avoid the place with a photo menu and a waiter at the door; enter the tasca with paper tablecloths and a handwritten menu. Half the price, twice the flavour, three times the story.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Lisbon

Portuguese is not Spanish — just knowing that wins hearts in Lisbon.

🗣️ Bom dia
Good morning — until noon; after that, boa tarde
🗣️ Obrigado / Obrigada
Thank you — ends in -o if you're male, -a if you're female
🗣️ Por favor
Please — add it to the end of your order
🗣️ Saúde!
Cheers! — ginjinha glasses rise to this
🗣️ Até já
See you soon — the warm goodbye Lisboetas use constantly
