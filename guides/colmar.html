Colmar

When the fairytale set turns out real: half-timber, canals and Riesling

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Colmar

Your first steps in Colmar feel like walking onto a film set: candy-coloured half-timbered houses, flower-draped canals, street names left over from the Middle Ages. Then the smell of a bakery hits you, glasses clink in a winstub, and you understand — this is no set, it's a living Alsatian town. French elegance and German order share a table here; on the table, there is Riesling.

🗣️ Local Concept: Winstub (VIN-shtub)

Alsace's wine tavern: wood panelling, checked tablecloths, choucroute steam and local wine by the jug. Warmer than a restaurant, heartier than a bar; a winstub table is the shortest route to understanding Colmar.

3 Unwritten Rules for Colmar

1

Be on the streets before 9 am

Colmar is small and its fame is big: by day, coach tours fill Little Venice. The town's fairytale self lives in the early morning and early evening — that's when both the photos and the peace are taken.

2

Don't leave the Wine Route unplanned

Colmar sits at the very centre of the Alsace Wine Route; Eguisheim and Riquewihr are half an hour away. Car, tour or bicycle — decide how on day one, not on your last day.

3

Mind the Monday–Tuesday closures

Museums and many restaurants close on Monday or Tuesday; check the day so you don't leave without seeing the Unterlinden.

Getting Around

Colmar Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Colmar's mode of transport is called shoes; the rest is for leaving town.

🚄

Arriving by train

About 2.5 hours from Paris by TGV, 30 minutes from Strasbourg, 45 from Basel. The station is a 10-minute walk from the centre; no taxi needed.

🚶

Everywhere on foot

The historic centre is 20 minutes end to end: Little Venice, the Unterlinden, the old market streets all within strolling range. Cobble-proof shoes are the only requirement.

🚲

The Wine Route by bicycle

Colmar sits on a plain; the wine villages are 30–60 minutes by bike. Rental shops in the centre, routes signposted — in spring and autumn this is the loveliest way to travel.

🚌

Buses and tours to the villages

Regional buses reach Eguisheim, Riquewihr and Kaysersberg but run sparsely; wine-tour minibuses with tastings included are a practical alternative.

🚗

Rental car — the free route

To ride the Wine Route at your own tempo, rent by the station; parking inside the villages is tight, use the main lots. The taster doesn't drive — tastings are serious in Alsace.

💡 Little train note

The tourist 'petit train' in the centre is sweet, but Colmar is a town made for walking; spend the money on a glass of Grand Cru instead.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Colmar: Best Areas

In Colmar the distances are small and the differences in atmosphere are big; canal view or vineyard view — your call.

Little Venice (Petite Venise)

Most romantic

Waking in a canal-side house puts you in Colmar's very heart: geraniums at the window, empty morning lanes. Prices high, rooms few — book early.

📍 Canal views • Morning quiet • Photographer's dream

Old Town — around the cathedral

Most practical

Around Saint-Martin and the market streets: restaurants, patisseries and museums at your doorstep. Daytime tourist density rises, but the location is beyond argument.

📍 Walk everywhere • Restaurant riches • Historic fabric

Around the station

Budget friendly

A calm quarter 10 minutes' walk from the centre: chain hotels and fair prices. A sensible base for Wine Route drivers and train connections.

📍 Good value • Next to the train • Quiet nights

Eguisheim — sleeping in a wine village

Most Alsatian

The circular medieval village 10 minutes away: when the tourists leave at dusk the village is yours, and you wake to walks in the vines. A sweet alternative to Colmar for those with a car.

📍 Vineyard views • Silent nights • Winstub next door

Towards Riquewihr — Wine Route bases

Wine route

The gîtes around Riquewihr and Kaysersberg suit a tasting-heavy program: the producers become your neighbours. Colmar is 20–30 minutes away; in the evening your own village's fairytale suffices.

📍 Producer neighbours • Fairytale villages • Car needed
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Colmar

Colmar has two peaks: flowers in summer, lights in winter. The seasons between are the connoisseur's secret.

Spring

Apr – Jun
The Awakening

Geraniums go up in the windows, the first green shows in the vines, the crowds haven't reached summer scale. The airiest window for cycling the Wine Route.

Summer

Jul – Aug
Flower Peak

The town drowns in flowers, canal boats and terraces run at capacity. The price of the beauty is the crowd: at midday you walk shoulder to shoulder in Little Venice.

Autumn

Sep – Nov
The Harvest

The vineyards turn gold, harvest festivals begin in the villages, the air smells of grapes. Colmar's most photogenic and most delicious season — those in the know always choose it.

Winter

Dec – Feb
The Christmas Tale

In December Colmar becomes one of Europe's loveliest Christmas markets: five separate squares, light shows, vin chaud steam. January and February are the town at its calmest and most local.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Colmar

Alsatian cooking is the marriage of French technique and German appetite; Colmar is that marriage's kitchen.

🥧

Tarte flambée (flammekueche)

Cream, onion and bacon on paper-thin dough: Alsace's 'pizza' comes out of the wood oven in seconds and is shared by hand. Beer works, so does Riesling.

⭐ Winstub classic · must be shared
🥨

Bretzel and kugelhopf

The icons of the bakery window: the glossy salted bretzel and the sweet almond-apricot kugelhopf. One of them beside your morning coffee — both if undecided.

⭐ From the bakery · fresh in the morning
🍖

Choucroute garnie

Fermented cabbage, three kinds of sausage, smoked meats: the monument of the Alsatian table. Portions are serious; sharing is not shameful, it is wise.

⭐ At a winstub · with Riesling
🧀

Munster cheese

The washed-rind cheese from the valley: its smell demands courage, its taste repays it. Served with cumin; a legendary match with Gewurztraminer.

⭐ For the brave · try it with cumin
🍷

Alsace whites — Riesling to Gewurz

Riesling, Pinot Gris, Gewurztraminer, Crémant: in the capital of the Wine Route, the glasses are in town and the cellars in the villages. Leaving without a tasting does Colmar an injustice.

⭐ Cellar tastings · ask for Grand Cru
🍰

Tarte aux quetsches — plum tart

The Alsatian tart of purple damsons in season: light on sugar, heavy on fruit, pure patisserie classic. If you've come in late summer, smile at your luck.

⭐ At the patisseries · seasonal
🍷 The winstub rule

The marks of a good winstub: a short menu, a French-German mix of language, wine arriving by the jug. Ask for 'Edelzwicker' — an honest Alsatian blend at a friendly price.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Colmar

Colmar's shop window belongs to everyone; these six corners belong to early risers and corner-turners.

✨ A quiet giant of world art

Unterlinden Museum — the Isenheim Altarpiece

At the heart of the museum, set in an old convent, stands Grünewald's Isenheim Altarpiece: the most shattering work of the Northern Renaissance. None of the Louvre's crowds, several times the impact.

✨ The sculptor's house

Bartholdi Museum

Auguste Bartholdi, creator of the Statue of Liberty, was a Colmar man; his birth house is now a museum. A courtyard full of models of New York's icon — the town's sweetest surprise.

✨ Keep your head up

Maison des Têtes

A mansion from 1609 whose façade is covered with 106 grotesque heads: most visitors pass without looking. Count them slowly from across the street — every one is different.

✨ The covered market's delicious side

Marché Couvert

The brick market hall of 1865: cheesemongers, charcutiers, tart stalls. Its rear terrace over the canal is lunch's best-kept address.

✨ Three castles on one hill

Above Eguisheim — the Three Towers

Just outside Colmar, three ruined medieval keeps on a hill above the vines: a short walk, an endless vineyard view, and solitude at sunset.

✨ The hidden language of façades

The rooftops of the Tanners' Quarter

The steep-roofed houses of the tanners' district were built with drying lofts; for those who know the façade colours once coded the old guilds, the town reads like an open book.

✨ Morning bonus

Little Venice's classic photo spot, the Saint-Pierre bridge, is empty at 8 am — and the light rises exactly there.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Colmar is a harmless town; its traps cost time rather than fortunes. Know these four and live the fairytale efficiently.

🚣 TRAP

The midday queue for the canal boats

Waiting 45 minutes at noon for a very short boat ride is the worst way to spend time in Colmar.

💡 What to Do Instead: Take the first morning boat or skip it entirely: the same canal is lovelier on foot, and free.
🍽️ TRAP

Photo menus on the Little Venice quay

Some canal-view terraces sell frozen flammekueche at tourist prices; it's the one thing that shadows Alsatian cooking's good name.

💡 What to Do Instead: Walk two streets in: in the locals' winstubs the same dish arrives at half the price with twice the flavour.
🧸 TRAP

Souvenir 'Alsace dolls' and stork piles

Mass-produced storks and imported ornaments labelled 'handmade' fill every corner of the old town; space in the suitcase, a hole in the budget.

💡 What to Do Instead: For a real keepsake choose wine from a producer, local pottery (Soufflenheim), or a jar of honey from the market.
🚌 TRAP

Cramming the Wine Route into one day

The five-villages-in-a-day 'completion' tour means seeing them all through a bus window; Alsace villages are beautiful in the lingering, not the rushing.

💡 What to Do Instead: Pick two villages (Eguisheim + Riquewihr is the classic) and give each half a day; never hurry a tasting.
🛡️ The golden rule

In Colmar the crowd flows one way; walk two streets against it. Ninety percent of the town waits quietly just behind the ten percent in the shop window.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Colmar

French courtesy is the key here too; a pinch of Alsatian on top guarantees smiles.

🗣️ Bonjour
Hello — the mandatory first word of every exchange
🗣️ Merci
Thank you — stronger with beaucoup
🗣️ S'il vous plaît
Please — add it to the end of your order
🗣️ Santé!
Cheers! — Riesling glasses rise to this
🗣️ Hopla!
The all-purpose Alsatian exclamation: 'come on, okay, there we go' — hear it and you're in Alsace
