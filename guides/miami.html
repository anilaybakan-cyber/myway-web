Miami

The tropical stage of art deco pastels, cafecito steam and turquoise water

Overview

The Vibe of Miami

Miami is America's tropical exception: pastel art deco facades face a turquoise sea, Cuban coffee steam mingles with the clack of dominoes, and Wynwood's walls are repainted every year. Spanish is the unofficial first language here, salsa spills out of the supermarket, and the pastelito bakery open at 5 a.m. feeds the crowd leaving the nightclub. The beach is the shop window, but the city's heart beats in its Cuban, Haitian, Colombian and Venezuelan neighborhoods. Sunscreen and dancing shoes — Miami wants both in the same day. Dale!

💬 Dale
🗣️ /DAH-leh/

The multi-purpose command of Miami Spanish: 'let's go', 'okay', 'go ahead', 'keep going' — all at once. Pitbull's choruses carried it to the world, but it has lived on these streets for a century. The waiter taking your order, the friend pulling you to dance, the light turning green: dale. Crack this word and you've cracked Miami's rhythm.

Insider Advice

Unwritten Rules for Miami

#1
See South Beach, but find Miami in the neighborhoods Ocean Drive is for photos; the city's soul lives in Little Havana's domino park, on Wynwood's walls and under Coral Gables' banyan shade. Half a day for the beach, everything else for the neighborhoods — that's how Miami is loved.
#2
Join the cafecito hour Standing at a ventanita in mid-afternoon with a thimble of sweet espresso is Miami's shared ritual: watch the offices order a colada at 3:05 with the obligatory joke. The colada is for sharing — drinking one alone marks you as a tourist.
#3
Take the summer noon and the hurricane calendar seriously June to October is humid, showery and hurricane season: the afternoon rain comes and goes within the hour, and flexibility is enough insurance. Late October to May is the postcard Miami promises — in this city, date choice is half the experience.
Off the Beaten Path

Hidden Gems in Miami

💎 HIDDEN GEM
Vizcaya Museum and Gardens
An Italianate villa of 1916: marble terraces, labyrinth gardens and a stone barge descending to the bay. Miami's most graceful morning — go early for the light; stumbling into quinceañera photo shoots is part of the show.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
The Venetian Pool
A 1924 public pool carved from a coral rock quarry in Coral Gables: waterfalls, grottoes and palms. The world's most beautiful municipal pool — go early on a summer morning.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Bill Baggs and the Key Biscayne lighthouse
Island calm twenty minutes over the causeway: an 1825 lighthouse, a waveless beach and picnic groves. The most peaceful address for gazing back at the Miami skyline.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Domino Park (Calle Ocho)
Máximo Gómez Park in the heart of Little Havana: masters in guayabera shirts slam dominoes all day as cigar smoke drifts. Watching is welcome, respect is essential — Miami's truest corner.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
The MiMo Boulevard
Biscayne Boulevard's 1950s 'Miami Modern' motels with neon signs: restored lobbies, retro pools and photogenic facades. Art deco's lesser-known sibling — cruise the strip, stop at the signs.
💎 HIDDEN GEM
Everglades Shark Valley
An hour west, the Everglades' 24-kilometer loop road: you cycle past alligators, and from the observation tower the 'river of grass' runs to the horizon. Miami's wild backyard — early morning, water bottle in hand.
Food & Flavors

What to Taste

The Miami table is Latin America's meeting point: the crunch of the Cuban sandwich, the lime of the ceviche and the coffee cup passed through the ventanita.

🍴 MUST TASTE
The Cubano sandwich
Ham, roast pork, Swiss cheese, pickles and mustard, pressed hot in Cuban bread: the crunch is the mark of mastery. Try it at Little Havana's storied counters — the 'medianoche' version is the midnight classic.
🍴 MUST TASTE
The cafecito and pastelito ritual
Cafecito at the ventanita with a guava pastelito alongside: the official pair of the Miami morning (and afternoon). If you bought a colada, hand out the cups — sharing is the protocol itself.
🍴 MUST TASTE
The ropa vieja table
The shredded beef stew named 'old clothes': with black beans, rice and sweet fried plantains. The masterpiece of Cuban home cooking — Little Havana's portions are generous.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Stone crab season
From October to May, Florida's stone crab claws are served cold with mustard sauce: Miami's seasonal luxury. Claw size sets the price — if you're in season, don't miss it.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Ceviche and Latin fusion
Peruvian migration brought world-class ceviche to Miami: fresh fish, lime, ají pepper. The city's stylish rooms are the laboratory of Latin fusion — tiradito and arepa share a menu.
🍴 MUST TASTE
Cuban sweets and batidos
Tres leches, caramel flan and tropical batidos (shakes): where else will you taste guanabana or mamey? The sweet answer to a hot afternoon.
Watch Out

Tourist Traps to Avoid

❌
Menus without prices, giant cocktails extended as 'happy hour', and a $45 surprise on the bill: Ocean Drive's oldest number.
✅ Better Alternative: Ask menu prices and cocktail sizes before sitting; refuse the 'two-for-one' pitches. Take the view walking Ocean Drive, set your table on Española Way or two streets in.
❌
A wrong park in South Beach means a tow truck within minutes; some private lots run outrageous event-day pricing.
✅ Better Alternative: Use the municipal garages and parking apps; read the sign to its last line. On beach days go early or arrive by ride app — a tow fee swallows the day's budget.
❌
Some cut-price airboat tours end in a fifteen-minute noisy loop and shabby animal shows.
✅ Better Alternative: Choose the national park side: the Shark Valley bike loop and ranger walks are authentic and economical. If you want an airboat, research a park-authorized, small-group operator.
❌
Tourist-zone restaurants add 18–20% service automatically; the unaware visitor writes another tip on top.
✅ Better Alternative: Look for the 'gratuity/service included' line: if it's there, nothing more is needed — a few dollars if you're delighted. Make this check a Miami reflex, especially around Ocean Drive and Bayside.
