Hong Kong

The harbour where skyscrapers meet mountains, and dim sum meets neon

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team • 🛡️ Verified Local Guide
Overview & Atmosphere

The True Vibe of Hong Kong

Hong Kong is a harbour of contradictions, and that is exactly why it's addictive: ten minutes from the world's densest forest of skyscrapers, subtropical mountain trails begin; the double-decker tram trundles beneath neon signs while the metro runs to the second. In the morning, tai chi is practised in the shadow of a bamboo-scaffolded tower; at noon dim sum arrives in steaming baskets; at night the harbour stands to attention for a light show on both banks. This city never slows down — but it has the grace to let you catch your breath on the Star Ferry's deck.

🗣️ Local Concept: Ga yau (gah yow)

加油 — literally 'add oil': the Hong Kong way of saying 'keep going, hang in there, you've got this!'. At the marathon, in the exam hall, on the uphill stairs — the city's spirit in two syllables.

3 Unwritten Rules for Hong Kong

1

Don't take a step without an Octopus card

The Octopus works everywhere — metro, tram, ferry, supermarket — and reduces your turnstile life to seconds. Buy it at the airport, top it up, forget about it — the tourist hunting for cash in this city is forever in a queue.

2

Stand right on the escalator, walk fast

Hong Kong's tempo is high: the escalator's left is for walkers, the right for standers, and nobody will explain the rule — they simply live it. Match the flow in a crowd, and step aside to check your map.

3

Share the cha chaan teng table

At busy hours being seated with strangers at a local diner (cha chaan teng) is normal: don't be surprised — nod, look at the menu. Fast ordering, fast eating, passing the table on — this is a rhythm, not rudeness.

Getting Around

Hong Kong Public Transit & Airport Transfers

Hong Kong's transport is a sightseeing programme in itself: a century-old tram, a legendary ferry and the world's most efficient metro meet on one card.

✈️ Airport to the centre

The Airport Express reaches Central in 24 minutes, with free shuttles fanning out to the big hotels. The budget alternative, buses A11/A21, is scenic and half the price.

🚇 The MTR — a backbone that runs to the second

The metro network weaves both shores and the New Territories: clean, fast, flawlessly signed. Be ready for the human tide at rush hour — the flow carries you; just steer.

🚋 The Ding Ding — the double-decker tram

The double-deckers have trundled along the island since 1904 and remain the city's cheapest tour: take the front seat on the top deck and glide beneath the neon signs. Board at the back, pay as you leave.

⛴️ The Star Ferry — the harbour's legend

The green-and-white ferries have linked the two shores since 1888 and give the world's best view cruise for pocket change: the evening crossing from Kowloon, as the skyline lights up, is the stuff of legend.

🚠 The Peak Tram and the cable cars

The Peak Tram has climbed steeply to Victoria Peak since 1888; the Ngong Ping 360 cable car glides to the Big Buddha on Lantau. Both love a queue — first departures of the morning are the rule.

💡 Ferry tip

Board the Star Ferry at Tsim Sha Tsui just before sunset, cross to the island and come back: the two tickets together cost less than a coffee, and this is the only 'harbour cruise' that watches the 8 pm light show from the water.

Neighborhood Guide

Where to Stay in Hong Kong: Best Areas

In Hong Kong choosing a shore is choosing a character: the island's financial polish, Kowloon's neon energy, or the islands' calm?

Central and Sheung Wan Most central

Antique lanes between the finance towers, the Mid-Levels escalator and SoHo's restaurants: the island's heart. High prices, flawless location — the classic for a first visit.

SoHo restaurants • The escalator • Finance heart
Tsim Sha Tsui (Kowloon) Best views

The skyline view from the harbour's edge: museums, the Star Ferry pier and Nathan Road's energy. Remember the skyline photo is taken from this side — your hotel window can be a gallery.

Skyline views • Museum waterfront • Shopping heavy
Wan Chai and Causeway Bay Most lively

Markets, the tram line, nightlife and shopping floors interlocked: the island's dense everyday life. Hotels for every budget, something open at every hour.

Tram line • Nightlife • Every budget
Mong Kok and Yau Ma Tei Most local

Neon signs, street markets and one of the densest crowds on earth: Hong Kong unfiltered. From cheap guesthouses to boutique hotels — for those who like their reality in high doses.

Street markets • Neon nights • Fair prices
Lantau and the islands Calmest

Lantau of the Big Buddha, the fishing island Cheung Chau or laid-back Lamma: another tempo half an hour by ferry. For those who want the city + nature balance built into where they sleep.

Ferry life • Mountain trails • Village tempo
Seasons & Weather

Best Time to Visit Hong Kong

Read the subtropical calendar like this: October-December is the golden window, summer is humid and typhoon-prone, spring misty but alive.

Spring Mar – May
Misty Vitality

The air warms, the humidity slowly climbs, and on some mornings the harbour wraps itself in mist — the Peak view becomes a lottery. The art season floods the city with galleries in spring.

Summer Jun – Sep
Humidity and Typhoon Season

The heat and humidity are heavy and the occasional typhoon signal stops the city; but hotels get cheaper, and the malls and museums are icy cool. Island beaches and junk-boat days are this season's reward.

Autumn Oct – Dec
The Golden Window

Humidity drops, the sky clears and the city returns to its best self: the Peak view sharp, the trails cool, the terraces full. This is the year's most reliable travel window — everyone knows it, so book early.

Winter Jan – Feb
Cool and Festive

Winter is gentle — cardigan weather, not coat weather. In late January-February the Chinese New Year paints the city red: flower markets, the night parade and fireworks over the harbour. Some businesses close for a few festival days.

Culinary Culture

Authentic Local Dining & Food Secrets in Hong Kong

Hong Kong holds some of the densest delicious square metres on earth: Michelin-starred dim sum and back-alley wonton live in the same block.

🥟 Dim sum — a ritual by the basket

Har gow (shrimp dumplings), siu mai, char siu bao: the table builds itself as the steaming baskets land. A morning-and-noon ritual — dim sum without tea is dim sum incomplete.

⭐ Morning to noon · with tea
🍗 Char siu and roast goose

The glossy red char siu and crisp-skinned roast goose hanging in the windows: the shopfront pride of Cantonese cooking. A portion over rice is the city's best fast meal.

⭐ At a roast-shop · over rice
🍜 Wonton noodles and fish balls

Shrimp-filled wontons over fine egg noodles: the century-old speciality of tiny shops. The street stall's fish-ball soup plays in the same league.

⭐ In a tiny shop · being squeezed in is good luck
🍞 Cha chaan teng classics

Silk-stocking milk tea, a pineapple bun (bolo bao) with a slab of cold butter, and Hong Kong-style French toast: the warmest menu of the east-west mix. Afternoon tea is drunk here.

⭐ Afternoon ritual · with milk tea
🥚 Egg tarts and egg waffles

A warm egg tart (dan tat) from the oven and the bubble-textured egg waffle (gai daan jai): the city's two sweet signatures. Whichever bakery has the queue is the right bakery.

⭐ Street sweet · warm from the oven
🍲 A dai pai dong evening

Wok flames at open-air stalls, plastic stools and ice-cold beer: the smoky flavour called 'wok hei' (the breath of the wok) is born here. Their numbers have dwindled — when you find one, don't pass it by.

⭐ At an open stall · by wok fire
🍷 The table rule

Fill your neighbour's cup before your own, and accept thanks by two fingers tapped lightly on the table — this silent courtesy is the table's heart. Don't fear the menu-less place: point + show how many you are; that method never leaves you hungry in this city.

Off The Beaten Path

Handpicked Hidden Gems in Hong Kong

Everyone goes up the Peak; these six open Hong Kong's layers.

The Lugard Road walk The Peak's free rival

Five minutes from the Peak Tram terrace's crowds, a flat path circles the summit: the skyline at your feet, no queue, no entry fee. The sunset loop takes 45 minutes — the city's best free view.

Man Mo Temple Silence under incense spirals

A dim temple in the middle of Sheung Wan with giant incense coils hanging from the ceiling, dedicated to the gods of literature and war. At the foot of the skyscrapers, you step into another century in an instant.

The Temple Street night market Fortunes under the neon

As evening falls the stalls, fortune tellers and open-air karaoke booths appear: the set of Hong Kong cinema in real life. Haggling allowed, atmosphere free.

Yick Fat / Montane Mansion A thousand lives in one building

Quarry Bay's colossal housing blocks are famous as the 'monster building': look up from the courtyard and the city's density explains itself in one frame. With respect and quiet for the residents — this is a home, not a film set.

Cheung Chau island Another century on an island

A car-free fishing island 40 minutes by ferry: the smell of dried fish, bicycles, mini beaches and a pirate's cave. The locals' Sunday escape — go on a weekday and find it empty.

Sunrise on the Kowloon promenade Tai chi at dawn on the waterfront

The Tsim Sha Tsui promenade at 6 am: the skyline turns pink, locals practise tai chi, runners pass. Choose this quiet hour over the light show crowds — the harbour saves its most beautiful face for morning.

✨ Trail tip

Most of Hong Kong's land is mountain and park: give half a day to Dragon's Back, Lion Rock or a Lantau trail. From a summit you understand — this is not a skyscraper city, it's a nature reserve with skyscrapers.

Scam Prevention

Tourist Traps to Avoid & Smarter Alternatives

Hong Kong is honest and orderly; its traps are few but well camouflaged along the tourist route.

🚡 TRAP

The midday Peak Tram queue

At midday the tram queue reaches an hour; on a misty day you meet a white wall at the top — ticket paid, view a lottery.

💡 What to Do Instead: Take the first tram of the morning or climb by bus 15 on the winding road; at the top, walk Lugard Road's free view instead of the paid Sky Terrace. If it's misty, swap the day.
📷 TRAP

The Nathan Road electronics shops

Some electronics and camera shops on the tourist strip inflate the price afterwards, swap the box or play the 'mandatory accessory' game.

💡 What to Do Instead: Buy electronics from chain stores or official dealers, and verify prices online. Never enter a shop without price tags in the window.
🍽️ TRAP

The surprise bill at a view restaurant

Some harbour-view tourist restaurants pad the bill with service, tea and 'peanut' line items; the menu price is half the story.

💡 What to Do Instead: Treat the tea-and-snack charge as normal (it's local custom) but know the rates: 10% service + a small tea charge is standard. Take the view with one drink at a bar, eat at a local place.
🚕 TRAP

Insisting on a taxi for a short hop

In traffic hours a taxi is both dearer and slower; the MTR often does the same route in half the time.

💡 What to Do Instead: Use the MTR + tram + ferry trio with your Octopus; if you need a taxi, make sure the red (urban) cab runs its meter — there is no haggling taxi in this city.
🛡️ Golden rule

Queue culture in Hong Kong is sacred and well signposted: smile politely at anyone offering a queue-free 'shortcut' and walk on. The city's system works with the same efficiency for visitors — trust the system.

Local Slang

Useful Everyday Phrases in Hong Kong

English works everywhere, but five words of Cantonese open a wide smile on the counter's far side.

🗣️ Nei hou
Hello — the morning version 'jou san' is even more charming
🗣️ M̀h'gōi
Thanks / excuse me / may I — the magic word you'll use fifty times a day
🗣️ Dō jeh
Thank you (for a gift or big favour) — m̀h'gōi's formal sibling
🗣️ Ga yau!
'Keep going, you've got this!' — on the hill, in the queue, in life
🗣️ Ho sik
'Delicious' — said after the dim sum basket, it wins you the counter
