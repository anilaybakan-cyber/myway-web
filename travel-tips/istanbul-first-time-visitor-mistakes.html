15 Mistakes First-Time Visitors Make in Istanbul (and What to Do Instead)

Practical advice for a first trip to Istanbul: the Istanbulkart, mosque visiting etiquette, ferries vs. tour boats, closing days, the Asian side, taxis and where to eat beyond Sultanahmet.

✍️ MyWay Editorial Team 📅 Published 15 October 2026 🔄 Updated 15 October 2026 ⏱️ 10 min read

Istanbul is one of the world's great cities: two continents, layers of Byzantine and Ottoman history, and one of the best food cultures anywhere. It is also big, busy and easy to experience badly if you only see the most crowded parts. As a team based in Turkey, these are the mistakes we see first-time visitors make most often.

1. Staying only in Sultanahmet

The historic peninsula has the headline sights, but its streets empty out at night and many restaurants cater mostly to tourists. Consider staying in or spending evenings in Karaköy, Galata, Cihangir or Kadıköy, where locals live, eat and go out.

2. Not getting an Istanbulkart

The rechargeable Istanbulkart works on the metro, trams, buses, funiculars and public ferries. It is cheaper than single tickets and saves queuing. You can buy and top it up at machines at major stations and piers. One card can be used for several people, tapping once per person.

3. Paying for a Bosphorus tour when the public ferry is right there

Commuter ferries between Eminönü, Karaköy, Beşiktaş, Kadıköy and Üsküdar cost very little with an Istanbulkart and offer superb views. The 20-minute crossing from Eminönü or Karaköy to Kadıköy at sunset is one of the best things to do in the city. For a longer trip up the Bosphorus, the public Şehir Hatları line also runs Bosphorus tours.

4. Skipping the Asian side

Kadıköy and Moda on the Asian side are where you will find the famous food market streets, independent cafés, and a seafront promenade full of locals at sunset. Many visitors never cross the water; do it at least once.

5. Not checking closing days and prayer times

Topkapı Palace is generally closed on Tuesdays. The Grand Bazaar is closed on Sundays and on religious holidays. Mosques remain active places of worship and close to visitors briefly during the five daily prayers, with longer closures around Friday midday prayers. Check times on the day and plan around them.

6. Ignoring mosque etiquette

Visitors are welcome, but dress modestly: shoulders and knees covered, and women should cover their hair (scarves are often provided at the entrance of major mosques). Remove your shoes, speak quietly, do not walk in front of people praying, and avoid flash photography. Note that Hagia Sophia operates as a mosque and foreign visitors now enter through a separate, ticketed visitor route.

7. Hailing taxis on the street in tourist areas

Overcharging and "broken meters" are a known problem around major sights. Use taxi apps such as BiTaksi or Uber (which in Istanbul books licensed yellow taxis), or ask your hotel to call one. Always make sure the meter is running.

8. Eating every meal near the big sights

Some of the best food in the city is in ordinary neighbourhood places called esnaf lokantası: tradesmen's restaurants serving home-style stews and vegetables from a counter at lunchtime. Kadıköy's market streets, Karaköy and Beşiktaş are full of excellent, inexpensive options.

9. Leaving the Grand Bazaar for the end of the trip and haggling badly

Visit early in your trip to get a sense of prices, but buy at the end once you have compared. Haggling is expected in the bazaars, but stay friendly; start well below the first price, and be prepared to walk away. For spices, the Spice Bazaar is lively, but the shops in the streets around it are often where locals buy.

10. Underestimating distances and hills

Istanbul is large and hilly. Galata, Cihangir and Beyoğlu involve steep climbs. Use the historic Tünel funicular between Karaköy and Beyoğlu, and the Kabataş–Taksim funicular, and combine walks with trams and ferries.

11. Only drinking Turkish coffee

Turkish coffee is a must, but tea (çay) is the true everyday drink, served in small tulip-shaped glasses all day long. Accept a glass when it is offered in a shop; it is hospitality, not an obligation to buy.

12. Missing breakfast culture

A Turkish breakfast (kahvaltı) is an event: cheeses, olives, tomatoes, cucumbers, eggs, honey and clotted cream (bal kaymak), simit and endless tea. Weekend breakfast in Beşiktaş, Kadıköy or along the Bosphorus is a local ritual.

13. Falling for the friendly bar invitation

A stranger who befriends you on İstiklal Avenue and suggests a bar may lead you to a venue with enormous bills. Choose your own bars. More on this in our guide to common tourist scams.

14. Trying to see everything in two days

The historic peninsula alone easily fills two full days. Three to four days lets you add the Bosphorus, the Asian side and neighbourhoods like Balat and Fener with their colourful houses.

15. Visiting the Basilica Cistern at peak time

The Basilica Cistern, Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque all have long queues by late morning. Go at opening time or in the late afternoon, and use the middle of the day for a ferry ride or lunch.

A simple first-day plan

  1. Morning: Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque at opening time.
  2. Late morning: Basilica Cistern, then the Hippodrome.
  3. Lunch: an esnaf lokantası away from the main square.
  4. Afternoon: Grand Bazaar (not on Sunday), then walk downhill to the Spice Bazaar and Eminönü.
  5. Sunset: public ferry from Eminönü to Kadıköy.
  6. Evening: dinner in Kadıköy or Moda.
